# Held books - morning rulings (prepared 2026-09-24 00:10, nothing placed)

1. Once Was Willem - M. R. Carey, 2025 (Orbit). WFA 2026 + Locus Rec Fantasy.
   What: Cosham, 1152, during the Anarchy. Grieving parents pay sorcerer Cain Carodoc to raise their dead 12-year-old; Willem comes back from the grave as a "monster", then joins other woodland "monsters" to stop Carodoc's child sacrifices at the castle (Locus/Mondor, PW).
   Why held: Supernatural was frozen; no 12th-century period leaf.
   Options: Undying (2) - necromantic return from the grave, exact definition; fault: none real. Revenants (9) - dead returning bound by love/grief; fault: Willem is a raised body, not a ghost, and leaf is one from frozen. Autumn Kingdoms (1) - fault: 14th century.
   Pick: Undying. Clean existing-leaf fit now that Supernatural is unfrozen.

2. Slow Gods - Claire North, 2025. Locus Award 2026 SF shortlist + Locus Rec SF.
   What: laborer Maw, in the corrupt Shine polity, becomes an FTL pilot; a jump kills everyone else and remakes him as "a copy of himself, a monster made in the dark". The Slow, a machine-god, warns a binary star will collapse in a century and wipe out worlds within 83 light-years; Maw lives on through centuries of politics and evacuation (Kirkus, Locus/Pierce, New Scientist).
   Why held: reviews don't settle Future history vs Hard conceptual.
   Options: Future history in space (4) - fault: one immortal's life, not a civilization's arc. Theological space opera (2) - fault: the Slow is god-like but religion isn't the engine. Remade Minds (7) - fault: the remaking is the hook, not the plot. Hard Conceptual SF (9, last slot) - fault: philosophical rather than rigorous world-model.
   Pick: Future history in space.

3. The City in Glass - Nghi Vo, 2024. Locus Award 2025 + Locus Rec Fantasy.
   What: the demon Vitrine loves and shapes her invented city Azril; angels raze it, she curses one angel and binds him to the ruins; over centuries the two enemies grieve and the city regrows (Locus x3, BookPage, Grimdark).
   Why held: invented-world demon fantasy; no Fantasy Realms leaf fits.
   Options: Invented Mythology (4) - fault: angels and demons are borrowed, not invented gods. Dark Fantasy (realms) (3) - fault: grief and love, not dread. Isolationist fantasy (2) - fault: the city isn't sealed.
   Pick: none clean. Best existing = Invented Mythology; honest answer is a new Fantasy Realms leaf (immortals/city-as-character) - Gustav's call.

4. The Siege of Burning Grass - Premee Mohamed, 2024. Locus Award 2025 + Locus Rec Fantasy.
   What: pacifist Alefret, jailed and tortured by his warring nation Varkal, is sent with fanatical soldier Qhudur to infiltrate the enemy's floating capital. Healing wasps, tank-pillbugs, pteranodons, a camp in a giant skeleton (Reactor, Locus/Brookins, Ancillary).
   Why held: SF-war label vs secondary-world setting.
   Options: Military fantasy (3) - war from the ranks in an invented world; fault: no confirmed magic, biotech reads SF (Reactor calls it SF, Locus files it Fantasy). Future War (2) - fault: must be Earth. Planetary romance (7) - fault: grim war, not setting-driven adventure.
   RULED 2026-09-24 13:28 (Gustav): -> Hard science fantasy. His reasoning, verbatim: "*wanted hard science fantasy*"; "The trick of placing the story far ahead and hiding the fact this is even our universe at the beginning was exactly the rea[son]" - the hidden lost-Earth-colony reveal is the masquerade pattern the leaf was built for; Earthborn's cards are all open-origin books. Research behind it: no magic trace in any review; creatures are bioengineered (Locus/Brookins: "bioengineered creatures"); late-book reveal admits Earth-descended colonists and colony-ship tech (Redheaded Femme); The Blurb calls it a trope-worn "rational explanation".

5. Metronome - Tom Watson, 2022. Clarke 2023 shortlist.
   What: Aina and Whitney had a child without state permission and serve 12 years' exile on a northern island croft, tethered by a pill clock against toxic spores from melting permafrost; on parole day the Warden doesn't come (Guardian x2).
   Why held: only Orwellianesque fit.
   Options: Cli-fi dystopia (7) - climate collapse and scarcity behind the birth licensing; fault: Guardian says climate stays background. Crowded Dystopias (4) - "population in crisis", licensed births; fault: overpopulation is only hinted. Orwellianesque (3) - fault: last-resort leaf.
   RULED 2026-09-24 14:15 (Gustav): -> Cli-fi dystopia ("Ok, execute", after asking whether the leaf was crowded). Placed; next held item: The Anomaly.

6. The Anomaly - Hervé Le Tellier, 2022 (trans. Adriana Hunter). Clarke 2023 shortlist; Prix Goncourt 2020.
   What: Air France 006 flies through a storm in 2021 and lands twice, months apart, with identical passengers; governments debate whether we live in a simulation while each person meets their double (NYT, Telegraph, Kirkus, Irish Times).
   Why held: present-day literary SF, no clean leaf.
   Options: Unmoored Selves (4) - identity divides from itself; fault: cause is external (simulation glitch), not consciousness. Parallel World fiction (7) - fault: one world, duplicated people, no crossing. Alt Present (1) - fault: nothing in history changes before the event.
   Pick: Unmoored Selves.

7. The Secret Market of the Dead - Giovanni De Feo, 2025. WFA 2026.
   What: Lucerìa, a fictional Neapolitan town, 1747. Oriana fights her twin to inherit their father's smithy while crossing into the Night, a shadow world of seven Nocturnals and phantoms ruling destiny ("for those who loved Neverwhere") (PW, LJ, BookPage, FantasyLiterature).
   Why held: Rococo fantasy is 18th-century but courtly; this is a village smithy.
   Options: Otherworld fantasy (7) - one hidden mythic world behind ours; fault: sits under Portal fantasy, and the period setting is half the book. Rococo fantasy (1) - fault: salons and courts, not artisans. Communal magic realism (6) - fault: the Night is a real realm, not shared memory.
   Pick: Otherworld fantasy. Alternative is a new 18th-century folk leaf under Antique times fantasy - Gustav's call.

8. The Ten Percent Thief - Lavanya Lakshminarayan, 2023 (India 2020 as Analog/Virtual). Clarke 2024 shortlist.
   What: Bengaluru is now Apex City, run by Bell Corp; everyone is ranked on a productivity Bell Curve, top 10% live Virtual, failures are deported to the Analog outside. Mosaic of vignettes (PW, Fantasy Hive, Grimdark).
   Why held: between Huxleyan and Orwellian.
   Options: Postcyberpunk (7) - near-future surveillance and corporate power over ordinary people; fault: less about networks than caste. Orwellianesque (3) - ranking enforced by deportation is coercion; fault: last-resort leaf. Huxleyan (5) - fault: no pleasure/sedation.
   Pick: Postcyberpunk.

9. The Wings Upon Her Back - Samantha Mills, 2024. WFA 2025.
   What: in Radezhda, a tower city built by five sleeping gods whose gifts were technology, Zenya joins the warrior sect and earns surgically fitted biomechanical wings; decades later, stripped of them, she joins the resistance against her abusive mentor Vodaya (Kirkus, Locus/Wolfe, LJ).
   Why held: Hard science fantasy vs Planetary romance.
   Options: Hard science fantasy (4) - reads as fantasy, engine is technology, no FTL; fault: Wolfe says it keeps SF/F deliberately unresolved. Planetary romance (7) - fault: one city, not an exotic world. Civil revolution fantasy (4) - resistance vs its own regime; fault: Fantasy Realms, loses the science-fantasy frame.
   Pick: Hard science fantasy.
