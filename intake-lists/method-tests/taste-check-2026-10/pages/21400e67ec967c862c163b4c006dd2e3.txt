# REVIEW: The Red Winter by Cameron Sullivan

![Fiona Denton](https://www.grimdarkmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/1630122605-bpfull-100x100.jpg)

By Fiona Denton

February 24, 2026

![The Red Winter Header Image](https://www.grimdarkmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/The-Red-Winter-Header-Image-696x364.jpg "The Red Winter Header Image")

It is not often that a debut release knocks it out of the park quite as well as Cameron Sullivan’s The Red Winter; this book deserves all the hype it is getting and more. I have described The Red Winter as the book baby of Richard Swan’s The Justice of Kings and Jay Kristoff’s Nevernight, and I had an excellent time with this tale, loosely based on the French legend of the hunt for the Beast of Gévaudan. If you like your fantasy dark, with a historical twist, this is a blood-soaked story you are sure to enjoy.

![The Red Winter Cover Image](https://www.grimdarkmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/The-Red-Winter-Cover-Image-195x300.jpg)

“ The girl was surprisingly beautiful in the moonlight. “Surprisingly” for two reasons. First: she had a reputation for beauty. In my experience, this would usually mean only that she’d managed to keep all her teeth in the long weeks between puberty and pregnancy. And second: she was two days dead, and I have violated enough graves to know that corpses are not, to common tastes, beautiful.”

The foreword to Sullivan’s The Red Winter intrigued me, suggesting this would be a retrospective memoir, narrated by our main character, Sebastian Grave. But those first few sentences of the first chapter had me hooked. Now, The Red Winter was my pick for our most anticipated releases of 2026, so I had high expectations based solely on the jacket blurb and Christina Mrozik’s fantastic cover art. Still, it is a really lovely feeling to click with a book and know it will work for you. I flew through the story, transported from ancient Rome to 18th-century France. I had very little pre-existing knowledge of the historical influences on the story and still found Sullivan’s writing vivid and engaging. The Red Winter is excellent, especially considering it is a debut novel and could be forgiven for being a little rough around the edges.

It would be reductive to try to stick just one label on The Red Winter, but in its simplest form, this is a monster hunt. In 1785, Sebastian receives word that the Beast of Gévaudan has returned and is running amok in the French countryside. Twenty years before Sebastian was contracted to kill the beast, and now his knowledge his essential in vanquishing it a second time. Recalled to the hunt by his former lover, Antoine, Sebastian travels to Gévaudan to yet again use his arcane skills to kill the monster, and finds the village much changed. The people are miserable and desperate, so much so that a rampaging beast might not actually be the worst of their problems.

The story The Red Winter is equally driven by its characters, like Sebastian, his demonic guest Sarmodel, the succubus Livia, and Antoine, as it is by the different chronologies within the story. I kept reading to find out what was happening to the characters just as much as I was to solve the magical mystery and to see the hunt reach its conclusion. Sullivan has kept the narrative threads, split across a few timelines, tightly woven. Overall, this was a very well-paced story. There is a great balance between the dark, violent parts of The Red Winter and lighter, funnier moments. This story will tick a lot of boxes for grimdark fans, particularly because of the moral complexity of all the characters. There is a queer romantic subplot that is significant, but I don’t think it overpowers the narrative; dark fantasy, not romance, is very much the front-and-centre feature of this novel.

The acknowledgments for The Red Winter suggest that Sullivan will be writing more adventures featuring Sebastian and co., and I, for one, am very excited to see what he will produce next. Thank you to Cameron Sullivan and the team over at Tor for sending us an ARC of The Red Winter, which is out now.
