No magic, invented fantasy world or actual temporal travel appears in the three-volume account. Spacefaring and Alien fiction lose to the Earth-bound human technological arena; Hard SF loses to the violent survival-thriller experience rather than a rigorous scientific problem-solving contract. Modern day and its Genetic caste child are rejected despite publisher near-future wording: the completed account describes a profoundly changed Earth after centuries of exploitation, rising seas, transformed land and pervasive body-Meld commerce. The opening reviewer estimates one or two centuries; that estimate is not needed as an exact date. Future of Earth > Techno-fiction wins over ecological collapse because the flooded landscape is the setting of a working technological civilization, not the principal catastrophe experience. Technomorphosis > Posthuman > Altered flesh is the closest rival: extreme body changes are pervasive and often important to tactics. But neither the personal experience of acquiring a new body nor the body change itself organizes the whole trilogy. An alienated thief and his ally are relentlessly hunted over stolen high technology; corporate power, invasive modifications, a macguffin, hired killers, betrayal and survival are the continuing engine. Thus Cyberpunk noir wins over socially embedded Postcyberpunk, virtual-world Metaverse, game LitRPG or neural experienced realities. Within noir, this is fugitives surviving and pursuing answers while hunted, not a professional sleuth working an assigned case, a heist job, or institution-building rebellion. Wobble: the title and abundant body-Meld detail make Altered flesh plausible if the chase is discounted, and publisher calls the future near, but whole-fiction plot and actual social setting decide.
