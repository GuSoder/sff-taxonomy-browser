# How duplicates and series are handled

Living doc of series/duplication doctrine, commissioned by the curator on the project channel (phonemsg-01M3KVPC5EGFC5GS90SQ2M6TD8). Reference for keeping one series from multiplying across the tree. Refine as the project goes. Rulings cited by message id.

## One card per series (standing practice)

A series lives on ONE card - the first book's - with the later books folded into its books list. A sequel never gets its own card, and intake checks the tree for an existing series card before placing (sweep.log: "already in series card"). Card counts count CARDS, not books: a duology on one card is 1.

## Straddling series: the series, not the book

(the curator, phonemsg-01M3KVPC5EGFC5GS90SQ2M6TD8)

"If different books [of a] series straddles multiple genres, interrogate what THE SERIES is about, then choose that one."

The walk runs on the series as a whole, not on each book's local flavor. One home, chosen by the series' about-ness; the card's books list carries every volume there.

## Instance: the Red Hand duology (2026-09-28)

Rise of the Red Hand (2021) and Fall of the Iron Gods (2024) sat as two cards in Cli-fi dystopia, inflating the leaf to 10 and nearly triggering a phantom split. Folded under the rules above 2026-09-28: one card, both volumes in its books list, the leaf drops to 9 and the golden rule goes quiet. The curator delegated the call ("according to the doc, what's YOUR ruling?", phonemsg-01M3KVTCS5D0PM7KNK2NJ1RPSS); ruling per this doc: coalesce only, no split.

## Instance: the Wormwood Trilogy (2026-10-03)

The Wormwood Trilogy (Tade Thompson, Rosewater) had two cards: "The Wormwood Trilogy" (3 books) in Alien invasion fiction and "Wormwood trilogy" (Rosewater only) in Near-future first contact. Folded under the one-card-per-series rule, series about-ness: the hidden invasion of Rosewater (biodome and xenoform assimilation, alien plans to take human bodies) governs, so the card stays in Alien invasion fiction and the near-future first-contact card is removed. ## A shared universe is one fiction (curator, 2026-10-06)

(the curator, phonemsg-01M483CARNEF5RBAW6KCCVW1ST, 2026-10-06 9:55 AM, restating rule 11j: "A shared universe collapses onto one fiction card"): "we should save image quotas and not make genres just for their own sake, in this taxonomy we group shared universe books together, and they should be a genre that speaks to what defines the universe"

How the walk applies it:
- Before placing a book, check the tree for an existing fiction of the same universe, not only the same series. Series, sequels, prequels, spin-offs and companion series set in one world go onto that one fiction's books list (Osten Ard, Shannara, Ender, Lost Fleet, Dragaera, First Law, Middle-earth).
- The one fiction sits in the leaf that fits what defines the universe, chosen by a full root-down walk of the universe as a whole, not of each series.
- Counts are fictions, so a universe never fills a leaf on its own, and a new universe book never triggers a split or a new leaf by itself. Do not make a genre only to hold more books of a universe.
- Cameo links between otherwise separate worlds are not a shared universe (for example Stephen King's Dark Tower tie-ins). When unsure, ask before merging.
- Unpublished books stay on hold (Splintered Sun, Osten Ard).
Merges done 2026-10-06 under this ruling: Osten Ard (Memory, Sorrow, and Thorn), Shannara, The Ender Universe, The Lost Fleet, Mercy Thompson, The Others, Saint of Steel, Kithamar, The Sixth World, Age of Darkness, The Black Magician, Revelation Space, Saga of the Skolian Empire, Uglies, Alliance-Union, Foundation, Middle-earth, Dragaera, The First Law, Siege trilogy.
