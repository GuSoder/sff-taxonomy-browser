# How shelves are named

Living doc of naming doctrine, recorded from the curator's rulings in live naming workshops on the project channel. Reference for future shelf naming. Refine as the project goes. Rulings cited by message id.

## The method: the search order

(the curator, phonemsg-01M3KD3Q6JVBBTXK6P7J3KG7AT)

1. **First: is there an established term? If yes, use it.**
2. **If not: go with catchy.** To find catchy, ask: what is a common thing in all these books? The most catchy uniting thing can often come from unlikely places - a detail the authors always re-use - and that kind of name tends to be tongue-in-cheek; put the bar HIGH for tongue-in-cheek (his note: an AI struggles with humor).
3. **If no perfect evocative word is found: go for the general thing** - the environment where an archetypal scene of the genre takes place.

### Special cases: help we're handed

- **Period branches** (appear here and there in the taxonomy): find aesthetics that unite the era of the books being named - short, catchy, evocative words. His example: Rococo fantasy.
- **Culture/geographic branches** (also appear here and there): find evocative words for the time and place. His example: Andalusian fantasy.

## No A + B names

(phonemsg-01M439HTXRPZD80GDSTZ8MBVCB)

An A + B name joins two unrelated things, one found in each book, into a genre name like "This and That". Such a name specifies nothing. Once the genre fills up it splits at once into "Books about This" and "Books about That", and the logic of why the books sit together in the taxonomy is lost. Instead, find what is genuinely in common between the books and name the genre for that.

## Hints point to the closest established genre

(phonemsg-01M43930N9FYJ2QTBDN0A6P0WB)

A hint in a name should preferably be attached to the closest genre above it that has a somewhat established name. Example: "quest" is kept in names under Destiny quest fantasy.

## The tests a name must pass

The method above is the search order; these tests are the quality gates a candidate must pass.


- **Latch test.** Instant personal recognition - the name clicks on sight, no explanation needed.
- **Seed test.** The name must describe what the seed book actually contains. Podium died here: punchy, mic-forest image, "I can't shake the punchyness of Podium fantasy" (phonemsg-01M3JBJDSK9HWW9M3BG83FATEP) - but the seed has no podium scenes ("Is there podiums in our seed book?", phonemsg-01M3JB38FT6YW3HE5R9S0EBTRG; text-level probe of the publisher excerpt: none).
- **Sibling-pair test.** The best names make the split visible against the sibling. Court intrigue vs Election fantasy: bloodline vs the vote. (The costume version of the same pair: robes vs suits.)
- **State, not transition, not absence.** Name what the world arrived at. Defeudal names the leaving; Afeudal names a lack - and covers pre-feudal Rome.
- **Logic coverage.** The name must not accidentally cover cases from before its era.
- **Era rules.** No calendar years in names. An era shelf can't point to a time before the shelf's era: "It can't point to a time before jazz" (phonemsg-01M3J9NXGN85AAVQHG5ZXF06M9). Era-neutral beats decade-pinned (the Pinstripe fault). Eras get the name people already call them: Great War fantasy, with Blitz banked for WW2 (phonemsg-01M3J3PTY493SQ4N06GYDSNVRG).
- **Evocative beats contrived.** Idiom-games die - Collar, Hat, Seat, Pocket: "Not evocative, to contrived" (phonemsg-01M3JC1MYPAG08T2VGZKPB4N8W). Less about aesthetics (phonemsg-01M3J9R0QTS2MHV9BA603NXAZ9): Chrome, Octane, Skyline died here.
- **Punchy, but sayable.** No tongue-stuck consonant clusters: "Suit fantasy is almost to short, the tongue gets stuck between those consonants" (phonemsg-01M3JBZ9BV8XFHJDY4V156KBMQ).
- **Engine, not cover.** The name must name the axis of the split, not the surface furniture. The Underground fault: samizdat is underground too - the axis is WHAT is saved: texts vs bodies (phonemsg-01M3K8NHWWEKN50AJKYBFKWBQ6).

## Case study: Election fantasy (2026-09-27/28)

The commission: a leaf under Political fantasy for post-feudal mass-franchise politics, seeded by Fire Becomes Her. Roughly fifty candidates across eight families died in one night and a morning.

**Winner: Election fantasy** ("Ok, execute with Election fantasy", phonemsg-01M3K66AZFCBJSWMEGEAKKWYZ2). Why it won: seed-fit (the plot IS an election), a clean sibling-pair against Court intrigue, and punch. Its known fault - popes and doges were elected too ("election is not always democratic", phonemsg-01M3JAZHR58BXTM1J16V5HCQ0Z) - is pinned by the definition: mass franchise is the gate.

**Strong backups, with their strengths:**
- Defeudalized - completed state, parses on sight, fixes the Rome flaw; lost on punch.
- Ballot - mass suffrage IS the modern invention; "the top contender... a little dorky" (phonemsg-01M3JAX551JE7EF53MJG1TXFYA); Venice and conclaves balloted too, so the def must pin mass.
- Machine - the political machine, Tammany; hints political AND industrial; the robot misread is contained by the parent shelf.
- Headline - liked; "a mouthful" was the only fault (phonemsg-01M3JANCW190R5136XBKX7FEAV).
- Airwaves / Amplified - strong, superseded.

**Banked for a future shelf:** Trenchcoat fantasy - the informant's garment, not the candidate's; wrong axis for an election shelf, the perfect name for a political-noir strain someday.

## The graveyard, by family

- **Era-name attempts:** Post-medieval (too academic); Muskets and motors, Smokestack, Soot and spark (point before jazz); Modern (fled); Electrified, Industrialized (strain/collision registers); Post-feudal (names what the world left, not what it arrived at); Afeudal (typo look, covers pre-feudal); Unfeudal (same, plus ambiguity); Defeudal (names the transition, not the state).
- **Arena attempts:** Senate floor (Roman vocabulary; names the chamber, not the campaign trail); Politician (a 1580s word - Elizabethan courtiers were called politicians; redundant nested under Political).
- **Media attempts:** Televised (points after jazz - TV lands in the 50s, excludes the Jazz Age seed); Broadcast (compliant but flat); Airwaves, Amplified (strong, superseded); Newsprint (penny press, 1830s - pre-jazz); Headline (liked; mouthful); Wireless (the actual 1920s word for radio; wifi collision); Bulletin (bureaucratic).
- **System attempts:** Ballot (strong #2); Platform (1840s party platforms; tech misread); Landslide (the idiom AND the feeling; disaster misread); Airtime (TV-adjacent); Machine (top-3); Elect (religious echo); Spoils (war-loot misread); Boss (video games).
- **Stage family:** Podium (the one he couldn't shake; died on the seed test - and it names the stage, not the system); Soapbox (era-exact ancestor; quaint edge); Megaphone (comical); Balcony (the Evita/duce power image; the royal balcony points sideways); Stump (tree).
- **Idiom family:** Bandwagon (stickiest idiom; circus origin pre-jazz by a hair); Swing (double lock: swing voters + jazz dance; playground misread); Backroom (smoke-filled room, 1920; generic alone); Wire (results over the wire; barbed misread); Ticker tape (finance echo).
- **Costume family:** Suit (the robes-vs-suits pair with Court intrigue was the most readable of the night; died: tongue-stuck, too short); Pinstripe (liked, but too decade-pinned - "less specific age stuff please", phonemsg-01M3JBZ9BV8XFHJDY4V156KBMQ); Collar, Hat, Seat, Pocket (contrived).
- **Evocative family:** Anthem (no medieval king had a national anthem - unbuyable by earlier ages; hymn smell); Square (the public square; geometry); Voice (court whispers vs this shelf SPEAKS; generic); March (month + military).

## Technique notes

- **The participle gift.** A word that is its own past participle signals completed state for free ("broadcast" - cast/cast); the -ed participle is the pattern behind Defeudalized. The question that surfaced it: "I want it somehow to be 'BroadcastEd..' but language doesn't work that way, why do I want that?" (phonemsg-01M3JAB4VDTN3BXW8PAF1T20F1).
- **Phone-grid length matters.** The name sits in a mobile tile.
- **Names carry feeling, not just parse.** The workshop's hardest question: "What word captures the mighty feeling of a roaring crowd on election night?" (phonemsg-01M3JBBDVAX0ZQRC8NMYJHR05Y).

Graveyard, with reasons:

- Evasion: precise, but "still not catchy enough".
- Getaway: punchy, but smells of heists and sits beside Caper.

- Slip: clean idiom, not chosen here; later chosen for the Otherworld stumblings split (Slip into myth / Slip into realm, 2026-10-15).

- Lam, Hegira (nobody knows it on sight), Breakaway (reads as secession), Sail (solar sails), Runaway (teen romance), Fugitive and Space trackers (no pattern).
