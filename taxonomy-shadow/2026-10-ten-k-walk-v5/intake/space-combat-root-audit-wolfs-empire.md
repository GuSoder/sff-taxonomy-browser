# Wolf's Empire root correction, 25 November 2026

## Decision and unchanged scope
Move the existing one-fiction/one-novel card Wolf's Empire from Space combat to Antique alterations. No added books, authors, dates or covers. Space combat10to9; Antique alterations8to9. Neither reaches ten, so the just-triggered Space combat split is no longer needed. Do not preserve a root-placement error merely to make a neat split. No genre, definition or portrait change.

This is the same published 2016 novel Wolf's Empire: Gladiator by Claudia Christian and Morgan Grant Buchanan, not a tie-in to Babylon5 despite the actress's role. ISFDB identifies one original novel and later editions, not further published sequels. Goodreads' publisher-style synopsis, Locus's contemporary new-books notice, PW and multiple completed reviews explicitly establish a future in which historical Rome never fell, conquered Earth and then the galaxy. This is not a new Roman-flavored civilization that happens to resemble Rome. The precise point of divergence is not supplied; Skiffy and Fanty's reviewer speculates about the absence of Christianity, and that speculation must not become a claimed plot fact. Continued survival of the real Roman Empire establishes an implied ancient/late-antique fork regardless of the unverified exact year.

## Story
After her mother and brother are killed, noblewoman Accala Viridius seeks vengeance through the imperial games, relinquishing status to train as a gladiator. Civil war between the noble houses turns the arena into the mechanism for settling imperial conflict; enslavement and an indigenous rebellion force her to cooperate with former enemies. Revenge, games and combat govern the immediate plot. The universe's sustained historical divergence governs the earlier temporal branch. These are compatible descriptions at different depths, not a reason to start at Space combat.

## Full root-down walk
Speculative fiction > Science fiction > Historical SF > Alternate history > Deep alterations > Antique alterations.

Root: SF, not Fantasy. Atomic attacks, interstellar travel, advanced weapons, alien peoples and genetically altered/nonhuman opponents occur in a projected continuation of real Earth history. Classical invocation of the Muses and Roman religious imagery do not prove actual gods or magic. Sources read do not establish literal supernatural intervention, a portal from Earth into an independent realm, or authored reality. Ancient Rome remains the actual historical polity that survives, so this is not an invented fantasy Rome.

Temporal: Historical SF's Alternate history gate overrides the old Futurist route. Futurist is the strongest rival because the immediate setting is roughly7000AD, and this is genuinely a space adventure rather than a recreation of antique daily life alone. But the novel expressly asks what if real Rome never fell, and the publisher-style synopsis and completed reviews agree on that inherited divergence. The current Alternate history definition admits an identifiable OR implied turning point. No rule requires the story to be set close to its historical fork. Deep alterations explicitly locates books by the enduring divergence, not the action date. Calling any far-future alternate history Futurist would erase that plain gate. Modern-day fails its future action; Atemporal fails because no actual time travel or world-crossing has been established. Counterfactual history by itself is not a parallel-world journey. No claim of a historical timeline being actively rewritten in the plot.

Historical siblings: Alternate history, not Proto SF, because this is a2016novel, not premodern speculative science. Period SF would make it an antique-era technological marvel even though it is set millennia later. Hidden history SF requires events concealed within our familiar history, whereas here Rome openly survives and remakes the inhabited galaxy.

Alternate history children: Deep alterations rather than Period alterations. The latter's divergence is within living memory of the narrative present; surviving Rome's multi-millennial empire is an inherited order, not a recent alternate event. The novel is lived experience inside that altered civilization, not a reenactment of the fork itself. Any other children, including near-present alternatives, would misstate this durable world. An exact named divergence date is not necessary under the existing implied-fork definition.

Deep alterations children: Antique alterations. Rome's non-fall is before1500and the current leaf's explicit example is Rome that never fell. Age of sail alterations would invent a1500-1800fork; Machine age alterations would invent a post1800fork. The presence of advanced spacecraft or atomic weapons does not relocate the fork. Antique alterations now9, no split.

The rejected Futurist path remains a meaningful lower-level reading: Spacefaring > Space opera > New space opera > Space adventure > Space combat fits the action. Galactic houses is another surface rival because house wars supply the stakes. Neither can override the prior temporal gate. This correction does not widen any definition or change unrelated fictions that may have their own historical/SF tensions.
