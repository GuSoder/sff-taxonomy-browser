# Sword-Dancer normal intake, 2026-10-30

Root-down: Speculative fiction > Fantasy > Fantasy realms > Sword and sorcery > Classic S&S > Pulp S&S. Literal runesword, sorcery and magecraft in an invented North/South/Skandi world establish Fantasy realms, not scientific explanation, historical Earth or an Earth portal crossing. Whole eight published novels follow Tiger and Del through rescue, revenge, trial-by-combat, possession, origins, exile and family danger. Intimate action/adventure is the persistent engine, not a campaign of armies or dynastic statecraft. Epic/Heroic > Outcast knights is a genuine rival: the pair are exiled sword masters, but their continuing magic and Tiger's later magecraft prevent a purely martial-class reduction. The broad journey/worldbuilding Epic resemblance is weaker than the continuing sword-and-sorcery adventure lineage. Heart/Romantasy is also real, but the established couple's later heritage, magic and family adventures extend beyond forming or securing the romance. Far shores: North/South cultural echoes are mixed invented-world adventure rather than one sustained counterpart tradition. Gothic menace, comic tone and inherited fairy-tale revision do not govern the whole. Within S&S, formal sword-dances recur but the whole fiction is not one rules/prize/deadline tournament; pirate voyage is only book5, no scientific sword-and-planet, technomancy or caper-job engine. Classic lineage, not timeless foundational Evergreen: the 1986-onward revival continues the pulp adventure tradition. Current Pulp8->9, below structural gate. Later volumes broaden magical stakes and book8 has a world-threatening weather-working source, but remains the trio's personal journey and magical confrontation, not a shift into war/statecraft. Confidence moderate on competing Heroic/Romantasy routes; no claim of unanimous genre labeling or full novel reading.
