# Empire of the East / Swords: whole-world audit repair
23 November 2026

## Verdict
One shared world, fifteen novels, belongs in existing Fantasy after Disaster, four to five fictions. Ozymandian tech falls from nine to eight. The current Empire of the East card contains only Ardneh's Sword2006 although its note claims four Empire novels. Add the missing fourteen novels and retain the existing fiction identity. Do not count the omnibus editions or the multi-author short-story collection An Armory of Swords as novels. Totals after this repair:927nodes/2975fictions/7358books. No split threshold is reached. No structural mutation is involved.

## Positive shared-world evidence
The official author's FAQ explicitly identifies Ardneh's Sword as the connection between Empire of the East and Swords. The author's Swords Bible, originally supplied to authorized anthology contributors, explicitly calls Empire a prequel and explains ARDNEH, the Old World, the Change, demons and gods across the shared setting. The Science Fiction Encyclopedia independently groups Empire, Book of Swords and Book of Lost Swords as Earth's End and confirms common characters/universe. This is not a merge based on a shared author or similar names. Full current index search found no separate Swords fiction to merge, so the fiction count does not change.

## The actual books and plot cycles
The Broken Lands1968, The Black Mountains1971, Changeling Earth1973: Rolf joins the West's resistance to the Eastern empire. A recovered tank, the computer ARDNEH, magic, demons and remnant institutions are materially present. The third book explains the technologically induced Change and confronts ARDNEH with Orcus. Changeling Earth was later retitled Ardneh's World; one novel, not two. The author page reverses the alternate-title direction in presentation but itself records the1973DAW original, independently confirmed by SFE and ISFDB's first printing.

The First Book of Swords1983, The Second Book of Swords1983, The Third Book of Swords1984: gods forge twelve genuinely magical swords for a game involving mortals. Jord's sacrifice and Mark's inheritance begin the human story; swords can also kill the gods who made them, transforming the game into a struggle for their survival.

Woundhealer's Story1986, Sightblinder's Story1987, Stonecutter's Story1988, Farslayer's Story1989, Coinspinner's Story1989, Mindsword's Story1990, Wayfinder's Story1992, Shieldbreaker's Story1994: after the gods' downfall, swords move among rulers, families, healers, thieves and opponents. Each has a particular power and limitation. The official individual blurbs establish the distinct plots, including healing a prince's son, stolen Stonecutter, Farslayer's murderous feud, Coinspinner's fickleness, Mindsword's compulsory loyalty, Wayfinder's journey and the final Shieldbreaker confrontation. The original trilogy and eight subsequent novels are not omnibuses. Official pages record the copyright years for all eight Lost Swords; the official First Swords omnibus page explicitly records1983/1983/1984 for its constituent novels, not its later reprint year.

Ardneh's Sword2006: set between the older trilogy and Swords, Chance Rolfson seeks evidence of ARDNEH and his hidden legacy amid active demons and supernatural danger. The existing2006row is retained, not replaced by the omnibus of the earlier trilogy. Publication order need not equal story chronology.

## Root-down walk
Speculative fiction > Fantasy: the old note reduced wizards/demons to mutation and machinery. Direct primary evidence contradicts that. The author's Swords Bible explicitly describes currently magical demons with lives hidden in objects, magic-forged blades, griffins whose flight is impossible under biology/aerodynamics but sustained by magic, and gods created by the collective human unconscious. It even identifies the Emperor as an aspect of the Creator. These are not merely robots mistaken for gods. A technological origin for a changed reality does not negate actual magic within it. The SF label in older references and the rationalized backdrop remain rival evidence, but the user's actual-magic gate controls this taxonomy.

Fantasy > Real earth fantasy: official Bible identifies twentieth-century Earth as the Old World and uses the Moon/Earth as locations. It explicitly describes future descendants of familiar animals and transformed Old World survivors. An unrecognizable future landscape does not turn it into an independent invented world. Fantasy realms/Magitech, Hard fantasy or sword-based hero classes therefore fail this ancestor. Portal fantasy fails a governing Earth-to-other-world crossing; a wizard's isolated time escape does not make the whole world a portal narrative.

Real earth > Future fantasy: these societies exist after the nuclear-era Old World, millennia in Earth's future. Not historical or contemporary. Space fantasy fails a sustained spacefaring-civilization narrative; a demon's possible banishment near the Moon is not that frame. End-times fantasy fails a biblical-apocalypse/angelic-war frame. Vampire future and Psionic fantasy fail their governing creatures/social-order premises.

Future > Post-apocalyptic fantasy: remnant/rebuilt civilization after the Change is material to every cycle. The original human technological order is gone; gods, temples, rulers, old weapons and the swords organize its successor world. Not a never-fallen future Earth.

Post-apocalyptic > Fantasy after Disaster: the original collapse is not assumed to have been caused by a god, spell or supernatural breach just because magic exists afterward. The initiating crisis was nuclear war and a technological defense system whose interacting safeguards changed physical reality and disabled the old technological world. Finished-third-book Black Gate review explicitly describes the untested technological precautions and mostly averted nuclear war. The author Bible locates the birth of demons at the passing wave of Change; demons and learnable magic arise from that transformation. Their later wars do not retroactively make the initiating cause supernatural. Post Magicalypse is a close rival because the Change's effects are fantastic and magic subsequently devastates societies, but its cause gate is not met by naming the effect instead of the trigger. This distinction follows the original6November approved cause axis, not a new definition. Moderate confidence in this finer cause classification, high confidence in Fantasy/Future-Earth/whole-world scope.
