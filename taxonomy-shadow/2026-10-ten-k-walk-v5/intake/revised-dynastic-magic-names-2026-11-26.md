# Dynastic magic naming revision, 26 November 2026, 16:51 CET

Proposal only. Owner rejected Spiritbound thrones as corny in authenticated message phonemsg-01M8C8RJDZPVWAAPTEDFV7G5EG. Neither child approved. No intake or structural execution.

Strongest replacement: Dynastic enchantments.

This uses a normal English term for magic exerting power over someone, rather than an invented adjective followed by fantasy-title furniture. It names the mechanism, not a throne prop or specifically a ghost. Cambridge's magic senses of enchantment cover a magical power over someone and the state of being under magical influence: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/enchantment . This is lexical support, not evidence that the full label is an established genre.

Same five fictions, fourteen published novels:
- Branion4: the Living Flame inhabits and constrains the royal vessel. Not just a ruler choosing a fire spell.
- Queens of Elben2: royal marriages bind queens to palaces and let Henry take their power; the magical bondage and its consequences organize the contest.
- Boreal Moon3: supernatural bargains, costly sigils and disqualifying pacts determine royal succession. Hidden innate Talent remains a real crossover.
- Obsidian Throne2: curse, dead emperor's consciousness and compelled witch power entangle the succession. No claim that the actual emperor inhabits a throne, and no future third novel counted.
- Tielmaran3: Kinghood rite and supernatural royal-land covenant make legitimate rule conditional.

Proposed definition: Dynastic contests in which an enchantment binding the ruling line is a decisive instrument of sovereignty: an indwelling power, pact, curse or royal-land bond that empowers, compels or disqualifies its rulers.

Sibling Sorcerer dynasties stays unchanged as a proposal, not an approved name: DrowningEmpire3, BloodFour1, CrownShards6, Cheysuli8, DragonPrince/Star6, total5/24. It names magical capacity acquired, inherited, stolen or made and wielded by rival ruling families. Revised pair distinguishes wielding sorcery from a dynasty's binding enchantments. Not a claim the first five never suffer spells or the latter five never cast them. Existing whole-fiction crossover priority rules remain documented in the original proposal.

Cold-name caveat: enchantments can also mean delightful qualities or individual cast spells. Dynastic keeps the royal-line context and the definition pins the binding sense. This is plainer but less vivid than the rejected name, a deliberate response to the owner's criticism, not an attempt to resell the same spiritbound wording.

Alternatives considered: Enchanted dynasties, a clear active/passive pair with Sorcerer dynasties, but still has storybook-title flavor and a near-singular existing artwork/product name. Royal enchantments is an actual cosplay business, verified search lead https://royalenchantments.com/ , not selected. Dynastic curses is clean but false for nonmalign divine Kinghood and voluntary pacts. Sacred kingship excludes Obsidian's curse and the predatory Beaconfolk. Dynastic pacts implies voluntary agreement, false for imposed curses. Possessed dynasties narrows to indwelling entities, false for Elben's palace bonds and Tielmaran's rite. Dynastic bindings by itself need not be magical. Bewitched dynasties sounds comic and implies externally controlled rulers across all five, too narrow.
