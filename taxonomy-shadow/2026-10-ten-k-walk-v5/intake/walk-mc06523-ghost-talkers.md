## Whole-story findings
1916, Battle of the Somme, Western Front. Ginger Stuyvesant is an American heiress and medium stationed with the British Spirit Corps at Le Havre. Dying British soldiers are conditioned to report what they observed before their souls cross over. Circles of mediums and living grounds process their deaths and send intelligence back through military channels. Fatigue, burnout and bodily vulnerability matter. Helen Jackson, a Black medium, and Indian soldiers confront the army's racism; Ginger and other women confront its sexism. Indian soldiers initially lack reporting tokens, and their own spiritual practices broaden what the British system recognizes.

A dead British officer reports being murdered by someone apparently on his own side. Ginger suspects a traitor but the command discounts her. German forces have learned enough to target the Spirit Corps and its protected location. The investigation leads from Le Havre toward the front, trenches and prisoners. Ginger's fiancé Ben is murdered in chapter six, confirmed by the author, and returns as a ghost with unfinished business. His presence is a major development, not the final reveal. They pursue the conspiracy while Ginger's grief and desire to follow him into death create a second danger.

The author discusses nightmare torture, Ben's poltergeist development, Mrs Richardson's death, the murder plot's resolution and Ben's eventual need to move on. The emotional ending lets Ben go rather than preserving an indefinitely happy living/dead couple. Ginger must continue living. The author describes earlier-draft revisions explicitly; those discarded details are not treated as final-canon events. The creator's interview confirms Ginger's willingness to risk death after Ben's murder. Secondary finished readers independently describe the meaningful conclusion amid continuing war.

The concealed operation is positive Secret history evidence: the Spirit Corps' magical work is disguised as hospitality for recovering soldiers, its actual Le Havre location hidden even behind a London reporting belief, and Doyle/Houdini debunking is a disinformation campaign protecting the program. Frumious gives the logistics and Seattle Times independently confirms Doyle's part in disinformation. This is not simply a generic war setting with one ghost attached. Secret supernatural intelligence influences actual troop strategy behind the recorded conflict, and uncovering/protecting its covert machinery drives the novel.

## Actual-tree hard walk
1. Speculative fiction > Fantasy, not Science fiction. The literal souls of deceased soldiers, afterlife, crossing over, living anchors, spirit auras and poltergeist effects are confirmed through the author's spoiler discussion and finished reviews. Rigorous operational rules do not turn spiritualism into a technological explanation or SF psi. No scientific demystification found. The author describes spiritualism working as advertised.
2. SF Fabulation fails. A consciously constructed system, historical cameos and ghostly perception are not editable narrative reality, textual trespass or a metafictional mechanism. No SF time travel identified.
3. Fantasy > Real earth fantasy. Actual Britain, France, Somme and Le Havre in 1916. Not an independent fantasy realm. Portal fantasy is not the organizing journey: departed souls report to mediums; the main narrative is the human military investigation on Earth. This tree explicitly permits the afterlife inside Real earth fantasy.
4. Real earth > Historical fantasy. The included novel is set in 1916, long before publication. Excluded 1920s short stories are not imported to create temporal spread. Neither Contemporary nor Future fantasy. No centuries-long chronicle or physical time-travel frame found.
5. Historical > Secret history. This is the load-bearing fork. Period fantasy requires the era to be the stage rather than a puzzle secretly explained. Here the hidden military-spiritualist institution supplies clandestine intelligence behind the Somme, is protected by active disinformation from historical figures, and must uncover a traitor exposing it. Secrecy is structural to plot and world. Marketing labels such as alternate history are too broad to settle this tree's narrower fork.
6. Secret history > Occult cabals. Its unchanged definition expressly includes occult orders and spies working behind recorded events to steer or contest them. The Spirit Corps is a covert military occult service, gathering information to direct attacks and defenses while countering German penetration. Arcane peoples fails: no concealed ancient people is the hidden truth. Untold magic is plausible because magic works inside WWI and spiritualism, but the more specific organizing hand is the secret intelligence institution and its espionage conflict, not a broad historical movement discovering magic.
7. Strongest rival walked: Period > Industrial age > Electrified > WWI fantasy. Its 1914-18 Great War stage, trench/home-front machinery and haunting are excellent surface fit. Unlike an isolated haunting amid war, however, the supernatural intelligence program and its secret protection actively explain tactical action behind the known war. That positive evidence makes Secret history the more precise route. Existing Dark Arts has a secret Allied magical warfare program, supporting the unchanged Occult cabals meaning; it is a useful comparator, not substitute evidence.
8. Alternate history fantasy fails its actual gate: an openly rewritten magical timeline with publicly magic-run world is not established. Operations are deliberately concealed. Tactical interference and broad reviewer alternate-history tags do not alone establish the required open divergence. Mythic retelling fails: no inherited myth-cycle narrative is the source. Doyle/Tolkien/Doctor cameos are not an Arthurian, Biblical, Norse or other mythic retelling.
9. No leaf under Fantasy realms or Contemporary is reachable by ignoring its ancestors. Romance, supernatural mystery and grief are real, but no present-day haunting/romance shelf overrides the historical concealed-service mechanism.

Final path: Speculative fiction > Fantasy > Real earth fantasy > Historical fantasy > Secret history > Occult cabals.
