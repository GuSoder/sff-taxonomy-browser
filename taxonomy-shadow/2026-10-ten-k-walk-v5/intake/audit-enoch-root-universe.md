## Whole-fiction story audit
- Quicksilver: seventeenth-century England/continental Europe, Daniel Waterhouse among Newton, Hooke, Wilkins and Leibniz; experimental method, political and religious turmoil, alchemical research. Jack Shaftoe rescues Eliza from Turkish bondage; trade, courts, intelligence and the Glorious Revolution draw them apart. The1713frame summons Daniel back from Boston to mediate Newton/Leibniz, with piracy on the crossing. Eliza develops financial and political power rather than becoming a generic magician protagonist.
- The Confusion: interwoven Bonanza/Juncto strands. Jack's galley-slave Cabal steals unexpected gold, loses it, builds Minerva, trades around the world, returns to Europe and is drawn into a French attack on English money. Eliza manages finance, court intelligence, blackmail, family and rival banking houses. The scientific circle's Juncto helps create the Bank of England and Newton's Mint appointment. Enoch's interventions and anomalous gold sit inside an account of modern commerce and knowledge. The literal word Cabal here also names a motley galley crew, not proof by itself of an occult world order.
- The System of the World: Daniel returns to reconcile rivals, investigates infernal devices; Jack counterfeits and attacks the Mint's system. Succession and money intertwine. Newton's apparent death and alchemical revival, unusual gold and the Trial of the Pyx are genuine anomalies, not dismissed as mere superstition. Jack survives hanging through human intervention and reunites with Eliza. These facts explain why the older fantasy interpretation was reasonable before the complete later frame.
- Cryptonomicon: WWII codebreaking and Unit2702's deception protect the fact Enigma has been broken; Bobby Shaftoe, Lawrence Waterhouse and Goto Dengo intersect with stolen gold and burial operations. Descendants in1990s pursue a cryptographic data haven and private digital money in Southeast Asia, with Randy Waterhouse and Amy Shaftoe, while inherited gold and old adversaries join both timelines. Enoch survives an apparent death and recurs across them. The creator explicitly describes the dual timeline and deception premise; SFE identifies information and money as its central concern. This is not standalone evidence of backward time travel.
- REAMDE: Richard Forthrast's T'Rain game economy and a Chinese ransomware attack collide with stolen financial data, Russian gangsters, espionage and terrorists. Zula's kidnapping pulls a geographically dispersed cast toward the Canadian/US border. It resolves in an Idaho gun battle and rescue, not a literal game-world apocalypse. Guardian stresses that the final thriller grows beyond the game. It supplies Forthrast family and technological-capital context for Fall, not a separate afterlife story.
- Fall: Dodge's medical death leaves a cryopreserved/scanned brain. Sophia later activates the connectome, which has severe amnesia and constructs Bitworld from fragments of memory. Other scanned minds follow. The physical world develops pervasive augmented/network systems, disinformation and costly compute; El Shepherd seizes the digital world, casts down Egdod/Dodge and imposes tyranny. Sophia's murdered consciousness enters it, and a quest overturns El's rule. Living and digital casts join the outcome, with technological rather than literal religious afterlife. Enoch's final exchange with Zula suggests a prior/higher reality and the completion of his task. The creator confirms a simulation interpretation is intentionally hinted but refuses a definitive exposition. Do not turn that careful answer into a claim every apparently magical detail is formally explained.

## Actual-tree root-down walk
1. Speculative fiction > Science fiction. Whole-fiction evidence changes the earlier isolated Baroque inference: brains scanned into digital minds, computational worldmaking, quantum/distributed computing and nested simulation are actual mechanisms. Root's longevity/alchemical interventions are deliberately uncertain within that later technological frame; author endorses the higher-simulation inference as a sensible intended hint. This meets recorded rule36's genuine ambiguity and rule44's no-proven-magic plus SF-tropes direction. It does not assert all fantasy-like events are fully demystified, nor require that as an invented extra rule. No positive proof of independent supernatural magic remains strong enough to override this whole-fiction context.
2. Fantasy rival fully tested: Real earth > Historical > Secret history > Occult cabals fits Baroque read alone. The completed universe is neither solely a past-era hidden society nor an independent invented fantasy realm. It includes physical present/future Earth and a manufactured digital fantasy afterlife. That constructed realm does not meet the owner's independent-fantasy-universe exclusion: its Earth technological origin is explicit. Portal fantasy fails because computational copies and simulations are not an Earth/faerie portal journey. Literal gods in Bitworld are human scans with compute, not proof of outside divine magic.
3. SF > Futurist SF. This is not the endpoint rule. The actual overarching concern is science, information, money and technological systems remaking human arrangements, a through-line from SFE's modernity/capitalism discussion through the digital monetary haven and T'Rain to brain/afterlife computing. No single leaf captures all of that. Under the owner's8November subset rule, the famous actual mind-upload concept in Fall supplies an honest discoverable reader hook, while the later technological frame makes that placement meaningful across the formerly magical-seeming continuity. Historical SF would describe three earliest volumes but omit the defining Earth-future frame; Modern day SF's Crypto thriller is exact for Cryptonomicon alone but not the linked future. Atemporal must be checked, not assumed from the centuries-long span.
4. Atemporal and time-travel check: long-lived Root, chronological skips and time ratios between simulations do not by themselves establish travel backward through Earth's time. Early fan theories proposed time travel; later author discussion supports nested simulation instead. No actual backward-time episode established in any included novel. No approved time-travel exception is invented. Atemporal's Fabulation is a serious rival for reality layers, but the actual mechanism is computational minds, not books literally revising their own narrative or a metatextual storyworld. Owner17October original phonemsg-01M55HT7W5XG2G57ECPHQ36DEZ says simulation theory is Futurist rather than Atemporal. The subsequent Extremes retraction concerned whether that particular book really was simulation theory, not a revocation of that temporal distinction.
5. Futurist > Future of Earth fiction, not Spacefaring/Alien fiction: Earth cultures, Earth history and Earth-made networks/digital people. Hard SF is a serious intellectual mode but the defining point is transformed human existence, not uniformly rigorous physical-science problem solving. No travel-to-aliens or interstellar civilization is the spine.
6. Future of Earth > Techno fiction rather than Dystopian/Future war/Future societies/Utopian/Psionics. Networks, currency and increasingly mind technologies make social changes possible; no single regime, war or psi science organizes all six. Fall's Ameristan, El's regime and the digital final conflict are elements, not grounds to erase the technological frame.
7. Techno fiction > Technomorphosis. Machine minds would imply AI as the subject rather than copied humans and their technological personhood. Cyberpunk > Metaverse is a genuine rival because T'Rain and Bitworld are virtual worlds. Yet the latter's inhabitants are copied deceased minds, not living users logging in through avatars. The irreversible transformation of death/self is more specific than shared immersive place. LitRPG fails literal stats/levels/loot as story laws; no numerical progression contract governs this fiction. Neural fiction's direct-brain access is not the point of destructive posthumous copying. Postcyberpunk is broad enough for networked society but less exact than copied identity. No Simulation theory seed is necessary when an established exact human-mind leaf exists and its strongest supporting book is already there.
8. Technomorphosis > Posthuman rather than Post-society: what a person is and whether mind persists after bodily death is the specific late-frame question. Posthuman > Remade minds rather than Remade bodies: connectomes/scanned selves, not designed anatomy. Remade minds > Copied minds rather than Rewired minds: patterns are recorded/transferred, not only altered in the original brain. Copied minds already holds Fall and comparable digital societies such as Permutation City/Diaspora. Keep its definition unchanged.
9. Countercheck against endpoint fallacy: not claiming every novel has uploads. Existing historical volumes outweigh Fall in pages, and money/information is the widest common theme. Since the actual tree has no honest existing all-era money-and-information-systems leaf, apply the user's explicit subset/famous-concept permission. Creating an abstract singleton would be worse than retaining a real distinctive book's already-established leaf. Confidence moderate, record the trade-off openly.

Final path: Speculative fiction > Science fiction > Futurist SF > Future of Earth fiction > Techno fiction > Technomorphosis > Posthuman > Remade minds > Copied minds.
