## Every constituent's own engine
The Hundred Thousand Kingdoms: Yeine is summoned as an Arameri heir after her mother's death; dynastic struggle at Sky conceals divine war, slavery and revenge. Enslaved Nahadoth/Sieh and Enefa's remnant within Yeine drive events; ending transforms her from mortal to divine and frees gods. Dynastic magic is genuinely strong for this volume, not dismissed.
The Broken Kingdoms: ten years later blind artist Oree shelters Shiny/Itempas, a punished god in exile, and investigates murdered godlings. His arrogance, guilt, intimacy and recognition of harm govern the divine emotional arc; Oree's demon blood is lethal to gods. No court succession contest dominates. Strange Horizons completedreview explicitly calls it Itempas's redemption and charts divine/human power imbalance.
The Kingdom of Gods: divine child Sieh narrates his unexpected aging/mortality after bonding with Arameri twins Shahar/Dekarta. His divine nature conflicts with maturation, responsibility and intimacy; family grievances and reconciliation with Itempas meet Maelstrom-scale crisis and a transformed divine order. Political Arameri legacy remains substantial, but experiencing divine existence is sustained subject, not occasional god-viewpoint.
The Awakened Kingdom: newborn godling Shill narrates attempts to inherit Sieh's role, discovers her own affinity/purpose, learns what gods owe fragile mortals, and meets Darren man Eino's gendered constraints. Author explicitly novella, completed SkiffyFanty review closely analyzes godhood learning and changing narrator voice. Already present fourth volume retained as novella.
