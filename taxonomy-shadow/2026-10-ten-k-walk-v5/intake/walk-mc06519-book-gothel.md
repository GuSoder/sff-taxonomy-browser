# The Book of Gothel, MC06519
24 November 2026

## Decision
Place Mary McMyne's2022 standalone in existing Occult cabals,8to9. Whole fiction one novel. No new genre, definition or portrait. Primary creator accounts and historical notes establish a suppressed magical circle linking women of medieval Germany, including Hildegard and Queen Beatrice, beneath the male/Church historical record. The circle's secrets, initiation, concealed Mother worship and revision of what scribes recorded provide the Secret history gate beyond the loose marketing label. Confidence moderate on this branch choice; high on corpus, date, magic, Earth and historical bulk. Medieval folklore is the closest existing rival.

## Corpus and research standard
One2022 novel. Not a shared series with A Rose by Any Other Name: creator's Goodreads answer explicitly says the second contracted historical retelling is not a Gothel sequel, and current press kit lists the novels separately. Common theme and research do not establish shared continuity. Wolf Skin is an excluded poetry chapbook. No evidence of a further published Gothel novel was found. Editions/translations are not added as books.

No full novel read claimed. Read creator interviews, essay and publisher book-club kit with authorized extracts, publisher and creator title pages, several independent finished reviews and public reader ending comments. Search surfaced automated chapter summaries; they are not used as final plot authority. No unauthorized full-book mirror fetched. Exact mechanics of every final rescue, the complete old-woman discovery twist, and the fate of Ursilda after childbirth are not fully established. Do not invent them.

## Whole-story check
Germany1156. Haelewise grows up as the isolated, black-eyed daughter of midwife Hedda and a fisherman, subject to fainting episodes and suspicion of possession. Her father's Christian remedies and her mother's older practices differ. Hedda dies; Haelewise loses protection and means of support, and Matthaus cannot simply marry her against his father's wishes. Driven from the village, she seeks the tower Gothel and apprentices to Kunegunde, eventually revealed as her grandmother. She learns that her mother's forbidden inheritance and her own abilities are real: alrune/mandrake, soul-sensing in birth, soul travel in animal bodies, protective magic and a Mother-centered secret tradition. This is not merely an untreated illness mistaken for magic.

The threatened young woman Rika and a violent prince pull Haelewise beyond the tower into royal and religious danger. Finished reviews establish witnessed murder, a search for justice, revenge with a dagger, and her dealings with Hildegard and royal women. The creator describes the hidden circle of wise women and sorceresses, including Beatrice of Burgundy and Hildegard, that Haelewise seeks to enter. Her grandmother's restrictions, inherited faith, longing for family and motherhood, and the choice to protect other women shape her journey. Prince Ulrich's wolf-skin transformation, invisibility cloaks and soul/animal magic are confirmed by the creator and publisher kit, not inferred from fairy-tale imagery.

Late-act and endpoint: independent Bookreporter review says the familiar Rapunzel accusation is overturned: Haelewise saves rather than steals the child and no one is imprisoned. Fantasy Cafe independently says the main action ends shortly after Rapunzel's birth, with a few pages of later highlights. Public reader Q&A identifies Ursilda as the child's mother and raises unresolved questions about why she does not reclaim the child; those questions are evidence of reader uncertainty, not grounds to invent her later fate. The book is chiefly Haelewise's early life and becoming the tower's wise woman, not a full retelling of Rapunzel's familiar adolescent escape. The modern scholar Gertrude Eisenberg discovers/translates the manuscript in the opening and closing bookends. Several finished reviews agree that the medieval narrative occupies the book between those frames; no coequal modern adventure or physical time travel is established.

## Actual-tree root-down hard walk
1. Speculative fiction -> Fantasy, not Science fiction. Creator confirms genuine transformations by wolf-skins, women inhabiting animals, invisibility cloaks and effective historical magical recipes; soul magic is independently described by finished reviewers. No scientific demystification or SF mechanism was found. Fainting illness alone would not suffice, but the later confirmed magic does.
2. SF-side Fabulation rival: manuscript discovery and a woman correcting a received story are narrative framing, not literal textual trespass, editable reality or a book transporting the scholar. No reality-editing mechanism is evidenced. Do not equate feminist revision with literal Fabulation.
3. Fantasy -> Real earth fantasy. Explicit1156Germany, Black Forest, Frederick Barbarossa, Beatrice and Hildegard. Not an independent fantasy world; magical alterations of Earth do not make Fantasy realms. Fairytale villain inversion would be an excellent leaf-level description but fails its Fantasy realms ancestor here. No licensed exception was found and none is invented. Portal fantasy fails because Earth/otherworld exchange is not the organizing journey; visions and soul projection are powers inside this world.
4. Real earth -> Historical fantasy. Modern discovery bookends do not outweigh the medieval bulk. The manuscript is written by old Haelewise but narrates her early medieval life. Not Contemporary, Future, time travel or a multi-era chronicle whose span is the point.
5. Historical -> Secret history, rather than Period, Alternate history or Mythic retelling. This is the main wobble. The story is strongly a medieval life narrative and its witch/folk magic could honestly attract a Medieval folklore reader. However, the primary creator essay explicitly reconstructs what Church scribes left out, why Hildegard devised a secret language, what manuscripts suppressed, and how Beatrice's hidden magical power fills gaps in the record. A secret women's tradition links peasant midwife, abbess and queen; Haelewise's desire for admission and conflict over concealed knowledge are central throughout. These are positive grounds for hidden history, not only an external label calling it Rapunzel's secret history. The manuscript's later discovery makes the recovered suppressed version explicit.
6. Secret history -> Occult cabals. The hidden truth is a human magical circle operating beneath the recorded religious and royal world, not a concealed nonhuman people, so Arcane peoples fails. Untold magic is the closest sibling: it holds magic operating in a documented movement or era. Here the concealed hand is specifically the secret interconnected circle of wise women, sorceresses and royal/religious practitioners. The source describes the circle and historical-record reconstruction directly. Occult cabals does not require an evil organization or global conspiracy. Its existing unchanged definition covers occult orders working behind recorded events, which matches this circle's covert part in the religious/royal world.
7. Period route tested fully as the strongest rival: Period > Antique times > Dark age > Medieval fantasy > Medieval folklore. Before1800, Christian High Middle Ages, not Celtic/Viking/Andalusian cultural stage, classical, early-modern Age of sail, Edo, Mughal or Abbasid. Within Medieval, not chiefly ghost/revenant returns for Haunting, not Fair Folk polity for Fey. Medieval spiritual is plausible for religion and the Mother but this is not open divine war or a whole publicly spiritual order. Medieval folklore fits witches, wolf-skins and old powers. It loses to the more specific suppressed-circle historical reconstruction established by creator and text evidence, not because period life is unimportant.
8. Alternate history fantasy fails: no openly divergent magical timeline/publicly magic-run polity is established. The rhetoric is missing/suppressed history behind the known record, not a new public timeline.
9. Mythic retelling is a serious rival because Rapunzel and other inherited tales matter. Its current children were checked: no Arthurian/Frankish chivalric cycle, Norse gods/hero cycle, Mesoamerican, Biblical, Slavic, Greco-Roman, Irish/Welsh or Japanese inherited narrative governs. German fairy tale alone does not imply Norse fantasy. More importantly, creator and finished reviews describe an invented historical origin for tales rather than retelling the traditional Rapunzel action as the main narrative. This is not a homeless mythic work requiring a singleton German seed when the covert historical circle already supplies an honest existing leaf. Fairytale revision on Fantasy realms cannot be reached by bypassing the setting fork.

Final path: Speculative fiction > Fantasy > Real earth fantasy > Historical fantasy > Secret history > Occult cabals.

## Caveats
Do not claim the circle controls all imperial history or that the book is principally a political thriller. Its human story is motherhood and belonging. The narrower judgment is that secret historical women and their suppressed magical tradition organize the revealed world enough to pass Secret history. If direct late text instead established that the circle is background only and does not explain the historical/royal/religious record, Medieval folklore would be the existing fallback. No definition is widened to settle this. Modern Frau Vogel's full identity/twist is not established beyond rediscovery and is not asserted. Bookey automated summaries are not used as evidence; review uncertainty about Ursilda remains uncertainty. First two failed creator cover URLs and generic US publisher homepage response were not treated as verified title sources.
