## Scope, original dates and whole-story evidence
The publisher explicitly identifies Dawn as the duology's conclusion. The first book's UK original hardback was 28 September 2023. Its 2024 US edition does not move the original year. Dawn was published in 2025, confirmed by the US publisher's 12 August 2025 on-sale date; the UK page's July 2026 is a paperback edition. No third book, unrelated author fiction, omnibus or short work is counted.

Sun follows Leonora/Tecuani, a mixed Spanish/Nahua woman in 1550s New Spain, secretly the shapeshifting vigilante Pantera. Her resistance to Spanish persecution, double life at court, ancestral weapons and relationship with Tezca are substantial parts of the fiction. The late-book plot is not merely decorative mythology: banned rites permit the Obsidian Butterfly/Itzpapalotl and star demons to threaten the current cosmic age; her mentor Toto is Quetzalcoatl and her father; divine lineage and returned tonalli let her confront the catastrophe. The licensed retailer's chapter-by-chapter ending discussion corroborates the entire final section, not only the publisher blurb.

Dawn begins after that victory. Leonora hides in panther form while her tonalli drains, returns to court as viceregent, faces northern resistance and the real prince, and confronts a false king seeking to summon the god of night. Divine origins and Tezca's transformation into a god remain sustained concerns. The publisher's conclusion synopsis and several completed reader accounts agree on those features. The final battle and reworked Spanish/Chichimeca conflict matter too. The exact final constitutional status of Mexico was not established and is not asserted here.

The research did not read both full copyrighted novels. It compared primary author interviews and publisher descriptions, a licensed retailer's final-chapters readalong, professional finished reviews of the first book, a finished independent second-book review, and multiple completed reader accounts. DNF accounts are not used as ending evidence. Long generic promotional-looking reader essays were not needed to settle claims supported by more specific accounts.

## Contradiction retained
Fantasy Book Nerd's completed Dawn review calls independence from Spain the overarching driving plot; several Goodreads finishers likewise stress political conflict. Other finishers emphasize Tezca's godhood, Leonora's divine identity and active Aztec gods. This is a real political/mythic straddle. The recommendation is not that politics is incidental. Rather, the named inherited cosmic order is a defining concept across both novels and is not replaceable with generic magic. Confidence is moderate, not categorical.

## Full root-down traversal
Speculative fiction > Fantasy. Tonalli, shapeshifting, ancestral magical weapons and literal inherited gods are actual within the story. No technology or naturalistic explanation displaces them. Science fiction's historical, modern, futurist and atemporal routes therefore fail the owner's any-literal-magic boundary. No experimental frame makes Fabulation the governing contract.

Fantasy > Real earth fantasy. Mexico City, New Spain, Spanish rule and inherited Nahua cosmology identify Earth's historical universe. Fantasy realms' Epic, Sword and sorcery, Heritage, Far shores, Comic and Heart routes fail that setting ancestor. Fifth sun is an invented-world counterpart shelf, not a substitute for real Mexico. Portal fantasy fails: shapeshifting, divine realms and supernatural meetings do not make Earth/otherworld exchange the organizing story.

Real earth > Historical fantasy. Explicit sixteenth-century setting, not author-present Contemporary or Future. A 2025 publication date is irrelevant to the story's time.

Historical children:
* Period passes the broad era match but loses priority to subject and openly remade history. Its Industrial and Long chronicle routes fail era/span. Antique times > Age of sail > Virreinato is the exact existing period rival. Renaissance and Rococo are European and/or later, not honest alternatives to Mexico. Other Antique regional/era shelves do not improve the fit.
* Secret history fails as governing subject. Leonora's concealed identity is important, but this is not primarily a hidden magical explanation preserving documented history with secrecy itself the historical engine. Magic, divine conflict and political resistance are acted in the world. Arcane peoples, Occult cabals and Untold magic inherit that failed gate.
* Alternate history fantasy passes. All eight children were tested: Crusade is medieval Christendom/Levant; Ming is China; Spice route is medieval Indian Ocean; Flintlock begins seventeenth-century Europe/American frontier; Diesel is twentieth-century; Renaissance expressly Europe; Swashbuckler expressly seventeenth-century; Steampunk is industrial nineteenth/pre-WWI. New Spain in the 1550s falls in none. This produces the candidate Alt Virreinato fantasy. No existing gate is broadened.
* Mythic retelling passes the active reimagining of inherited gods and cosmology. Original protagonists do not disqualify it, matching the existing Mayan precedent. Arthurian, Norse, Biblical, Slavic, Frankish, Classical, Celtic and Japanese traditions are not the inherited source. Mayan is geographically adjacent but culturally not an Aztec shelf. New Aztec myth fantasy is required on this route.

## Proposed carve and honest ceiling
Aztec myth fantasy: Fantasy that retells or reimagines inherited Nahua/Mexica (Aztec) gods, narratives and cosmology as the story's source. Not merely an Aztec historical setting; not a wholly invented Mesoamerican-inspired world; not Maya mythology; not myth alive in the author's present-day world. The familiar Aztec label is used for findability, while the definition preserves the author's more exact Nahua/Mexica distinction.

It is not confined to colonial resistance, a female Zorro, shapeshifting or this one pair of books. Those would be narrow premise-carves. It covers the full inherited tradition, before or after conquest where the mythic framework governs a past-set fantasy.

Verified kin, not automatically moved or added: Aliette de Bodard's Obsidian and Blood trilogy. Her own series page identifies the three connected Acatl novels, and her author guest essay describes literal Aztec gods, cosmic survival through sacrifice and active star-demon threats throughout the series. It is not currently placed in this tree. This establishes more than one plausible fiction for the broad carve, but only the Lares seed is proposed for intake now. No full separate Acatl root walk or new addition is smuggled into approval. The Bone Flower trilogy and Chalchiuhite Dragon are leads, not claimed verified members.

Mesoamerican myth fantasy: Umbrella for the inherited Mesoamerican myth traditions, initially Mayan and Aztec. Two leaves, three fictions and five novels after the seed: Gods of Jade and Shadow one novel, Sky Knife two novels, and Lares two novels. Mayan remains intact with two fictions/three novels; Aztec begins with one fiction/two novels. The umbrella does not claim that all Indigenous American traditions are one culture. It does not include Inca/Andean material, and it excludes invented-world Far shores counterparts.

The shared regional-cultural umbrella is grounded by scholarly treatments of Maya and Mexica-Aztec traditions as distinct within a shared Mesoamerican religious framework. This is a real common tradition region, unlike an arbitrary Europe/rest-of-world bundle. It follows the existing Classical/Celtic pattern and avoids a shallow new Historical sibling.
