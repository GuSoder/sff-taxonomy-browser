# Dragon Ops whole-fiction placement
23 November 2026

Place Mari Mancusi's two published Dragon Ops novels together in Private metaverse. This is a technological shared-world adventure, with closed beta-test access in both books. The first physical island uses immersive mixed reality; the sequel enters the unfinished Mech Ops game. Its dragons, apparent spells and digital dead girl have affirmative technological explanations. One fiction, two novels: Dragon Ops (2020), Dragon Ops: Dragons vs. Robots (2021). No published third was found in the current series bibliography.

## Root-down walk

Speculative fiction > Science fiction: the official first-novel sample explains GPS-mapped mixed reality, sensory suits, electronic brain synchronization and time compression. Atreus is perceived as a metal drone without goggles. The sequel's Ikumi is a digitized copy of the creator's deceased daughter according to the finished Kirkus review. This is positive mechanism evidence, not an inference from a publisher's genre label. Fantasy and its technological-looking fantasy rivals fail because no independently literal magic is established in the sources read.

Science fiction > Futurist SF: human technology beyond the writing present, not historical-era celebration, a time-travel premise, or a rigorously scientific puzzle. Modern-day SF is a real temporal rival because familiar gaming, internet culture and ordinary family life frame the story. The sensory/brain interface and commercial mixed-reality park are the prospective technology organizing the fiction, rather than a secret scientific intrusion into an otherwise ordinary present. No invented calendar year is needed. Atemporal SF fails: electronically compressed subjective time is not travel to another era.

Futurist SF > Future-of-Earth fiction > Techno-fiction: Earth-based human gaming technology and its power over bodies, senses and identities define the whole world. Not spacefaring, alien life or hard-science constraint. Test the rest of the Earth-future branches, not merely the dragon imagery: no environmental-collapse society, posthuman civilization or political future order governs these books. The technology creates the adventures.

Techno-fiction > Cyberpunk > Metaverse: invasive networked interfaces let people inhabit avatars and shared worlds, with a rogue AI controlling their experience. The whole-fiction reading experience is an immersive game-world survival and rescue adventure. Not cyberpunk noir, a hardboiled job, a heist against a social system, or ordinary life governed by ratings and platform terms.

Metaverse > Private metaverse: the first park has not opened to the public. The three children are specially invited into a test, alongside staff and a creator's daughter. The sequel's Mech Ops is still unfinished and its rescue party enters a closed test environment. Actual access is to designated participants of these projects. Eventual public commercial launch is not current public access. Platform metaverse is the closest access rival, but neither visited environment is open to anyone with an account, money or a rig in the sources read. Existing Private metaverse includes a research project's mind-linked world, so neural entry does not by itself disqualify this shelf.

## Serious sibling tests

1. LitRPG: current publisher copy explicitly uses the label and compares the books to Dungeon Crawler Carl. The sample mentions levels, quests and treasure; finished reviews describe boss objectives, character choice, low-level players and later high-level allies. These are genuine mechanics, not denied. However, the local leaf specifically requires that numbers are part of the reading and mechanics define the central experience. The official preview ends early in chapter 3, before creation/combat. Its exposed text does not establish numerical stats, and an isolated Google Books index entry for hit points cannot prove the required centrality. Reviews of both completed novels stress immersive-world peril and rescue, not stat-driven progression. Under this tree's explicit place-versus-mechanics distinction, Metaverse is the better fit. Do not silently broaden LitRPG to match marketing.
2. Neural fiction: first-novel sample explicitly describes electronic pulses synchronizing the brain, compressed experienced time and sensory feedback. That is more than ordinary screen goggles, and the fatal-danger mechanism depends on it. Nevertheless, brain passage is the access mechanism to the shared adventure world; the whole fiction is not primarily dream entry, alternative conscious realities or neural identities. Private metaverse's existing mind-projection example shows why direct brain access is not automatically decisive. This is the material wobble, with moderate confidence in the fine leaf, high confidence in the SF root.
3. AI-centered, robot and uploaded-mind shelves elsewhere: the rogue AI and uploaded girl are major actors, but the story follows human participants through shared games, not the life of an artificial society or the upload transition as the central project. The second book changes from fantasy-game scenery to robot-game scenery without changing that shared-world engine.

## Scope, fronts and limits

FictionDB lists two novels, first May 2020 and second June 2021, no new book scheduled. Kirkus gives first publication May 12, 2020 and sequel June 8, 2021. Do not count later paperback/audio editions as new books; do not infer a third from an older library blurb calling this a trilogy.
