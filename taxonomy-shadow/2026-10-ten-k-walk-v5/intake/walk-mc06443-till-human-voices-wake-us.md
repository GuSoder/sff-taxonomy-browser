## Whole story, including limits
Rudi, a radio preacher with brain damage, seeks recovery and family connection in near-future California. Benjamin, an architect in1937SanFrancisco with an inoperable tumor, seeks a higher self through Madame Grurie's teachings. Olavo, a posthuman copied mind operating in physical, soft and design spaces, is damaged by a cosmic discontinuity and tries to reconstruct himself. Finished criticism establishes these are linked lives, not merely three unrelated historical stories: Olavo shares the other men's memories, and the final strand draws together their outcomes and discloses the SF mechanism. Creator confirms three timelines and a psychological recovery concern. SFRevu describes the physical and virtual Olavos and a higher-order self across material planes; StoryGraph finished-reader evidence independently describes four-dimensional personality.

## Root-down walk, final
Speculative fiction > Science fiction: copied digital consciousness, quantum disruption and reconstruction frame the causal account, despite spiritual vocabulary. No proof of literal magic replaces it.
Science fiction > Atemporal SF: three temporally unanchored/interlinked selves and unstable realities span1937, near future and far future. No single historical/present/future era governs.
Atemporal > Fabulation > Inner space > Consciousness fiction > Unmoored selves: experimental intertwining of broken selves foregrounds unreliable identity and memory. The boundary between damaged consciousness and experienced world is the governing question. Unmoored selves expressly owns identity coming loose from body, memory or time, precisely this whole-novel experience.
The any-SF-time-travel rule still applies when actual temporal travel or communication is established. Here it is not: shared memories across three narrated eras do not by themselves prove an actual historical channel rather than reconstruction/higher-order identity.
