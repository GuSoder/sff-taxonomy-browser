## Root walk
Speculative fiction > Fantasy: actual One Power, Dark One, channeling and magical world destruction, not scientific explanation. Science-like magic rules are not sufficient for SF. Fantasy > Real earth fantasy rather than Fantasy realms: Jordan's archived verbatim1994AOLanswer says 'The books are set in our future and in our past, depending on which way you look.' The1996answer similarly says both, the characters are the sources of our myths and we of theirs. Earth is explicit, not inferred from a map or an Arthurian allusion. The old independently invented-universe gate fails even though the surface looks secondary-world. Portal fantasy is not the whole-fiction crossing engine: characters live in another age of Earth; dreamworlds and gateways do not make Earth-to-anotherworld travel the governing premise.
Real earth > Future fantasy: a cyclic rather than linear chronology is a real wobble. Author explicitly permits both past and future; choose the future-facing turn because our events become its legends and the material stage is Earth after a lost highly advanced civilization's magical collapse. This is not a claim Jordan asserted a uniquely linear future, nor a claim Earth loses its historical component. Historical fantasy's recognizable past-period shelves cannot describe the Age-of-Legends/Third-Age social world; Contemporary fails. Future fantasy has the positively fitting after-collapse setting and no redefinition required.
Future > Post-apocalyptic fantasy: the Age of Legends civilization fell in the Breaking; magical disaster literally remade geography and destroyed its institutions/knowledge. The books inhabit successor nations in that enduring aftermath, not simply a future without catastrophe. Real-world-biblical angels/demons do not govern its own invented Light/Shadow mythology, so End-times fails; vampires or never-fallen psychic society do not describe the whole fiction.
Post-apocalyptic > Post Magicalypse: Dark One taints saidin, male channelers go mad and use One Power to tear world/civilization apart. Material supernatural causation, not a secular flood, climate or nuclear disaster with fantasy afterward. Reactor's completed-series reread directly quotes the book's Breaking passage and notes it appears again in A Memory of Light; the foundation and its reversal are consequential through the ending. TarValon chapter-referenced history corroborates societal and technological collapse, not just one mountain appearing. This meets the current leaf without broadening it.
