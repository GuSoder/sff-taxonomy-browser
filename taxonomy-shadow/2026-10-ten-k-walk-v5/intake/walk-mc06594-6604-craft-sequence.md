## Decision
Consolidate existing Dead Country/Wicked Problems and Dead Hand Rule fictions as The Craft Sequence, add the six original novels, and keep the whole nine-novel universe in Technomancy. Existing leaf seven to six fictions. No genre, definition or portrait change. Publication order is used, not the numbers' internal chronology. Games, short fiction and omnibus duplication excluded.

## Root-down walk
Speculative fiction > Fantasy > Fantasy realms > Gothic realms > Technomancy.

Actual magic is verified by author, publisher, and independent review. Necromantic lawyers resurrect dead gods, souls are currency and divine contracts power cities. Technological analogy is not a scientific explanation. SF/Science fantasy fails at the root. This is an independent invented world, not a real-Earth historical setting; there is no verified Earth portal. Fantasy realms over Real earth and Portal.

Fantasy realms siblings: Epic fantasy is a real rival, with large cities, invented gods, revolution, international power and the later cosmic crisis. The distinctive whole-fiction attraction is nevertheless necromancy organized as engineering, business, infrastructure and law, not generic scope. Sword and Sorcery overweights fighting; Fairytale fantasy has no governing inherited tale or fairytale grammar; Heart fantasy is not driven by romance, cozy community or a horse bond; Far shores fantasy mistakes invented cultural influences for the governing about-ness; Comic fantasy mistakes wit and financial satire for an absurd/comic engine. Gothic realms fits occult institutions, dead gods and their dangerous remains, soul extraction, monstrous powers, deathless corporate rulers and the threatening systems that contain people's lives. This is not a claim of uniformly bleak tone.

Within Gothic realms: Fantasy mystery captures the first novel's investigation but not the whole universe's civic systems, economy and later wars; Isolationist fantasy has no governing sealed-world confinement; Fantasy of manners lacks the British imperial gentlemen/class-performance frame; Dark realms captures dread but not the organized necromantic machinery as precisely. Technomancy specifically names actual magic through technological forms, including necromancy as engineering. The author calls the setting postindustrial fantasyland and likens it to cyberpunk with magic instead of technology. Independent Library Journal describes the latest volume's distinctive magic system, falling markets and gods/corporations uniting against destruction. This is the same industrial-necromancy identity the owner recognized when he selected Technomancy for Dead Hand Rule on27September2026.
