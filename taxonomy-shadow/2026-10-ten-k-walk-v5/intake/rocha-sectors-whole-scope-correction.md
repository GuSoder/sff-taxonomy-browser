## Whole-fiction root-down walk
Speculative fiction: genetically engineered soldiers, implants/kill switches and altered human capabilities in post-Flare future. Fantasy rejected: no sourced literal magic; Rider soul-sacrifice/prophet language is an in-world religion and social institution, not verified supernatural power. Kora is military doctor from the Makhai project, not proof of magical healing. Primary author frames powers genetically. No time travel found.
Science fiction > Futurist SF: future America, author explicitly futuristic solar flares, Librarians independently2086. Historical/Modern day fail date; Atemporal fails no time/multiverse/consciousness premise. Futurist's other children: Hard SF fails rigorous scientific inquiry as engine, Spacefaring/Alien fail Earth-based human order. Future of Earth wins.
Future of Earth children: not Psionics or Techno-fiction, genetic enhancement supports social oppression and resistance rather than a power/posthuman inquiry; not Future war's military campaign structure for whole15; not Future societies' social inquiry as primary promise; not Utopian. Dystopian fiction catches the built regimes and their overthrow.
Dystopian children: Post-holocaust survival is a real rival but collapse is established background, not immediate living aftermath/survival engine. Ruined Earth's later lost-knowledge/wild successor-culture frame fails. Dying Earth fails terminal world. Social dystopia is less exact than named theocratic/corporate totalitarian orders. Totalitarian wins.
Totalitarian children: Huxleyan conditioning/engineered bodies and Orwellian repression are both real apparatus; the recurring whole arc follows those resisting and overthrowing orders already in power, not primarily depicting compliance. Dystopian rebellion remains the positive home.

## Whole-fiction straddles
Beyond9is an explicit primary-author resistance-to-overthrow arc. Completed finale review independently traces the buildup from first installment to war against Eden and final resolution. Librarians3moves from heist/survival toward organised Atlanta revolution, explicit in final novel synopsis. Riders3is genuinely post-dystopian, set6months after Beyond's ending, featuring refugees, dynastic intrigue and assassination attempts rather than a new overthrow. This is a substantial three-novel aftermath straddle within one shared fiction, not hidden or redefined as rebellion. Romance and found family drive individual novels throughout; no separate SF-romance leaf should be invented from them. Broad catastrophe survival and genetic-engineering leaves would distort the larger combined arc more.
