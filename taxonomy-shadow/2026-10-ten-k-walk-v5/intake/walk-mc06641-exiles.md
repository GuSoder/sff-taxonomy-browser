# Exiles whole-fiction walk, 26 November 2026

One shared fiction, two published novels: The Ruins of Ambrai (1994) and The Mageborn Traitor (1997). Queue MC06641 Exiles1995 is PRH's reprint title of the first book, not a third novel. MC06650 folds into the same fiction. Current ISFDB marks The Captal's Tower unpublished; projected2006/2009 dates elsewhere do not establish publication.

Root-down: Speculative fiction > Fantasy > Real earth fantasy > Future fantasy > Space fantasy. Literal words/gestures, memory wards, Wild Magic and Wraithenbeasts establish genuine magic, not technological marvels inferred from offworld ancestry. The novel index, read aloud in Hot Nuance chapter5 and independently discussed by Pat's completed reviews, identifies Lenfell as a future human colony: second Great Migration2458-2493, mainly Catholic settlers and a seven-year starship voyage. This is positive provenance, not an inference from names. No independent fantasy universe invented to force an Epic placement. A wiki hint about Earth's demise is not independently corroborated and is not used to manufacture an Earth apocalypse.

Whole two-book engine: rival Mage Guardian and Malerris institutions in a matrilineal caste society. Glenin joins her father and Malerris; Sarra joins the Rising then governs and pursues legal/economic equality reforms; Cailet becomes Mage Captal and rebuilds Mage Hall. Book2 develops the next generation, Glenin's hidden son as planted traitor, and destruction of Cailet's institution with deaths and damaged reforms. Family secrets and magical political power organize both novels. No ending of the unpublished third book is invented.

All ancestors and siblings tested. Magic politics, Dynastic intrigue and Revolutionary fantasy are strong thematic rivals but fail the independent-universe Fantasy realms ancestor here. Council/Assembly and dictatorship evidence does not establish a hereditary throne. Collan's bard role does not make the whole fiction a performance career. Memory wards are not proven rewritten history, and rapid narrative years are not traveler displacement; the podcast's time machine refers to its1994 retrospective. No demonstrated authoring/revision of lived reality supports Fabulation. Neither a present-day nor actual past-era frame governs. No sustained Earth-to-otherworld exchange makes Portal fantasy the common subject.

Future siblings: Post-apocalyptic children explicitly require future Earth, whereas the Waste Wars devastated Lenfell. No verified Earth-collapse aftermath justifies borrowing either cause leaf. End-times lacks biblical apocalypse, Vampire future lacks vampire return, Psionic fantasy's suppressed-emotion social order does not explain real magecraft, and Dying Earth fantasy requires terminal Earth rather than old offworld institutions. Former Space fantasy's sustained-spacefaring gate excluded migration-only backstory. That exact scope gap was presented, not concealed.
