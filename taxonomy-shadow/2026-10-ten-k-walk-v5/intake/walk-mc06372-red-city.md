# The New Alchemists, Red City intake
23 November 2026

## Decision and scope

Existing Occult underworlds2to3, moderate fine-leaf confidence. One fiction, The New Alchemists, with one published novel Red City2025. Author website calls this Red City Series; both current publishers use The New Alchemists. Silver Dawn is the same characters/world but remains forthcoming: Tor US27April2027, Pan UK29April2027. Record the forthcoming continuation without adding an unpublished book/card or calling the series complete. No other Marie Lu series is evidenced as this universe.

## Whole published story

Sam Lang and Ari Rathod are immigrant-background childhood friends in Angel City, explicitly alternate present-day Los Angeles. Both enter competing alchemical crime syndicates, Sam through Grand Central after her mother's financial/medical crisis, Ari through recruitment by Lumines with his Indian family used as leverage. Childhood training and friendship give way to adult advancement, violence, control, ambition and star-crossed love. Alchemy consumes portions of the practitioner's soul. Sand, made from the philosopher's stone, enhances users and finances the criminal order.

Full published-novel evidence was checked beyond blurbs: Paste, Kirkus, Grimdark, Quill to Live, BiblioSanctum and Worlds Unlike Our Own finished reviews; public SuperSummary plot guide used with attribution as a secondary synopsis, not a substitute for a claimed primary read. The guide traces assassinations, Connie's murder, Sam's bargain with detective Sinclair, the prisoner exchange and police raid, collapse of Grand Central, rival leadership takeover, and Sam's informant role as a bridge to the next syndicate in Londinium. Current publisher sequel synopsis independently confirms the collapse, continuing protagonists and European syndicate. Exact ages disagree among review/guide accounts, not needed for placement and not asserted. No full novel read.

The criminal social world persists through the ending. A detective and informant appear, but the published whole is not primarily a police case or covert state-protection mission. The sequel is not classified as if published, and its blurb does not overwrite the first novel's actual arc.

## Root-down walk and rivals

Speculative fiction > Fantasy: hand transmutation is fueled by literal soul expenditure. Author interview explicitly presents it as a magic system studied as if science; her first-person guest essay likewise calls it magic that reshapes real history. The promotional excerpt contains an in-world dark-matter/science claim and molecular vocabulary, retained as a genuine SF rival rather than ignored. These are the fictional practitioners' explanation, not a nonmagical scientific demonstration. Primary author framing and soul-cost ontology pass the owner's literal-magic Fantasy gate. Neither science-flavored alchemy nor a performance drug makes it SF, cyberpunk or Freakbusters.

Fantasy > Real earth fantasy: author and publisher explicitly identify alternate contemporary Los Angeles, China, India, Oxford and Europe. Angel City is not an independent fantasy universe merely because renamed. Fantasy realms fails the positive Earth evidence; Portal fails no governing crossing to another world.

Real earth > Contemporary fantasy: publisher expressly present-day; childhood and adult portions do not make historical eras. Past gods explained as early alchemists are backstory, not a retold ancient myth. No future aftermath or apocalypse governs. Alternate history is a superficial rival, but there is no past-era primary setting or divergence campaign.

Contemporary children:
- Urban fantasy wins, metropolitan secret institutions and criminal magical power organize work, family and relationships.
- Magical coming-of-age is substantial: recruitment, instruction, tests and adolescent identity matter. Magic school is the strongest deeper rival. But the adult syndicate careers, violence, leverage, betrayal and criminal collapse take the book beyond school life; the school is the recruitment/training mechanism, not the whole narrative institution. Adult marketing alone does not settle this, the whole arc does. Birthright/found-power leaves fail inherited-family or accident-found primary access.
- Domestic fantasy: mother-daughter relationship is central emotionally, as Quill to Live argues, but household/family is not the source/site of a multigenerational magic practice. Family pain and ambition feed entry into the urban criminal order.
- Dark fantasy: brutal torture and manipulation are present, but neither haunted destination nor a pursuing supernatural tormentor is the whole framework. Social institutions wield the violence.
- Contemporary mythic: Greek names and explanations of old gods occur, but no living inherited pantheon/retelling is the central engine. Author calls ancient gods disguised human alchemists.
- Supernatural fantasy: not a single creature-kind story, ghost/afterlife/immortality story, or demon-ownership bargain. Souls spent as fuel do not mean a devil purchases souls. Evil deals is therefore weaker than organized alchemical society.
- Magical realism fails named, trained and explicitly theorized occult practice. Folktale fails tale-shape/comic moral engine.

Urban children:
- Modern Goth > Modern occult wins, coercive secret organized social life carries the uncanny power and harm. This ancestor is not being reduced to a haunted mansion or horror mood: its actual definition includes occult institutions/professions/cities, and recruitment/ritual hierarchy, bodily violation and predatory ambition are materially present.
- Fae founders fails Faerie peoples/courts; these are human alchemists, not fae by metaphorical resemblance to courts.
- Publicly paranormal fails explicit secrecy. Author interview says outsider detective barely understands alchemy; public elite consumption of a drug is not public understanding of its magical origin.
- Paranormal bricks: city is a recognizable political/economic arena, not a sentient city or metaphysical double/nightmare as whole subject.
- Occult mystery is real through Sinclair's investigation and late informant arrangement but does not govern the main protagonists' entire published story. Occult secret service similarly fails recurring state containment/protection.
- Modern Weird West fails metropolitan rather than ranch/frontier society. Freakbusters fails occult transmutation rather than engineered weird-science enemy.

Modern Goth children: Modern occult positively fits secret alchemical social order. Postcolonial Gothic is tempting because of immigrant vulnerability, but there is no ancestral/colonial haunting returning as agent. Suburban Gothic lacks neighbourhood persecution as source; War Gothic lacks returning war dead.

Modern occult children:
- Occult city wins: competing criminal empires, secret elite drug economy and city turf war shape both protagonists across training and adulthood.
- Occult institutions > Occult orders is a serious rival, not dismissed. Each syndicate recruits/trains/names/tests/rules its alchemists; this would honestly fit the broad leaf. Prefer Occult city because the organizations' territorial conflict, crime-boss patronage, assassinations, commodities and collapse of urban power remain the governing engine after initiation. No definition change or claim of mutually exclusive leaves. Fine-leaf moderate.
- Workplace occultism fails its explicit no-magical-society gate. This is not an ordinary office job with occult incidents.
- Occult bloodline fails inherited practiced family tradition or born claim; immigrant family relationships do not establish hereditary occult transmission.
- Modern technomancy fails magic running through media/machinery. Experimental sand production and alchemical research alone are not a technology/media magic conduit.

Occult city children:
- Occult underworlds wins, hidden magical society concretely criminal: mob families, weapons, syndicate leaders, brutal discipline, territorial war. Two protagonists' belonging/loyalty and escape from these structures are the whole arc.
- Cursed markets is substantial because sand is manufactured and sold and control funds the syndicates. Prefer Underworlds because the story follows members rising in, warring through and breaking their criminal society, not a discrete occult commodity's purchase/theft/debt as the main adventure. Commodity trade supplies their power; the gangland relationships are the action.
- City soul trade: literal soul erosion matters, but no demon wager, damnation or return from Hell, and souls are fuel rather than merchandise transferred between owners. Do not choose from the word soul.
- Haunted cities lacks the city's dead/history/gods as present haunting. Possessed citizens lacks external powers wearing bodies; drug effects and eroded empathy are not possession.

Other apparent matches elsewhere were rejected at ancestors: Caper/Contract assassins/Heart fantasy require Fantasy realms, Noir fantasy requires the1930s/40s setting. There are assassinations and forbidden love, but neither changes the positive contemporary Earth root. No shallow seed needed.
