## Actual story and world
Selene Dreshé's father, once King's Mage, went mad and tried to kill her. She killed him in self-defense and grew up at the opera house, determined to restore his name by winning the royal musical-magic competition. A rival steals her aria, forcing a dangerous improvised performance. Below the opera house she discovers a forbidden mirror containing a nameless ghost, imprisoned for a century. He offers forbidden blood magic in exchange for freedom. Her growing obsession, dangerous lessons, buried institutional secrets, the threat behind the King's office and the competition converge. She wins the office, but its apparent triumph is a trap: the creator's published sequel synopsis reveals the memory-eroding royal necklace and enforced magical service. I use that synopsis to check the first book's ending, not to pretend the future sequel is published or fully read.

This is not Paris with real history added. In her November Fresh Fiction interview the author explicitly calls Mondreves a French-inspired secondary world. In Coachella Review she discusses the invented kingdom's France-like nineteenth-century culture and other countries. The mirror is internal to this universe, not Earth-to-another-world portal fantasy. Actual sung magic and blood magic are literal, corroborated by creator description and authorized excerpt. No SF pseudoscience or technology disguise. No fiction authored into existence or editable narrative reality: composing spell music is an in-world craft, not books-in-books causality revision.

## Full tree walk and rivals
Speculative fiction > Fantasy > Fantasy realms > Gothic realms > Dark realms > Haunted realms.

At Fantasy root, Real earth fantasy fails on the creator's positive secondary-world statement, so Gothic in gaslight and Devilish fame deals are not candidates despite their tempting period/opera/Faustian furniture. Portal fantasy fails because no Earth is included. At Fantasy realms, Epic fantasy is the strongest content rival because Selene is a music caster and the office has political stakes. It loses at its ancestor gate: the published book is the intimate opera enclosure, grief, ambition and forbidden bargain, not epic geography, wars, a long journey or world-scale political construction. Bard hero would describe her power accurately but cannot bypass Epic fantasy; a musical protagonist alone does not prove epic register. Hard fantasy loses because a composed magical vocabulary is explained, but no source demonstrates rigorous system problem-solving as the governing reading experience. Political fantasy loses because court-level plotting is promised more fully for the future sequel, whereas the included book's royal prize is the trap reached through the opera-house story.

Sword and sorcery > Tournament fantasy is the strongest engine rival: clear rounds, rivals, sabotage and a royal prize. I tested it rather than ignoring the obvious competition. Its ancestor demands action-forward physical adventure, combat and battle. Here the contest consists of musical performance, desperate ambition, a haunted mentor bargain and looming ruin, not a tourney action-adventure. Tone alone does not exclude S&S, per owner rule50, but neither can any formal competition automatically admit a book past its action gate. Caper has no criminal job/heist, Pirate has no sea adventure, Sword and planet has no planetary romp, Technofantasy has no central machinery, Classic S&S has no classic wanderer lineage.

Fairytale fantasy loses because Phantom is the Gothic novel/musical source, not a fairy tale or fairytale quest grammar. Far shores loses because this is a France-like European kingdom, not a non-Western counterpart exploration. Comic realms has no governing comedy. Heart fantasy is a real rival, especially Romantasy: Selene has a ghost/childhood-prince triangle, and some reviewers use romantasy. The creator explicitly says the two men externalize her wants; the ghost begins as a means to win, and completed review says she uses both love interests toward the competition. Her power/legacy ambition and dangerous institution remain primary, not a co-equal romantic bond arc. Cozy fails on menace, death and ruin; Horse has no horse bond.

Gothic realms admits it positively: a forbidden subterranean mirror, imprisoned ghost, ancestral violence, a decaying artistic institution's secrets, the desire for forbidden power and the royal cage. This is the creator's repeated Gothic self-description supported by concrete mechanisms, not only marketing. At Gothic, Technomancy loses because mirror imprisonment/blood magic is not magic organized as engineering. Isolationist fantasy loses because confinement is a story setting and the ghost's curse, not a whole sealed civilization's premise. Fantasy of manners loses because etiquette/class performance does not govern. Fantasy mystery is a rival because secrets are uncovered, but Selene's goal is winning and power, not undertaking a detective case. She holds no investigator office, no client and no ghost-assisted murder inquiry. Dark realms wins because the threatened ruin, haunting and forbidden bargain govern more than case-solving.

At Dark realms, Hungry realms loses because no feeding/appetite mechanism is established; the King's coercive use is not automatically literal consumption. Godforsaken realms loses because the mentor is a bound humanlike ghost, not an established demon or god whose presence shapes the world. Faustian is the bargain's register, not proof that the counterparty is the Devil. Haunted realms wins on the actual dead, a century's mirror-bound imprisonment, forbidden opera basement and buried tragedy that encloses Selene. The ghost is alluring as well as frightening; friendly/romantic attraction does not undo the dead-and-haunting mechanism or the threat around him.

Wobble: the competition is a strong and explicit plot spine, and the heroine knowingly seeks out the ghost rather than fleeing him. If the book were primarily a series of action challenges, Tournament fantasy would win. Multiple completed reviews and the creator's own discussions instead establish Gothic enclosure and a corrupting bargain; that is why Haunted realms is preferred. Research includes creator interviews, primary excerpt and publisher copy, professional and independent completed reviews, not a full novel read. No claim about the unpublished sequel's complete plot.
