# Dragonships of Vindras whole-fiction walk
23 November 2026

Four published novels by Margaret Weis and Tracy Hickman: Bones of the Dragon (2009), Secret of the Dragon (2010), Rage of the Dragon (2012), Doom of the Dragon (2016). Existing Invented worship accepts the tenth fiction. Intake freezes for immediate content-first split audit. No structural change is authorized by this placement.

## Scope and contradictions
Tor's complete bundle explicitly lists the four. Its editor's 2009 newsletter and early reviews projected six, superseded by the publisher and final-volume review identifying book four as the conclusion. Fantastic Fiction's 2008 date for Bones conflicts with Tor's January2009 newsletter, the parenting review and catalog edition date6January2009; use2009 actual publication, not2008 copyright. Omnibus excluded. The primary editor explicitly calls this a new fantasy world, so shared authorship and dragons do not connect it to Dragonlance, Death Gate or Dragon Brigade. The bundle's Doom paragraph mentions Dragon Brigade and conflicts with its actual standalone synopsis; that paragraph is not used for story details.

## Root-down walk
Speculative fiction > Fantasy: gods appear bodily, divine possession occurs, priestesses summon real dragon spirits, druids transform sailors into rabbits. Not scientific explanation.

Fantasy > Fantasy realms: independently invented Vindras/Sinarian world. No Earth inclusion or crossing demonstrated, so neither Real earth nor Portal ancestor passes. Norse parallels are substantial, including the Norn and World Tree in the final excerpt, but this is not a retelling of an inherited Norse narrative and does not become historical Earth by analogy.

Fantasy realms > Epic fantasy: world-spanning relic journey, nations and armies, divine conflict and creation-level stakes govern the whole. Not intimate episodic S&S; not fairytale revision, literary Heritage school, comedy, cultural Far shores classification or bond-first Heart fantasy.

Epic fantasy > Mythological fantasy: serious quest/high-fantasy rival, plus Skylan's martial leadership and large battles. The quest, military and leadership plots consistently serve the struggle between divine orders and their worshipping peoples. Political succession and a magic system are not independent governing subjects. Select mythological register over High/Heroic/Military/Political/Hard fantasy.

Mythological fantasy > Invented mythology > Invented gods: Torval, Vindrash, Aelon and other newly invented deities actively fight, appear to mortals, possess a dead priestess, send visions or creatures, with gods and their earthly adherents mutually dependent. Not Mythlike past: divine action is current throughout. Not Mythic power struggle: borrowed Norse furniture does not make these invented gods inherited characters.

Invented gods > Invented worship: human service, failure, loyalty and resistance to rival gods organize all four. Bones makes Skylan's task saving his dying gods; Secret pursues creation-bones for the old gods against Aelon's priest; Rage explicitly centers prayer, visions, rival divine alliances and service; Doom follows Skylan meeting his wounded patron and continuing the same quest with priestess Aylaen. Its official chapter1 includes divine vulnerability and Norn mortality for gods, a real Invented godhood wobble. But sustained experience of being or learning to be a deity is not demonstrated. The gods remain those humans meet, serve, defy or must save, including Skylan's disputed admission to his patron's hall. Occasional divine scenes and their limits are not enough under the existing priority definition. Positive Invented worship, not a capacity-based choice.
