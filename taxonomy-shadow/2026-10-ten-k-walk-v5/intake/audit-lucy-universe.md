# The Commission (Lucy Universe): whole-fiction repair
24 November 2026

## Result
Consolidate the existing Lucy's Blade (2007) and Wolf in Shadow (2013) cards as one fiction, The Commission (Lucy Universe), in existing Spook fantasy. Northern Renaissance fantasy falls 9 to 8; Spook fantasy stays at 6. No novel is added or removed: 937 nodes, 3013 fictions, 7563 books. No definition or structure changes.

## Corpus and authority
The original normal-intake commission and individual-fiction/shared-universe grant were recovered directly from the authenticated conversation before this repair: phonemsg-01M43MYMGAMQTNA3CY402KK7FB and phonemsg-01M66AQB0JEGYRYN8Q7XTWHVDX. The latter entrusts individual fiction judgment and says a shared universe is one fiction.

ISFDB's Lucy Universe bibliography lists exactly these two novels, plus short fiction and London Days, Demon Nights (2019), a collection. LibraryThing independently calls the shared series The Commission and numbers Lucy's Blade 1, Wolf in Shadow 2. This is not a same-author inference. The author's 2009 and 2011 essays explicitly identify Lucy's modern-frame Jameson and Karla as Commission enforcers, connect the service to Walsingham, and trace the shared modern stories. The authorized Wolf chapter6 features the same enforcers and institution. The previous two standalone claims were wrong.

Eligible books: Lucy's Blade, first printing May2007; Wolf in Shadow, 2013 (ISFDB's ebook June15 precedes its July print release; the year is unchanged). As Black As Hell, Night of the Wolf, Siren Tears, Beauty Is a Witch and Haunts of Guilty Minds are short works, excluded. The 2019 collection is excluded, not a third novel. The author's 2017 reply says no Wolf sequel will appear; current bibliographies show no later novel. Neither Citizen nor Venus, Mars and Hell is attached without shared-world evidence.

## Whole narrative and the root audit
Lucy's Blade has an Elizabethan main narrative and a modern London frame. Walsingham has Dee summon a supposed demon to investigate threats against Elizabeth. Lilith is actually a created information/energy being from a far-future dark universe, launched by dimensional engineers through the Shadow Worlds. Authorized chapter2 explicitly calls the transit backwards time travel, shows a black-hole launch apparatus and an internet stop, then Isabella's sea-diamond mirror and Dee's containment interrupt it. Chapter5 describes Lucy's enhanced biology, Lilith's refusal to kill her host by departing, and their developing negotiation over bodily control. Finished criticism confirms the late plot moves away from the expected palace assassination climax into an Atlantic voyage. The publisher TOC names Bimini and Endings And Beginnings. The author confirms the book starts and ends in modern London and introduces Jameson/Karla there. Do not invent the exact final duel or claim all apparent magic has been demystified.

Wolf in Shadow follows Rhian, fleeing a violent tragedy in Wales, into contemporary East London, her wolf-pendant transformations, consulting witch Frankie, vampire/elf-like beings and the Commission's Jameson and Karla. The freelance and state-service threads converge on the same danger. Completed criticism describes the institutional obstacle as a suspect protected by MI6, and the larger stakes as stopping an Otherworld portal releasing destruction into London. Its city layers are memory-like magical Otherworld spaces, not simply an SF future expedition. No exact final killing, romance outcome or portal-closing procedure is invented here.

The original Lucy SF lead was genuine and cannot be suppressed: Lilith is not a traditional supernatural demon. However the whole fiction is not justified as SF merely by her future origin. Authorized Wolf chapter4 shows practical geomantic/elemental spellwork. Chapter6 explicitly describes Frankie's reciprocal love-geas experiment binding Karla and Jameson, later failed repetitions, and sunlight's spiritual rather than merely photochemical harm to daemons. Finished reviews likewise treat working witchcraft, enchanted objects and Otherworld as real. This supplies positive magic evidence, not only unexplained anomalies. The owner's no-magic-in-SF rule therefore keeps the shared fiction Fantasy-side; the any-SF-time-travel rule is downstream of that root decision and does not override it. The scientific flavour in Lucy remains an explicit mixed-ontology caveat, not erased.

## Root-down walk and rivals
Speculative fiction > Fantasy > Real earth fantasy > Contemporary fantasy > Urban fantasy > Occult secret service > Magic counter ops > Spook fantasy.

Fantasy beats Science fiction because the shared corpus contains actual operative witchcraft and a spiritual supernatural order. Real earth beats an independently invented realm: England, Elizabeth's court, modern London and its institutions are explicit. Portal fantasy is a serious rival because Wolf uses Otherworld crossings and a portal threat, while Lucy crosses times/dimensions. The shared through-line is the British occult service and threats intruding into its Earth jurisdiction, not a continuing journey through a system of worlds. Earth-rooted excursions are allowed by Real earth's existing gate.

Historical fantasy > Northern Renaissance fits much of Lucy alone, but cannot describe Wolf or Lucy's modern institutional frame. Contemporary has the broader recurring Commission setting and the entire later novel. This is a whole-fiction choice with a genuine substantial historical-volume strain, not a claim every scene is contemporary. The owner's whole-fiction rule allows an overarching hook or, if none covers every book strictly, the defining setting/concept of a subset. The occult state-service lineage supplies that hook across both: Walsingham's earlier defence and the modern Commission.

Urban beats Contemporary mythic (not a retold national myth), magical coming of age (Rhian's awakening is not the whole corpus), Domestic (no household/family spine), Dark (horror menace not the governing contract), Supernatural creature shelves (not primarily vampire or werewolf society), magical realism (marvels are investigated/controlled, not ordinary) and Folktale (no governing moral-tale form).

Occult secret service beats Paranormal bricks/Shadow city: London's layered history is a strong Wolf setting and mechanism, but not a better shared two-novel identity than the service. It also beats Fae founders (not Faerie peoples as the whole subject), Publicly paranormal (secrecy retained), Modern goth/Occult institutions (not chiefly a campus, profession or haunted institution), Occult cop (not police caseload), and Occult PI (Frankie's paid jobs are one thread; Walsingham/Commission defence is the cross-book hook). Freakbusters fails: Lilith is futuristic, but the recurring enemy is occult intrusion and rogue magic, not weaponized scientific inventions.

Magic counter ops beats Eldritch counter ops: entities are not established Lovecraft-scale cosmic outsiders, and a multiverse alone does not make that register. Demon counter ops is a real naming resemblance, but daemons, Lilith and the wolf are not a shared Hell/damnation/apocalypse hierarchy. Rogue witchcraft, mythic creatures and magical incursions govern. Within Magic counter ops, the British state-service origin and Commission place it in Spook fantasy, not a knightly order, a society of sorcerers policing itself, or private commercial monster hunters. Frankie freelances but the whole-fiction card is not solely her business.

Confidence: high on shared corpus and consolidation; moderate on the exact shelf because one major novel is purposefully Elizabethan and the series mixes SF explanation with literal magic. No full-novel read claimed. Authorized openings, creator accounts, multiple completed reviews and bibliographies support the decision. A future explicit author revision making all spellwork technological would reopen the root, not justify hiding today's evidence.
