## Evidence
SFRevu's2002 author interview explicitly identifies kingship as the theme of books2/3 completing the trilogy. It confirms the world is invented and describes worship-returned power under12divinities, a real worship rival. Its completed final-volume review distinguishes the actual prize: Benet's lost Kinghood ritual, a god-instilled bond between ruler, people and land that makes Tielmark independent. Gaultry must recover/enact it while High Priestess Dervla and a divided, magically created royal coven manipulate events. The first volume saves sister and nation from a treasonous chancellor and imperial invasion; the second foils assassins poisoning the prince with dark magic and investigates the imperial ties; the third completes sovereignty and coronation. PW independently supports both court and foreign-sovereignty strands, not merely a blurb's religious premise.

## Full walk
Speculative fiction > Fantasy: witchcraft, sacred kingship and active gods. No science explanation.
Fantasy > Fantasy realms: Reimann explicitly contrasts these three made-up-world books with her later Earth historical/contemporary projects. No Earth portal, no inherited myth narrative. Not Real earth or Portal.
Realms > Epic fantasy: national sovereignty, empire, magical covenants and history dominate; not intimate S&S, fairytale transformation, inherited cultural retelling or bond-first romance.
Epic > Political fantasy: who can legitimately rule Tielmark and end Bissanty's claim organizes the whole. Mythological fantasy is substantial, with on-page gods and worship powering magic, but divine existence/faith/service is the mechanism and condition of political kingship rather than the governing prize. Not merely any novel containing gods. Heroic mage growth/quest are strong secondary forms, not the whole arc's center.
Political > Court intrigue: insider sovereignty, rival lineages, Benet's kingship and coven sabotage. Geopolitical is serious, but cross-border independence is achieved through the ruling prince's sacred legitimacy, not primarily diplomacy among states. Revolutionary/rebel also serious, but this is royal ritual, courtiers, priestess, assassins and dynastic ties rather than organized mass open resistance, underground insurgency or caste emancipation. Magic politics fails: no public regulation of who may use magic as governing prize. Election absent.
Court intrigue > Dynastic intrigues: contested legitimacy and royal continuity, rather than professional espionage or day-to-day ruler governance.
Dynastic intrigues > Dynastic magic: divine ritual, royal-land bond, magical poisoning and a coven made to protect/act for the dynasty are decisive instruments. Not chiefly armies, mundane assassination, murder investigation, spy intelligence or patronage. Existing Queens of Elben likewise binds royal marriage magically to the land's survival; Branion has sacred royal power contested by competing dynasties. No gate widened to take this fiction.
