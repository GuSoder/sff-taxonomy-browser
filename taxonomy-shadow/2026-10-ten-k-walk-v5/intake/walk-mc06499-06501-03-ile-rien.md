# Ile-Rien: whole-fiction placement
24 November 2026. MC06499 and MC06501-03.

## Decision
Place one five-novel Ile-Rien fiction in existing Technomancy, six to seven fictions. Route: Speculative fiction > Fantasy > Fantasy realms > Gothic realms > Technomancy. No new genre, definition change or extra omnibus book. Confidence moderate: the books change their immediate mode from court intrigue to occult crime to interworld resistance adventure. The repeated scientific-sorcery apparatus, and its increasingly explicit disembodied-soul machinery, is the most useful existing whole-fiction home. This is not a claim that every scene, every machine or the first novel alone is necromantic engineering.

## Corpus
The creator's Ile-Rien page explicitly groups The Element of Fire (1993) and The Death of the Necromancer (1998), described as standalone novels set in Ile-Rien, with The Fall of Ile-Rien trilogy: The Wizard Hunters (2003), The Ships of Air (2004), The Gate of Gods (2005). Nicholas connects the second novel to his daughter Tremaine's trilogy; the world's earlier court history connects the first. SFE independently lists the same overall sequence. One shared universe is one fiction under the standing intake instruction.

The Book of Ile-Rien (2024) is the revised omnibus of the first two novels, confirmed by current Tor and SFE. Both texts are revised preferred versions, not two new novels or a sixth book. Original years retained. Between Worlds (2015) is a short-story collection, not a sixth novel. No connection to City of Bones, Emilie, Raksura, Rising World or Murderbot is inferred from common authorship. The Reactor reviewer's explicitly wishful multiverse theory is not canon evidence. No existing Ile-Rien or constituent-title card was found in the current tree.

## Actual content across all five novels
1. The Element of Fire, 1993. Thomas Boniface, captain of the Queen's Guard, protects Ravenna's court and weak King Roland amid Denzil's coup and Urbain Grandier's scientific sorcery. Kade, the king's half-fae sister and new Queen of Air and Darkness, brings another magical allegiance. The early rescue of Dr Dubell turns into a struggle over palace wards and an invading Unseelie Host. Kade and Thomas must work together to save the court and stop Grandier. The court is not a generic map decoration; political action genuinely governs much of this installment. This novel is the least exact fit for the eventual apparatus-centered shelf. No assertion of world-scale mechanical industry is made for it.
2. The Death of the Necromancer, 1998. Nicholas Valiarde, art importer and master thief Donatien, plots revenge on Montesq for framing his foster father Edouard Viller for necromancy. A burglary encounters Dr Octave's rival operation; Octave uses a Viller sphere in communion with the dead and is backed by the apparently long-dead Constant Macob. Arisilde falls into a deathlike coma. Nicholas, Madeline, Reynard and their associates trace necromantic machinery, revenants and animated dead through the city, eventually cooperating with Inspector Ronsarde against the threat. Viller's original project made sorcery accessible through a manufactured device. Scientific magic and actual necromantic misuse are tied together, not merely a word in the title. Completed reviews confirm the plot resolves; the exact final destruction step is not claimed here.
3. The Wizard Hunters, 2003. Ile-Rien, now technologically comparable to the early twentieth century, is losing to Gardier airship bombing and a spell that disables ordinary machinery. Tremaine finds Arisilde's original sphere after he and Nicholas vanished. Testing sends a group into the Syprian world, where Giliead and Ilias resist a Gardier base in a cave system. Back-and-forth raids, escapes and an alliance reveal the sphere's apparent life and the horrific treatment of captured sorcerers. The base suffers a setback, but the war continues. The machinery is not ordinary aircraft given a magical-looking jacket.
4. The Ships of Air, 2004. The passenger liner Queen Ravenna carries refugees and the resistance through the Syprian world. Magical sabotage and political conflict aboard lead to a hunt for a hidden wizard. Tremaine's marriage to Ilias helps secure alliance. An attempt to pass the Walls of the World leads some of the group onto a Gardier airship and into a third world. Nicholas reappears as an infiltrator. The group destroys another Gardier outpost and returns to the liner, earning a respite, not ending the war. The vessel's steam machinery is genuine; nevertheless, the decisive world-gates, sphere and enslaved-soul avatars operate by sorcery, not steam.
5. The Gate of Gods, 2005. In Capidara, allies disastrously seek to use Ixion. Florian and Nicholas confront his sabotage while Tremaine, Ilias, Giliead, Gerard and others follow the world-gate network and the lost Arisilde trail. They encounter Aelin survivors from the Gardier world and uncover the source of the system that transformed it into a conquering society. Gardier avatars contain forcibly displaced sorcerer-souls; these crystals power sorcery, ships and control. Arisilde's soul inhabits the original Rienish sphere. New copied Rienish spheres need not contain souls, an important distinction. Finished reviewers confirm the confrontation ends the invasion and resolves the character arcs. The exact identity/ontology of Orelis and the terminal destruction sequence remain insufficiently verified for detailed retelling. Neither is needed to turn the already positive soul-apparatus mechanism into mere speculation.

## Root-down walk
### Root, world and time
Literal sorcery, fae, necromantic dead, magical gods and soul displacement establish Fantasy. Scientifically studied magic in this invented world is not a license for the SF side. SFE describes the sequence as fantasy; the creator explicitly says the first world's historical model is seventeenth-century France but the world is entirely imaginary. The invented realm advances through several centuries. It is not our France, Victorian London or WWII Britain. Real-earth Historical/Period/Gaslight and future-Earth shelves fail their ancestor despite recognizable analogies. The preserved core world plus Syprian and Gardier worlds stays Fantasy realms. Portal fantasy requires Earth in its system; these interworld gates do not establish Earth. No actual time travel is established. Two centuries between novels and learning the enemy's origins are not characters traveling through time. No Fabulation device, story becoming real, simulation or narrative-revision premise appears.

### Fantasy realms alternatives
Comic is not the engine despite plentiful sharp dialogue. Heart fantasy fails the whole-fiction bond test: Tremaine and Ilias's romance and the refuge community matter, but do not govern the entire court/crime/war arc. Fairytale fails: fae courts in the first book are not a recognizable retelling or fairy-tale task grammar. Far shores fails both the main Western-counterpart setting and culture-aboutness test; Syprian matriarchy is a substantial encounter, not the work's governing cultural portrait.

Epic is a real rival through political stakes, siege, several worlds and war. Military Army/World-at-war overweights the trilogy's setting and the state's survival. Most viewpoint action follows an improvised civilian-led group investigating devices, escaping bases, exposing spies and opening gates, not a sustained military unit, commanders' campaigns or whole-front tapestry. The first two books are not military at all. Political Court intrigue fits Fire, criminal intrigue and authority recur, but dynastic legitimacy, sovereign statecraft, a court spy office, mass franchise, revolution or interstate balance does not organize all five. Magic politics is not a generic home for magical people struggling for power; control of publicly sanctioned magic is not the persistent contest. Heroic class routes vary from Thomas's martial office to Kade's sorcery, Nicholas's professional thievery and Tremaine's civilian judgment. Selecting one protagonist/class erases much of the corpus. High fantasy has consequential interworld travel in the trilogy but no sustained inherited Tolkien fellowship/marked one's rise or revisionist interrogation of that template across all five. Hard fantasy has alchemical science, yet its explicit learnable laws and costs are not the reading contract. The final Reactor review specifically observes that the author avoids a rigid magic system. Time magic lacks positive evidence. Mythological has genuine Syprian gods but not myth itself as the whole fiction's work.

### Gothic realms and its alternatives
The Gothic menace test is positive, not inferred from the word necromancer. Grandier's hostile magic and fae assault, Macob and the revenants, Arisilde's disembodied existence, forcibly enslaved sorcerer-souls and the system behind the Gardier are recurring uncanny threats. Technomancy does not inherit Dark realms' separate requirement that dread outweigh adventure. Dark realms' Haunted/Hungry/Godforsaken choices would mistake one manifestation for the larger apparatus-and-sorcery system; this is not principally enclosure or appetite. Isolation is absent, travel and cross-cultural contact drive the later books. Fantasy of manners is a recognized influence on Fire (SFE), but current tree narrows it to British-imperial social-code narratives. Neither its marriage, class-performance nor reputation leaves govern this whole sequence. A cited influence is not automatic membership under a narrower live gate.

Fantasy mystery is substantial in Necromancer and the sabotage plots, but Nicholas is the thief, not a hired private eye or official investigator; Ronsarde does not become the whole fiction's protagonist. Thomas has an official court role but not a persistent uncanny criminal-case series. Tremaine's search for enemy origins is resistance adventure, not an institutional case. No gumshoe/official/ghost-sleuth leaf is widened to absorb the universe.

### Technomancy over Technofantasy
Technomancy explicitly accepts Gothic realms where actual magic is organized through technological forms: necromancy as engineering, haunted apparatus, occult systems and ominous science-fantasy. Viller/Arisilde's spheres recur from the second novel into the trilogy; a device contacts the dead, a wizard's soul occupies a manufactured sphere, and the enemy's soul-prison crystals make magical power into a military and coercive system. This is a positive instance of the necromantic-apparatus distinction, not just grim action or spooky atmosphere.

Sword and sorcery is a genuine broad adventure fit. Caper is strong for Nicholas, weak for the court-rescue and Tremaine trilogy as a whole. Technofantasy is also a genuine broad mechanism fit but its definition expressly sends necromancy run as engineering to Technomancy. Magitech repeats that exclusion. This is where it applies. The copied Rienish spheres not requiring souls and ordinary steamliner engines are recorded, not hidden. The decisive original sphere and Gardier avatars still place disembodied or stolen souls in central engineered forms. Steampunk realms has real steam transport in Ships but steam does not define the whole sequence's technological premise, and a steamliner does not nullify the positive soul apparatus. Dieselpunk's combustion mechanism is likewise not the decisive source of the interworld technology. The Gothic route wins on recurring apparatus and menace, not on a count of three trilogy books beating two standalones.

## Limits and confidence
The first novel's court/fay story is the strongest mismatch; it is not rewritten as a necromancy-engineering story to hide the variation. The whole shared universe must remain one fiction. The second novel develops the actual apparatus later central to the trilogy, making Technomancy a better existing whole-world home than a leaf dedicated to one protagonist or period. No definition is widened. Exact endgame details are bounded above, no full books were read or unauthorized mirrors used. Completed reviews across all five, original contemporary trade reviews, creator pages and current publisher/encyclopedia corpus records supply the classification evidence.
