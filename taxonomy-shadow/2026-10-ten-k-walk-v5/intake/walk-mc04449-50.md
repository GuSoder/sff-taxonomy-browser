## Whole published fiction
Fossil hunter Mary Anning discovers and reanimates a baby pterodactyl, Ajax, and initially hopes this will gain entry to the male-only Geomagical Society. Her mentor Buckland and former fiance Henry compete for that society's presidency. Her loyalty to Ajax, ambition, relationship with Henry and religious risks put her at odds with the scholarly establishment. Lucy's Prometheans push against a system in which poor people sell their magic for wealthy buyers' use. These are not independent decorative subplot labels: Mary's choice between joining an exploitative institution and opposing its basis makes the economy and institutions of magic the historical engine. ## Root-down hard walk
Speculative fiction > Fantasy: actual innate magic, stored energy for spells and fossil reanimation; paleontology vocabulary does not establish a known-physics scientific engine. Not scientifically reconstructed dinosaur/biotech story. Fantasy > Real earth fantasy: Mary Anning, Lyme Regis, London and England are deliberate historical referents. No independently invented fantasy universe, no portal crossing. Historical fantasy > Alternate history fantasy: the author explicitly says alternate magical England1829 and details everyone's tradable magic changing wealth/labor/religion. Not secret history: Mary's specific resurrection power is hidden/heretical, but ordinary magic is publicly sanctioned and commodity traded; secrecy about one discovery does not make all history secretly magic-explained. Not mythic retelling: a historical paleontologist, no inherited myth being retold. Period fantasy/Magicians in gaslight is the genuine near miss: council, exclusion and gift-hoarding fit its lower definition, but its ancestor says the documented era is stage, not openly rewritten. This world revises political economy and church/society around public magic, so the alternate-history gate wins above the guild-level likeness. Gaslight technomancy is another honest lower resemblance because magic is commodified; it too loses at Period parent, not because fossils/science are absent.

Alternate history fantasy > Alt flintlock fantasy: real-world early nineteenth-century Britain1829, the musket/Regency-adjacent era with public magic, closest to existing Sorcerer Royal and Alvin Maker. 'Roughly' seventeenth century to Napoleonic/Regency/early-American frontier is an era band, not an exact1820cutoff. Source marketing calls this Victorian, but author explicitly fixes1829; do not repeat marketing as a verified Victoria-era date. Alt diesel starts1910s, too late; Alt Renaissance/crusade/Ming/spice-route each fail era/geography. No need to seed a nineteenth-century sibling from an approximate existing band. Wobble: moderate because1829is after formal Regency and magic remains a society-based practice, but the alternate-world/open-magic evidence is unusually explicit. Do not rename or widen existing definitions.
