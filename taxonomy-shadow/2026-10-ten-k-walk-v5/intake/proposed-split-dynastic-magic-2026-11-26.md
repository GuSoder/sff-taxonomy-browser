# Dynastic magic: proposed split, 26 November 2026

Status: PROPOSAL ONLY. Ten fictions, 38 published novels. All intake frozen after Dragon Prince and Dragon Star. No new nodes, names, definitions or portraits executed. Keep original Dynastic magic as umbrella with zero direct fictions if approved.

## Content before labels

- The Drowning Empire, 3: Lin and rival constructed people seize and defend an emperor's throne by creating and rewriting bone-shard constructs, alongside Alanga powers. Rival revolutionary movement substantial. Engine: royal manufacture and command of magic, not an obligation to a supernatural sovereign.
- Blood of the Four, 1: Phela moves from spying and usurpation to mastering forbidden magic and making herself divine. Gods underpin the existing order but her own acquisition and use of their power is the bid for supremacy. Engine: becoming the sorcerous ruler, not fulfilling a pact. FantasyLiterature is a DNF/skimming source and does not prove ending; creator/Kirkus supply this interpretation.
- Crown of Shards universe, 6: ruling families use stolen, inherited, suppressed and weaponized magical powers in royal massacres, tournaments and succession contests across Evie and Gemma's linked trilogies. Engine: sovereign spellpower and counterspell, not supernatural kingship obligations.
- Chronicles of the Cheysuli, 8: deliberate royal marriages combine shapechanging and sorcerous bloodlines into the final heir who unites four kingdoms. Gods and prophecy are real, but the continuing concrete instrument is inherited magical blood and its manipulation through generations. Engine: breeding the magic into the dynasty.
- Dragon Prince and Dragon Star, 6: planned Sunrunner-prince marriage, concealed sorcerous heritage, magical succession duels and final Pol/Andry alliance. Engine: ruling families acquire and wield magical capacity through marriage, descent, training and coalition.
- Branion, 4: the rightful ruler is the vessel of the Living Flame; a dangerous divine presence inhabits successive royal people, with rival religious and elemental allegiances attacking legitimacy. Engine: the throne bears a supernatural being, not merely a fire spell talent.
- Queens of Elben, 2: the king binds his six wives to six palaces, steals their power and sustains his rule through a false sacred-marital order. Gods, rotting land and breaking the bindings expose the dynasty's claim. Engine: magical royal bonds as a condition and instrument of sovereignty.
- Boreal Moon, 3: Beaconfolk supply powerful sigils at the price of suffering; pacts, prohibited royal Talent and disinheritance determine which heir may rule. Engine: bargains and obligations to greater powers govern succession, despite substantial innate-Talent and spy crossover.
- Obsidian Throne, 2: a witch's revenge curse leaves the dead emperor in her consciousness; his successor coerces a second curse, while nature spirits and hidden royal ancestry complicate succession. Engine: curses and revenant/witch entanglement weaponize the ruling family, rather than a continuous dynasty of self-directed royal spellcasters. Third book is unpublished and excluded.
- The Tielmaran Chronicles, 3: lost Kinghood ritual, a god-instilled royal-land bond and a coven created to act for the prince make sovereignty magically conditional. Engine: recovering and fulfilling the binding that makes a real king.

## Discovered split

The concrete distinction is a dynasty wielding sorcery as its power versus a throne entangled in supernatural beings, bindings and conditions. These overlap in local furniture. The governing instrument takes priority, not a claim that the first group never has gods or the second never casts spells. Neither is a leftover bucket.

### Sorcerer dynasties: 5 fictions / 24 novels

Dynastic contests whose decisive instrument is the ruling families' magical capacity: inherited, cultivated, stolen or made, and directly wielded against rival heirs and houses.

The Drowning Empire; Blood of the Four; Crown of Shards universe; Chronicles of the Cheysuli; Dragon Prince and Dragon Star.

### Spiritbound thrones: 5 fictions / 14 novels

Dynastic contests whose decisive instrument is the ruling line's binding to supernatural powers: an indwelling presence, pact, curse or royal-land covenant that empowers, compels or disqualifies its rulers.

Branion; Queens of Elben; Boreal Moon; Obsidian Throne; The Tielmaran Chronicles.

## Honest boundaries

- Branion has hereditary magical blood as well as a living god inside the ruler. Give actual divine vessel priority over ordinary inherited magical skill.
- Cheysuli has gods' prophecy and king-making rites. Its eight-book mechanism is marrying and transmitting the rival magical bloodlines, not the rite alone. This is the strongest crossover opposite Branion.
- Boreal Moon has hidden inherited Talent, but its Beaconfolk bargains/sigils and disqualifying pact are repeated concrete succession levers. Put it with bound thrones, not merely because the king has a secret.
- Blood of the Four is built on sorcerer-gods, but the claimant seeks to acquire their magic and become one, not serve a binding sovereign entity. That places her with sorcerer dynasties.
- Obsidian Throne has a late magical ancestry reveal, supported at reader level in the existing audit; do not let that alone recast two books whose repeated instrument is a curse, revenant consciousness and a captive witch's compelled speech.
- Spiritbound is the weakest label: it can suggest only ghosts or possession. The proposed definition includes gods, spirits, curses and metaphysical bonds. All five have such supernatural agency or binding, not an ordinary political contract. Ask the curator's naming judgment rather than pretend the name is perfect.

## Other content-derived partitions tested

- Inherited versus acquired magic: poor clean boundary because Branion is both divine vessel and heredity, Elben draws on wives' inherited power, Drowning Empire mixes learned construct craft and Alanga nature, Blood of the Four claims divine descent while seeking new sorcery.
- Benevolent versus tyrannical rule: unstable over whole fictions, Ashoka's reversal and sympathetic competing regimes defeat it.
- Restoration versus usurpation: almost every series contains both; not a partition.
- Constructs / curses / divine vessels / royal-land bonds / magical bloodlines: literal but fragments ten into tiny leaves while several contain more than one mechanism.
- Generational versus one reign: approximately 3/7 and an era/length axis, not what the magic does. Branion reverse chronology and changing protagonists complicate it further.
- Sacred kingship versus sorcerer rulers: established first term, but too narrow for Obsidian's curse and Boreal's predatory spirit pacts; forcing them there would be a leftover bucket.

## Names checked

Read full taxonomy/SPLITTING.md and NAMES.md, including content-first, standalone hint, no A+B names, original umbrella, name-cold and exact-trademark-only rules. Generic monarchy/trope terms do not name the complete partition cleanly. Sacral kingship is an established concept, supported by https://dc.swosu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1224&context=mythlore and https://www.ricorso.net/rx/library/celtica/M-Cana_P/Celtic_Myth/5_S-King.htm, but too narrow here. Magocracy/thaumocracy describes who rules, not these two different succession mechanisms.

Sorcerer dynasties is an attested descriptive reader/worldbuilding phrase, not a claimed formal genre: https://www.reddit.com/r/d100/comments/emq4qk/lets_build_100_sorcerer_dynastiesfamilies/ and https://arthoslibrary.com/wiki/Sorcerer . Exact searches bring a Sorcerous Dynasty of Sarion fictional polity and general RPG worldbuilding, not an exact complete-name franchise identified by this check. Sorcerer dynasties carries supernatural ruling-family power cold.

Spiritbound thrones is a descriptive compound, not an established genre. Exact full-phrase and book/game/series scans found unrelated Spiritbound properties and generic throne material, no exact full-name match in checked results. This is not trademark registry clearance. The complete phrase carries magic binding and royal authority; dynasty hinted through succession/throne rather than repeated parent word. It is not a new portmanteau requiring invented-root wordplay.

Rejected working names: Mageblood dynasties (existing Mageblood node and RPG item confusion); Spellbound dynasties (near-identical existing Spellbound Dynasty novel, plus less precise binding); Godbound dynasties (too narrow for curses); Sacred kingship (as above); Dynastic sorcery / Dynastic bindings (bindings alone can mean ordinary royal contracts); Enchanted thrones (literal furniture/events and does not specify binding); Sorcerer succession (video-game class specialization ambiguity); Bloodright sorcery (blood magic misread); Crown covenants (drops magic); Spiritbound dynasties (longer without improving the ambiguous spirit hint). No need to invent a stitched-together word when clear ordinary words exist.
