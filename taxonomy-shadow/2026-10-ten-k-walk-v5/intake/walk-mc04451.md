## Every published novel
Elysium: Adrian/Adrianne and Antoine/Antoinette change gender and relation through repeated city/apocalypse scenarios, interrupted by computer faults. The second half reveals alien mutagen dust, engineer Adrian constructing underground refuges, his bereavement and atmospheric encoding memorial. His scanned self supplies its interface; an external observer accesses reconstructed memory. This is a formally unstable computer-mediated retelling, not literal movement among alternate Earths or incarnated souls. Strange Horizons full review traces the complete reveal and ending; SFE calls the versions thought-experiment environments rather than parallel worlds. https://strangehorizons.com/wordpress/non-fiction/reviews/elysium-by-jennifer-marie-brissett/ ; https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/brissett_jennifer_marie ; https://locusmag.com/review/paul-di-filippo-reviews-jennifer-marie-brissett/ . Author explains the program remakes its world again and again to tell loss/mourning, using Hadrian/Antinous as theme: https://www.jennbrissett.com/content/docs/guest-post-jennifer-marie-brissett-on-the-inspiration-for-her-novel-elysium.pdf . This is not a claim every chapter of this novel contains actual time travel.

Destroyer of Light: human refugees arrive on tidally locked Eleusis after centuries in cryosleep; genetic modifications give Deidra agricultural power, Cora phase-shifting abilities and twins shared psychic perception. Cora is abducted into Okoni's child army and forced marriage while Deidra searches; twins investigate another missing human/alien child. The planet's Lattice AI Cate joins Cora and ultimately their fusion preserves Eleusis. Human/alien coexistence, abuse, class and agency are major subjects. ## Root-down hard walk
Speculative fiction > Science fiction: computers, engineered genetics, alien invasion, multidimensional species and displaced human colonies ground both novels in Earth's universe. Myth references and supernatural-looking gifts are reworked through these mechanisms, not autonomous magic. Fantasy/Mythic retelling cannot override this root. No independent invented fantasy universe.
Science fiction > Atemporal SF: sequel's real concurrent temporal perception passes the user's any-time-travel element gate, and first's unstable computed era-versions also resist straightforward chronology. Historical-SF/modern-day fail because first's contemporary/alternate-Roman scenes are reconstructed scenarios, not whole-world period setting. Futurist alien life, invasion aftermath, lost colony and planetary politics capture true content but lose to temporal sorting. Colony is not lost to Earth through technological forgetting; sequel retains Lattice, ships and deliberate genetic engineering. No invented new colony child.
Atemporal > Time travel fiction > Mind over time. Fabulation is the strongest rival, not denied: first is openly computed narration of unstable identity and history. A first-volume-only walk could choose Projected worlds, Scripted books in books or Unmoored selves. Whole shared fiction cannot be separated to preserve such a choice. The sequel's AI/Cora consciousness experiences time beyond flashback narration, and explicit temporal-perception sorting wins. Even below Fabulation, Projected worlds explains first's atmospheric reconstruction better than sequel's actual society; Scripted books would falsely imply Eleusis itself is confirmed fictional text/simulation; Unmoored selves captures both fluid identities but loses to the more explicit temporal gate. New Weird does not override the known computational/genetic explanations, Parallel world fiction would mistake simulations for literal alternate universes, Reincarnation would mistake avatars for souls. Moderate wobble: primary language calls much of the past memory; do not describe physical temporal intervention or changing history. Future perception plus original widened rule is why this placement is defensible rather than silently treating a nonlinear chapter order as time travel.
