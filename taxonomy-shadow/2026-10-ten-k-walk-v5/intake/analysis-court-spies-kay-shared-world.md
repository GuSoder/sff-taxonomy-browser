- Seed: Geopolitical fantasy

Positive scope: political fantasy organized around relations and contests among states, empires or rival polities. The balance of power across their borders, and its effects on people within them, is the shared engine. This is not a category for any book with a map, a war, a historical analogue or an empire in the background.

Boundaries: Court intrigue retains internal ruling-court contests and ruler-level governance. Revolutionary retains a governing liberation or overthrow movement. Election retains mass franchise. Magic politics retains public authority over supernatural power. Military requires the army, campaign or soldier's war as the organizing unit. A particular fiction still takes the more exact governing engine when one exists. Do not sweep other political books into this seed just because they have foreign relations.

Geopolitical fantasy is an attested literary phrase, including Seth Dickinson's own description of his work. This does not establish a universally formalized subgenre. It is preferable here to an invented near-history label that would organize a new Epic sibling by surface historical resemblance instead of the governing engine. No direct Fantasy realms child is proposed.

## Complete-fiction boundary

Author confirmation, not common moons alone, joins these books. Kay explicitly names Lions, Sarantine, Last Light and Children as the same world in his 2016 Locus interview. His 2016 AMA calls it the same near-Europe. PRH explicitly returns All the Seas to Brightness and Children. Publishers Weekly and Locus confirm Brightness shares Children's world. Written on the Dark's continuity is documented in its reference entry and corroborated by its Jad faith, two moons and Sarantium history in independent criticism.

Do not fold every Kay novel together. His 1995 interview expressly characterizes glancing Fionavar references and the earlier Tigana/Arbonne two-moon links as metaphorical grace notes, not an intended tying together of worlds. The author-site FAQ preserves this distinction. Shared cosmological allusions alone do not justify attaching Fionavar, Ysabel, Tigana or Arbonne to this world. Under Heaven's separate moon/cultural setting also must not be merged from a third-party assertion; this gate does not require a wider Kay audit. No changes to those cards are proposed.

A descriptive fiction label may be selected at execution, without claiming an official franchise title. Use the author byline and explain the grouping. Do not imply that every two-moon Kay book belongs here.

## Eight novels and traits

1. The Lions of Al-Rassan (1995), currently Khayal. A physician and two celebrated warriors from different religious societies become entangled as rival kingdoms approach holy war. The three faiths and societies are part of the common world's political history. The old card records a curator-directed Khayal placement. Preserve that history, while applying the later whole-world rule. Its setting cannot define the whole multi-region fiction as a single non-Western counterpart culture.
2. Sailing to Sarantium (1998), currently The Sarantine Mosaic in Court spies. Mosaicist Crispin journeys to an imperial capital; a threatened foreign queen uses him to carry a marriage offer that could determine her kingdom's future. Imperial art, court manipulation and reconquest ambitions intertwine. Court spies is a genuine local fit, not the complete-world fit.
3. Lord of Emperors (2000), same card. Crispin's commission is threatened by coup and religious change; physician Rustem arrives from rival Bassania under orders to spy and assassinate. Sarantium's plans to retake Batiara, and the consequences for the artists, physician and ordinary people, connect the palace plot to the inter-state system. Do not erase the substantial court-spy content.
4. The Last Light of the Sun (2004), currently Viking fantasy. Erling raiding, Anglcyn defenses and diplomacy, and Cyngael rivalries place several cultures in a changing regional balance. Faerie and family relationships matter. It is not exclusively a Viking-world fiction once joined with the other seven books.
5. Children of Earth and Sky (2016), missing from current titles and nested books. A revenge-seeking Senjan woman, an artist, a spy and a merchant travel through competing maritime and imperial powers. Senjan opposition to Osmanli expansion, Dubrova's trade and Seressa's power shape their journeys. The author explicitly distinguishes ordinary people's needs from the great conflicts they live within; do not call every protagonist a professional geopolitical operator.
6. A Brightness Long Ago (2019), currently Courtly statecraft. Danio's recollection and Adria's attempted assassination draw multiple lives into rival mercenary commanders' contest in a city-state system. The political dynamic includes armies for hire, but the novel's art, memory, romance, healing and horse races are not all military campaigning or ruler-viewpoint governance.
7. All the Seas of the World (2022), currently Court spies. Two people put ashore for an assassination intended to alter the world's balance of power. Their pasts include religious exile, enslavement, escape and revenge. The publisher expressly ties the mission to empires and lives great and small. Covert action is important but does not define the entire eight-novel world.
8. Written on the Dark (2025), currently Court spies. Tavern poet Thierry Villar assists a royal murder investigation as Ferrieres faces civil conflict and invasion from Angland. Conspiracy remains an important local engine, alongside foreign war and the place of ordinary people and art within recorded history.

## Root-down walk

Speculative fiction > Fantasy: literal supernatural events, gods, visions, faerie and soul-bearing mechanical birds establish more than a wholly nonfantastic historical novel. Low magic is not no magic. Science-fiction extrapolation does not explain the governing premise.

Fantasy > Fantasy realms: a separately invented near-Europe with renamed polities, invented faiths and moons, not recognizable Earth history itself. These eight books do not establish the Earth crossing required by Portal fantasy. Do not import an Earth connection from an author's allusive grace notes after he distinguishes those from literal shared-world continuity.

Fantasy realms > Epic fantasy: many polities, centuries of internally realized history, long journeys, wars, religious and imperial change. Sword and sorcery's action-forward adventurer mode does not unite artists, physicians, merchants, poets and rival historical societies. Fairytale revision and Gothic dread do not govern. Comic fantasy fails the register. Heart has no shared courtship, cozy community, horse bond or family bond as its organizing spine; the horse races in Brightness do not change that. Far shores requires a non-Western counterpart culture and an aboutness gate: Lions alone meets a tempting local cultural label, but the whole world extends across Iberian, Byzantine, northern and Renaissance European analogues.

Epic > Political fantasy: repeated contests among rulers, armies, states and faiths shape the historical order and individuals' choices. Heroic/RPG class cannot describe this variable ensemble. High/Classic quest is not the continuing plot, Hard magic's explicit system is not the engine, Mythological retelling is not the common form, and Military's army/campaign/soldier unit does not contain all eight books.

Political has no honest existing child for this complete fiction. Court intrigue explicitly requires insider struggles within courts. Its Dynastic branch concerns contested legitimacy and a ruling house, Court spies concerns covert manipulation within a ruling court, and Court statecraft requires ruler-level diplomacy, government and survival. Each describes parts of this world, but none defines its complete recurring form across these eight outsider-rich narratives. Revolutionary has no sustained common liberation/overthrow movement. Election has no mass-franchise organizing contest. Magic politics has no common contest over public control of supernatural power. A geopolitical engine spanning rival polities is a positive uncovered slot at this existing deep parent.

Confidence: moderate. Court plots and military conflict overlap strongly with the proposed scope, and the author foregrounds art, memory and individuals as much as state systems. The recommendation does not pretend all eight novels are policy manuals, ruler narratives or spy missions. It asks which existing or proposed genre honestly describes their shared world's recurring conflicts without reducing it to one region or three of its books.
