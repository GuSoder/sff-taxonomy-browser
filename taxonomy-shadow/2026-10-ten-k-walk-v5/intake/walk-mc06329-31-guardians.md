# Star of the Guardians, whole-fiction walk
23 November 2026

## Verdict
No honest existing leaf. Propose Space fantasy as a fifth child of Future fantasy, level 4 (four clicks from homepage), for one connected seven-novel Star of the Guardians universe. Intake pauses for this one structural decision. No mutation or placement has been made.

## Scope
SFE explicitly connects the Star of the Guardians quartet with the Mag Force 7 trio, whose enlarged mercenary team first appears in the earlier sequence. ISFDB groups them under one universe, and the Robot Blues publisher description explicitly names that universe. One fiction, not two.

Seven original novels: The Lost King (1990), King's Test (1991), King's Sacrifice (1991), Ghost Legion (1993), The Knights of the Black Earth (1995), Robot Blues (1996), Hung Out (1998). SFE and ISFDB agree all seven years. Reprint years, excerpts, card game and omnibus editions are not additional novels. Kingmaker is a separate Dragon Corsairs title and is not included.

## Whole-fiction content
The original trilogy follows genetically engineered royal heir Dion's escape, return, contest over the space-rotation bomb and eventual coronation. Sagan's allegiance, Maigrey's rescue mission and sacrificial death, the rival republican regime and alien invasion all matter. Ghost Legion follows an illicit royal relationship, rival claimant, alien ghost army and Sagan's return, but also makes Maigrey an actual dead spirit under divine law, with supernatural assistance and eventual reunion beyond death.

Mag Force 7 adds three substantial technological mercenary adventures: Xris's revenge investigation and a royal assassination plot by Earth-supremacist religious extremists; a stolen robotic artifact and its pursuit before Corasian exploitation; and a mission to revolutionize a pacifist planet that becomes a criminal and imperial power struggle. These are not being silently described as seven necromancy novels. The divine afterlife is a real feature of their one universe, sufficient for the Fantasy root under the owner's rule, while interplanetary civilization and adventure are sustained across the whole.

## Root-down walk
Speculative fiction: all seven are speculative.

Fantasy rather than Science fiction: PRH's authorized Ghost Legion chapter-one sample directly gives dead Maigrey's viewpoint, her lack of a body, inability to touch living Sagan, continuing spiritual bond, dead Platus and a radiant celestial being deliberating under divine limits. This is not merely the scientifically explained alien Ghost Legion, nanobiotic bloodswords, genetic psi or a marketer's fantasy adjective. The dead-spirit viewpoint independently passes the literal supernatural test. SFE's normal SF/space-opera label is a documented label conflict, not controlling in this custom tree.

Real-earth fantasy rather than Fantasy realms: Kirkus explicitly locates the Knights' religious extremists on Earth. Their project is restoration of Earth supremacy, not a wholly independent invented cosmos. The existing Real-earth definition expressly includes Earth's universe, other planets and the afterlife. Galactic scenery does not negate that gate. No sustained Earth-to-independent-world exchange governs the fiction, so Portal fantasy fails.

Future fantasy rather than Historical or Contemporary: humanity inhabits an interplanetary galactic civilization with royal and republican regimes, starships, cyborgs and technologies. No past Earth era, present-day secret world or historical myth retelling governs.

Future fantasy children tested:
- Post-apocalyptic fantasy: the recent overthrow of a dynasty is a political revolution, not a civilization-ending Earth catastrophe. The functioning galactic government, navy, commerce and Earth-based supremacist movement do not establish a ruined/rebuilt Earth. Consequently neither Post Magicalypse nor Fantasy after Disaster passes its ancestor. No unverified collapse is inferred just to reach a deeper shelf.
- End-times fantasy: heavenly spirits and divine law are present, but this is not the biblical end of days or an angel-demon apocalypse. Literal Christianity-like theology is not sufficient.
- Vampire future: no vampire return or protected future-city vampire conflict.
- Psi future: engineered telepaths do matter, but the existing leaf is specifically a near-future Psy/changeling/human social order with suppressed feeling as rebellion. Genetics do not account for Maigrey's literal afterlife; seven books of dynastic space conflict and mercenary adventure do not become this sibling's specific social engine.

Fantasy realms' Gothic/Technomancy is a tempting visual rival, and The Locked Tomb sits there. Its independently invented-world ancestor fails here: Earth is explicit. Invented worship and Dynastic magic similarly cannot be borrowed from the invented-world side just because religion and royal legitimacy are important. Theological space opera and Galactic courtiers on the SF side fail the literal-supernatural root gate. No existing definition has been changed to admit the seed.

## Proposed leaf
Name: Space fantasy.
Parent: Future fantasy.
Definition: Future fantasy in which humanity's civilization extends beyond Earth and genuine magic, gods or supernatural agency act within its interplanetary or interstellar life. Space travel, inhabited worlds and their wider conflicts are sustained parts of the fiction, rather than a single alien visitor or a brief trip away from Earth. Not Post-apocalyptic fantasy's Earth-collapse aftermath, End-times fantasy's biblical apocalypse, Vampire future's future-city vampire return or Psi future's psychic social order. Not SF Science fantasy, where apparently fantastic effects have scientific engines; not independently invented Fantasy realms merely decorated with stars.

Scope is not limited to Christian theology, royal heirs, mercenaries, seven books, or Weis's exact devices. It covers the whole positive spacefaring future-fantasy slot. The name is an attested genre phrase, not a newly invented term. Mythcreants (2025), Book Riot (2021) and Esprit SF independently use it. Ordinary usage overlaps science fantasy, explicitly discussed in Mythcreants, so the custom tree's real-supernatural versus scientific-engine boundary must remain clear. No trademark clearance is claimed.
