Her first inadvertent intervention saves Brian but releases a weaponized-smallpox catastrophe. The Society, an ancient institution divided between factions and opposed to a female Shepherd, captures her. She escapes with disaffected members. ## Root-down hard walk
Speculative fiction > Science fiction: technical implant/interface, a physical probability machine, and altered event paths in Earth's near future. Sacred-site imagery and Shepherd terminology do not establish literal magic. Not an invented-world fantasy setting. No appeal to publisher genre alone is necessary.

Science fiction > Atemporal SF > Time travel fiction: actual retrospective crisis-point intervention and changed history invoke the owner's any-SF-time-element rule, not merely predictions. Near-future setting, plague disaster and suspense are surfaces rather than reasons to stop in Futurist, medical catastrophe or technothriller. Parallel world fiction is a substantial conceptual rival, but the protagonist changes past events and thereby her present; this is not a tour of independent coexisting worlds. No hard physical derivation controls the reading, so not Hard time travel.

Time travel fiction > Time machines: the causal engine is a physical machine connected by an implanted interface, not Olivia's unaided mind. Locus explicitly separates carrier-device from the machine at a physical location, which she tries to find. The Nameless Zine independently identifies the implant as an interface, one connection daily and the probability machine. Criminal Element describes the ancient device powering her abilities. The detailed completed Goodreads review ends with her destruction of that machine. The previous YAML's assertion that nothing is built is contradicted by this convergent evidence.

Mind over time remains the strongest mechanism rival: Olivia uses mental attention remotely and experiences altered possibilities, and the owner's approval says technological triggers are allowed. That does not erase the apparatus gate. Here the machine remains the ongoing source and object of institutional control, not a one-time technical trigger that leaves a person perceptually unstuck like Flux. She is the remote operator of the machine through a neural interface. The shelf definition does not require a wheeled vehicle or a passenger seated inside a cabinet.
