# Millennium's Rule whole-fiction root correction, 6 November 2026

Required Worldhopper frozen-member interrogation, not a corpus sweep. One shared fiction, all four published novels retained unchanged: Thief's Magic (2014), Angel of Storms (2015), Successor's Promise (2017), Maker's Curse (2020).

Rootwalk: Speculative fiction > Fantasy > Fantasy realms > Epic fantasy > Hard fantasy > Hard magic > Channeled hard magic.

Fantasy rather than SF: science-like handling of magic does not turn it into a physical scientific engine. Canavan's explicit premise is magic, sorcerers, magic creation and magical world-crossings. Portal's EARTH ancestor fails: primary book-one interview calls this a new universe; author essay explicitly describes her invented worlds, Leratia's postindustrial empire and Fyre's desert city. All four official synopses and completed-series review give invented worlds, not actual Earth. The old gateway rationale skipped that ancestor. No claim of full-text proof that the word Earth never appears; the positive evidence supports an invented-universe setting.

Fantasy realms: no governing Earth setting, fairy-tale revision, inherited author school, far-shore culture, comedy or bond-as-spine. Epic scale: interworld domination, magical depletion, wars, machines and the rebuilding/restoration of worlds. Not intimate sword-and-sorcery or one hero's class adventure. Political power matters but the sustained load-bearing engine is the magic resource itself: who can draw it, who creates it, depletion and replenishment, inventions that spend it, immortality and its price. This supports Hard fantasy, not a generic quest, divine worship or court-politics shelf.

Hard magic: official volume1 gives magic-powered industry and depletion; volume2 prohibited mechanical magic and the Raen's imposed controls; volume3 machines overrunning and draining worlds and immortality; volume4 Rielle's restoration and Tyen's attempts to neutralize war machines. Newtown's completed-four-book review follows those mechanisms and consequences across the whole sequence, with further consciousness-copy and creation costs. Not Time magic, written/code magic or spirit-bond terms. Vella's enchanted book is not the source of Tyen and Rielle's spell power: treating the whole fiction as Scripted or Bonded because of one companion would confuse an object with the engine.

Channeled: Strange Horizons describes practitioners drawing magic from the surrounding air, leaving soot/stain; the official volume3 excerpt directly shows Tyen drawing ambient magic from above a city, shield formation, depletion range and return to the surroundings, with the cost to weaker sorcerers. Reach/capacity and bodily vulnerability are explicit; the crossing costs breath, and the excerpt contrasts ordinary and ageless bodily needs. This is a drawn-resource magic system, not an inscribed-command or spirit-bond system. Do not invent a universal exhaustion threshold absent from the read sources.
