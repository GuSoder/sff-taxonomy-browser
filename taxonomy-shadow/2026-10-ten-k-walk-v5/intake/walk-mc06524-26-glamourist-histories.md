## Completed-fiction story coverage
1. Shades of Milk and Honey: Jane Ellsworth's gift for glamour contrasts with her beautiful sister Melody's marriage prospects. The art creates light, sound and appearance, and is treated as an accomplishment for young ladies. Vincent's professional glamour work develops Jane's capabilities and a meeting of minds. False suitors, dowry motives and an elopement threaten the family; Jane's exertion and intervention lead to a happy romance and marriage to Vincent. Later volumes are married-life adventures, not a reset to finding a suitor every book.
2. Glamour in Glass: the newlyweds honeymoon near Binche in Belgium1815 and experiment with storing glamour in glass. Vincent is secretly spying, causing a marital trust conflict Jane first misreads. Pregnancy makes glamour dangerous to her, raising questions of her worth outside her profession. Napoleon returns; Vincent's camouflage innovation makes him a target and he is captured. Jane disguises herself as a man and rescues him, using glamour despite risk. She miscarries; a detailed completed reader notes the novel does not conclusively make spellcasting its sole cause, while the creator explains exertion as her intended worldbuilding. Their stored-glamour invention gives the British a decisive advantage at Quatre Bras; creator explicitly says Waterloo never happens and its bridge has a different name. Do not repeat title-page blurb's France as the final location when creator confirms Belgium.
3. Without a Summer:1816London commission, sister Melody's Season and Irish Catholic Alastar O'Brien, lingering cold, unemployment and attacks on coldmongers. Tambora is still the real cause, not secretly magical weather. Jane's prejudices and Vincent's father's cruelty complicate matters. The protest march draws violent suppression and treason trials involving the couple, O'Brien and boys; family manipulation drives the crisis. Independent completed Locus review reaches the trial and stresses magic cannot alone save them. Later creator/finished-review continuity confirms Melody's marriage and Verbury's flight to Antigua, so this is not merely an unresolved-trial jacket account. Exact courtroom testimony choreography not established from accepted full sources and is not asserted.
4. Valour and Vanity: after Melody's wedding the couple travels toward Murano to refine glamour glass. Supposed pirate attack, apparent rescue and financial entanglement leave them penniless; a swindler seeks their secret process. Vincent's concussion, shame and family trauma stress the marriage, Jane works at a convent, and poverty exposes her class assumptions. They turn a scam back on its maker with a group including nuns, puppeteer/glassblower allies and Byron; completed reviews describe the caper's double-crosses and successful, satisfying execution. This is a whole-book heist, but not the organizing genre for all five novels.
5. Of Noble Family:1818, mourning for Princess Charlotte disrupts glamour work; a letter about Lord Verbury's death and family accidents sends them from Europe to Antigua. Jane discovers pregnancy on the voyage. Estate lies, abusive management, enslaved relatives and family coercion undermine the supposed routine legal task. Jane cannot safely perform glamour but explores African/Igbo traditions with Nkiruka and Dolly, confronting limits in her supposedly expert vocabulary and her privilege. Vincent must address the abusive paternal legacy while supporting her. The creator's long spoiler discussion says her planned successful slave rebellion was rejected during research, so do not label the actual novel a triumphant armed liberation of Antigua. The final story leaves the married couple on a satisfying high note, and the enslaved people have their own agency, plans and magical practices, not merely a white-rescuer function. Fantasy Literature flags medical uses of glamour and a larger final use without spelling it out. Exact birth/fate/estate settlement is not established by these accepted sources. Do not invent a baby name, death or island-wide abolition.

Across all five, physical exertion and pregnancy constrain genuine ether/illusion magic. Glass storage, coldmongers, medical UV and culturally different manipulation develop that same system. Scientific light terminology does not turn innate ether magic into SF: the creator repeatedly calls this magic and the story is written as openly magical historical fantasy, not a technological demystification. No literal textual manifestation, reality-retcon act, time travel or crossing out of Earth universe established.

## Actual unchanged tree walk
Speculative fiction > Fantasy: positive literal glamour, not only historical superstition or staged conjuring. No known-physics mechanism or buried-machine explanation established. Not Science fiction, Hard science fantasy, Fabulation or SF time travel. Austen homage concerns social form, not characters escaping a book. No invented requirement that the magic be epic in scale.

Fantasy > Real earth fantasy: identifiable England, London, Napoleon, Belgium/Quatre Bras, Murano/Venice and Antigua, real cultural period. Not independently invented Fantasy realms; not Portal fantasy, no Earth-to-otherworld crossing as shared premise. A Caribbean journey is geographic travel, not a portal. This excludes fantasy-realms Heart fantasy/Romantasy even though marriage is the emotional spine; never jump directly to a romance leaf and override Earth ancestry.

Real earth > Historical fantasy: early nineteenth-century Regency, roughly1814-1818, not Contemporary/Future. No chronicle straddling1900 and no timeless invented setting.

Historical > Alternate history fantasy: publicly practiced magic is socially accepted, economically/artisan regulated, militarily used, and creates a positively confirmed changed battle and later bridge history. Creator explains she keeps many broader balances familiar but chooses the Quatre Bras divergence. Thus Period fantasy's documented-era-as-stage/no-rewriting gate is the strongest rival that fails, not because Regency manners are absent. Secret history fails public magic despite Vincent's secret spying and the villains' plots. Mythic retelling fails: reworking Austen's plot/style is not an inherited sacred or legendary narrative, and the characters are new people rather than literal Bennets.

Alternate history > Alt flintlock fantasy: musket-age Regency/Napoleonic courts, armies, sail and social order. Not crusades, Ming, medieval spice routes, Renaissance, diesel wars or later steam industrial order. No eighteenth-century-only Rococo shortcut; series is nineteenth-century anyway. Alt steampunk loses to explicit early-Regency powder-and-sail setting and no central steam revolution. Existing definition expressly names Regency, so no widening or period seed.

Alt flintlock > Alt European flintlock: the fiction's shared milieu is Britain/continental Europe and its colonial extension, not the early American frontier of its one sibling. Final volume in Antigua is a serious geography countercheck: it is an imperial plantation novel connected to British family, property, navy and law, not an American-frontier setting. Do not pretend all pages occur on European soil. The fiction-wide European Regency court/marriage/craft world supplies the broader setting under the owner's whole-fiction instruction. The creator says family/relationship remains the series focus through the colonial volume. Existing Temeraire also travels globally, but comparator alone is not authority. Original genre YAML definition checked because embedded node has no definition field. No change to that definition.

Strongest rejected lower neighbors: Magicians in gaslight (craft, gender, institutions, era) and Gaslight technomancy (experimental glamour glass) both sit under the wrong Period-history ancestor given the overt alternate outcome. Caper or anti-colonial shelves would pick one volume and disregard the shared altered-world milieu. No dedicated Regency leaf currently exists to be presumed. No seed needed: exact existing era/geography leaf holds eight and fits honestly, nine after placement.
