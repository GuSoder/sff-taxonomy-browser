# Quest Kids whole-fiction walk, 24 November 2026

## Decision
One fiction, Quest Kids, three novels by Mark Leiknes, in Quest parody realms, taking that leaf from seven to eight. No structure or definition change. High confidence in root, corpus and leaf; form is a disclosed prose/comics hybrid, not silently treated as either pure prose or excluded graphic fiction. The old prep note saying skip MG is not an owner instruction and conflicts with the repository's source-grounded juvenile-scope reconciliation. Age alone does not exclude the novels.

## Corpus, form and dates
- Quest Kids and the Dragon Pants of Gold, 13 September 2022.
- Quest Kids and the Dark Prophecy of Doug, 26 September 2023.
- Quest Kids and the Mystery of the Goblin Gazebo, 7 January 2025.

Primary publisher calls Goblin Gazebo the conclusion to the three-book series. Kirkus's completed reviews give the original US release dates. The Hachette UK editions list later December 2022, December 2023 and June 2025 dates, respectively; those are not earlier originals. The creator's 2022 interview anticipated a third volume the next year after book two, but actual January 2025 publication supersedes that old plan.

Kirkus explicitly calls volume one a novel in a half-prose/half-comics style, and volume two a journal alternating block text and drawings. Publisher describes diary-style writing with graphic sequences. ISFDB classifies Doug as NOVEL, juvenile; Publishers Weekly places it under Novels, distinct from the Picture Books and Graphic Novels section. Unleashing Readers calls the books highly illustrated novels. This is enough positive novel-form evidence to include the hybrid under the same scope as other illustrated novels. No blanket children's-book ban is inferred. No media adaptation, anthology, omnibus or short work is being added. No wider shared-universe claim found in primary series sources.

## Whole story, including conclusion
Dragon Pants of Gold introduces an inexperienced five-member quest party: Ned, whose personal mission is to find his absent parents; apprentice wizard Gil with a false beard; elf child Terra; rock troll Boulder, more cook than fighter; and flatulent hybrid companion Ash. Their practical commission is not a dignified dragon-slaying quest: they must shave a golden-fleeced rage beast to produce stylish loungewear for a threatening dragon. Their failed prior quests, role reversals and fantasy-party conventions are the joke, not incidental relief in an otherwise solemn epic. Creator interview, publisher and completed Kirkus/Unleashing Readers reviews agree on this engine.

Dark Prophecy of Doug advances Ned's parent search through dueling prophecies, shipwrecks and absurd monsters. The apparent dark prophecy is the villain's fabrication. Doug is an ex-bard trying to conquer the world through his music and soul-stealing power. Kirkus's completed account reaches the revelation about Ned's parents and the escape leaving Ned comatose. This is a genuine continuing trilogy, not three unrelated funny adventures.

Goblin Gazebo resumes after a year with Ned awakening and Doug's undead-head fans almost controlling all seven kingdoms. The party seeks the means to neutralize him in the last free citadel, and the climax turns the Dark Lord confrontation into a battle of the bands. Completed Kirkus identifies the decisive help from a wizard and friendly monsters, the disarming of Doug, resolved family strands and the party's tearful separation. Its negative judgment of the finale does not change the concrete plot evidence. Full concluding review was read; publisher's teaser alone did not establish the ending.

## Root-down walk
Speculative fiction > Fantasy: wizard spells, enchanted barriers, soul theft and a magical monster world are literal, not scientific demystification or realist comedy. The in-world journal, fictional commentator J.B. Lucastoothes and fake prophecy do not establish that writing authors reality, that book characters cross out of their texts, or that the actual world is unstable. Those devices are narration and deception, not Fabulation machinery. Thus no root shortcut from the immediately preceding Library placement.

Fantasy > Fantasy realms: an invented Seven Kingdoms with elves, trolls and magical beasts. No evidence of actual Earth origins or Earth-to-world crossing, so neither Real earth fantasy nor Portal fantasy passes. The creator's generic description as middle ages is an era-like fantasy setting, not proof of our medieval Earth.

Fantasy realms > Comic fantasy realms: creator states he wants readers laughing; publisher and finished criticism identify the rapid gags, one-liners and spoofed fantasy-party roles. Comedy governs all three volumes, even though the search for Ned's parents supplies real feeling. Epic fantasy offers expanding realm stakes but worldbuilding at epic scale is not the main reading contract. Sword and sorcery has real action but the spell/sword/adventure conventions are being sent up. Fairytale fantasy would require a fairy-tale revision or its grammar as governing form, not merely a dragon and magical quest. Gothic realms fails the sustained menace register; Far shores fails the culture-counterpart aboutness; Heart fantasy has friendship but no central romance/horse/care contract, and a violent comic villain does not make it cozy.

Comic fantasy realms > Parody realms: the joke is the inherited quest party, dark prophecy, would-be hero, wizard costume, monster assignments and Dark Lord. Absurd realms is a real secondary reading because rock concerts and consumer-style dragon clothing transpose modern life, but its world's institutions and customs are not the main target independent of the quest script. Creator and three completed reviews repeatedly center unsuccessful quests and the fantasy team's conventional roles.

Parody realms > Quest parody realms: the whole trilogy follows inept questers, a fabricated dark prophecy and the villain's failed-world-conquest plot. Fairytale parody realms is a rival for the first dragon episode, yet royal romance, marriage, princess rescue and fairy-tale script are not the trilogy's governing joke. The last volume's battle of the bands does not abandon the quest parody; it is its climax. Whole fiction remains one card with all three original novels. No new leaf is required and eight does not trigger a split.
