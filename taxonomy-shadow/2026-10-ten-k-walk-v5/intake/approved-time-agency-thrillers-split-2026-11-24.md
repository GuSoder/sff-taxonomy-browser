## Distribution and proposed gates

### Time agency missions, 4 fictions / 20 novels
Institutional time-machine thrillers carried by an expedition, rescue or intervention: a team must reach, survive or change a historical situation. Not a plot whose organizing task is following one quarry, and not chiefly solving an unexplained crime. Protagonists need not be willing or salaried operatives.

The whole universe stays together rather than pretending all seven concern a time patrol. |

### Time agency manhunts, 3 fictions / 19 novels
Institutional time-machine thrillers carried by pursuit: tracking, escaping, protecting a target from or recovering control from a specific temporal intruder or fugitive. The quarry may be a human, cyborg or the carrier of an interface. Investigation can occur, but the central contest is hunter versus hunted.

## Counts
Across the proposed split: 4 + 3 + 3 = 10 fictions; 20 + 19 + 8 = 47 novels. These totals are calculated from the current manifests, not inferred from queue titles.

## Why this axis, and rejected alternatives
The action verbs come from the books: intervene/rescue/survive, pursue/escape/protect, investigate/uncover. The missions label is bounded positively by expedition or situational intervention, not everything that is neither a chase nor a mystery. All ten meet the same institutional-apparatus ancestor before this split.

An owner-type split into government/corporate/alien would scatter stories with similar action and overstate the uncertain origins of the Society apparatus and Neku's technology. A willing-operative versus unwilling-civilian split is unstable within Gordian, Acorna and Chaos, and names a character's initial status rather than the story. Historical-destination versus future-destination fails the whole worlds, which cross settings. A binary action-versus-mystery leaves seven under a broad action residue and three in mystery; the actual pursuit cluster is more useful. A Time war child would be dominated by Terminator and parts of Gordian, overlapping military and multiverse branches without covering the rest cleanly.

## Names
Exact full-phrase searches did not establish these as formal subgenres or identify an exact named-property collision. This is not trademark clearance. The established source terms are time agency, mission, manhunt and mystery; these full labels are descriptive combinations. A Time Travel Nexus taxonomy explicitly uses time-travel missions while stating it is the writer's proposed identifier, so it is supporting vocabulary, not proof of a settled critical genus.

The name pair/trio works without the parent: institutional temporal missions, temporal pursuit, temporal investigation. Keeping Time agency carries the institutional time context and the distinguishing noun carries the action. Generic Missions / Manhunts / Mysteries would fail standalone context. Time missions loses institution; Temporal casefiles implies a detective bureaucracy that Company Town and End of the World Blues do not have. Time agency noir would exclude the procedural tone of the Gordian Files. Time agency fugitives wrongly implies the For King protagonist is chiefly fleeing rather than hunting. No borrowed franchise name, calendar-year bound, A-plus-B construction or new fused word.

These are conservative, readable names rather than a claim to have found a witty genre coinage. They remain subject to the owner's ear.
