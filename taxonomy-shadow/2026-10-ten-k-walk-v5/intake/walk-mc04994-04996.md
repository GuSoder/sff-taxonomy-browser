# The Phoenix Hoard whole-fiction walk - 2026-11-06

Living city fantasy, 3 to 4 fictions. One trilogy by Julia Vee AND Ken Bebelle: Ebony Gate (2023), Blood Jade (2024), Pearl City (2025). Publisher calls Pearl City the conclusion; author interview confirms trilogy acquisition. Bronze Blade is explicitly a prequel novella in the author interview, not a fourth novel. Authors list Seattle Slayers separately; shared authorship and Asian urban magic alone do not establish shared continuity.

## Every novel's own engine
Ebony Gate: retired clan assassin and antiques importer Emiko Soong must answer her mother's family's blood debt to a shinigami by recovering the Ebony Gate that seals Yomi's hungry ghosts, or lose her soul as its anchor. San Francisco itself awakens into sentience and chooses her as guardian. Completed reviews independently establish both the blood-debt action and literal sentient-city development.
Blood Jade: now Sentinel of sentient San Francisco, Emiko is called by the city and an allied clan to investigate a murder with a magical signature like her mother's. The investigation takes her to Tokyo, her brother Tatsuya's tourney and the General's hidden army empowered by Blood Jade. Family secrets collide with city duty. Publisher and Library Journal describe this volume itself; it is not assumed to repeat book one's retrieval.
Pearl City: returning to San Francisco after unmasking the General, Emiko faces clan war and an ancient dragon power crossing from the Realm. The General wants that power; she must defend her city and its magical AND mundane people. The completed Reading Reality review identifies her continuing Sentinel duty, the dragon threat, and San Francisco as a character. The finale concerns how she uses her destructive power and chooses her loyalties, not another detective case.

## Actual root-down walk
Speculative fiction -> Fantasy: dragon magic, shinigami, soul-bound sword, ghosts and a sentient city are supernatural, not technologically explained. Fantasy realms fails as governing setting: dragon Realm/Yomi are important otherworlds but these books are rooted in contemporary Earth, San Francisco and Tokyo. Portal fantasy fails as governing crossing narrative despite liminal gates. Real earth fantasy -> Contemporary fantasy: present-day diaspora urban life, not a historical-era tale or future setting.
Contemporary alternatives: Mythic fantasy is a strong rival because the authors explicitly draw on East Asian lore. Modern Chinese myth is too narrow for this mixed Japanese/Chinese invented-clan cosmology; however mixture alone would not defeat its parent. Here the city's awakening, calls, power bond and guardianship supply the whole-fiction urban subject rather than chiefly encounters with inherited deities. Modern invented myth is a rival for original dragon cosmology but less specific to this city-centered trilogy. Magical coming-of-age fails adult ex-assassin identity, not adolescent initiation; domestic fantasy fails household-primary site, despite clan/found-family stakes. Dark fantasy fails dominant horror/torment, despite trauma and violence. Supernatural fantasy's being/immortality divisions less specific than city subject; magic realism fails sustained martial supernatural conflict and explicit separate hidden community; folktale fails governing tale-shape/comic-animal moral structure.
Urban fantasy passes contemporary urban institutions and hidden clans. Its eight children: Paranormal bricks passes because the city becomes a literal living subject and chooses/calls its Sentinel, not merely attractive scenery. Fae founders fails faerie-foundation premise; publicly paranormal fails ordinary outsiders' magical filter (foo lion perceived as dog); modern goth/Occult legacy is a substantial rival for hereditary clan debts, power and duty, but the ongoing city-Sentinel bond and civic defense displace the inherited-family position Emiko has left. Occult tradition fails practiced-family-art as main whole-fiction subject. Occult mystery is true of book two's investigative setup, not whole three's organizing engine; neither police nor hired detective series. Occult secret service/counterops fails organization/state service as whole engine, despite city defense and hidden magical enemies. Modern weird west fails small-town/frontier-desert engine; Freakbusters fails weaponized weird-science engine. Paranormal bricks -> Living city fantasy: literal sentience and city-Sentinel agency across all three. Shadow city fantasy's separate hidden double is not the decisive subject, despite a hidden magical community. No gate changes. Wobble explicit: clan identity and blood inheritance are central emotional stakes, and Tokyo occupies much of the middle novel. Positive living-city aboutness is nevertheless evidenced, not inferred from a single mention.
