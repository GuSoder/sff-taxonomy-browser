Retain Future social SF as umbrella, zero direct fictions. Three new children with positive definitions and their own original portraits after approval. Counts 5/3/2. These are plain descriptive shelf names, not claims of three formally established literary subgenres. Futures carries the speculative-social setting in each name; the first word names a concrete subject shared by the books. No invented blend needed. Weakness: broad, less punchy than some fantasy names, and futures can sound like foresight studies or financial contracts. That is preferable to a clever word that obscures the books. Future-social context is strongest when paired with the concrete adjective, but the user may prefer a more explicit SF suffix. Do not silently add it to the served names.

Political futures (5): future Earth societies whose defining rules organize public power, legitimacy, citizenship, diplomacy or political violence, tested through characters living within those rules.
- Solar Lottery: computerized sortition, Quizmaster, sanctioned assassination and manipulation of who rules. Keep the Preston space-search subplot explicit. Do not reduce the book to an election campaign.
- Adiamante: Earth power-debt and nonaggression institutions tested by the cyb fleet. Diplomacy and conservation determine legitimate public action. It is not an electoral story, and not primarily an economic marketplace.
- Game of the Gods: judge Max travels six political orders and confronts how citizenship, faith, liberty and loyalty structure power. Author's own statement makes the political systems explicit, while also calling the book an action-adventure. Family rescue and ensemble adventure are the real wobble.
- The Centenal Cycle: entire three-novel arc, elections, Information's legitimacy and monopoly, uneven inclusion, alternative infrastructures. Narrative Disorder short story rides with the fiction but is not a fourth novel. Do not reclassify the trilogy from Infomocracy's first election alone.
- All the Lives He Led: the hardest political placement. Refugee indenture and Pompeii tourism give its future society, but pervasive terrorism and the final third's research into political violence/revenge govern the whole novel beyond the work setting. Could sit economically if focused only on its first half, which would be dishonest. Retain the fragility rather than pretend it is a neat government-design novel. Definition must say political violence as well as institutions, not restrict to elections.

Cultural futures (3): future Earth societies examined through the production, transmission and valuation of language, art and public meaning. Artistic or communicative work is the way into the social order, not a physics puzzle.
- The Tongue Trade: professional dialects, interpreter guild/oath and communication across social strata. Murder investigation tests the linguistic institution.
- Archform: Beauty, including Flash: art/music/media value and technologically amplified persuasion connect both novels. Flash adds product placement, rezrock election campaigning and corporate power. This is the strongest crossover with Political futures. The cultural mechanism, shaping judgment through forms of expression, is consistent across both, while Archform itself is not exclusively an election story. Keep one fiction/two novels and do not split its books.
- Paris in the Twentieth Century: a poet and musician's place in an industrial society that values technical/financial utility. Education, art and work are linked, so this also crosses economic life. The cultural exclusion and loss of humane expression are the distinctive whole-book subject, not merely a bank-job story.

Economic futures (2): future Earth societies examined through access to goods, paid services, expertise and markets, with systems of value and exchange organizing everyday cooperation and exclusion.
- The Age of the Pussyfoot: revived Forrester misunderstands wealth, the costs and access rules of joymaker life, paid services, employment and the excluded Forgotten Men. Culture shock, alien danger and the Luddite ending remain. Do not call it just a smartphone prediction or ideal post-scarcity utopia.
- Earthweb: one1999 novel with2018 revision; distributed markets, forecasts, contractual trust, reputation and paid expertise coordinate the planet. The boarding/command mission is the test and a genuine rival plot. The branch should not imply stock-trading-only novels. Primary sample gives reward incentives and livelihoods repeatedly; three completed critics make cooperation systems central.

Why this shape: discovered after per-book content reads. The initial government-versus-livelihood4/6 hypothesis failed because Flash and All the Lives He Led straddle it. A simple abstract individual/group or good/bad society split would also bury concrete subjects. Three shared subjects preserve the real culture/language cluster and avoid an all-other-social-science remainder. Still not orthogonal: these novels intentionally combine politics, culture and economics. Definitions and per-fiction dominant subject settle overlaps rather than pretending the borders are natural laws. 5/3/2 is acceptable without distorting contents for balance.

Rejected/unused names and reasons:
- Civic futures: strong future-politics signal but exact name already used by multiple organizations and a crypto product; not a fiction/game clash found, but confusion unnecessary. Civic also understates violent political conflict.
- Market futures: existing M. Ian Bell short fiction title and reads as futures contracts; too narrow for Pussyfoot's whole society.
- Working futures: exact speculative anthology title, and work-only excludes wider goods/access systems.
- Livelihood futures: real development-policy phrase, precise about earning but too narrow for Earthweb's collective decision system.
- Culture futures: sounds too close to Banks's Culture when spoken; Cultural futures is ordinary adjective grammar and has conference/government-program usage.
- Government futures: rules out All the Lives He Led's political violence and informal power.
- Future civics/culture/commerce: the commerce word excludes paid-access social arrangements, while civics sounds educational. No need to coin a portmanteau for familiar subjects.
