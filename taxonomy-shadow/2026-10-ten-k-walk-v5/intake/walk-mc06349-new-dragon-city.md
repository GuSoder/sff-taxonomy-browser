# New Dragon City whole-fiction walk
23 November2026

## Verdict
Place one fiction comprising New Dragon City(2022), Dawn of the Dragons(2025) and War of the Wings(2026) in existing Post Magicalypse,9to10. Freeze intake immediately for a split audit. No eleventh fiction, no new structural execution without approval. Moderate-to-high classification confidence, with the SF/Fantasy boundary and contradictory elapsed-time blurbs disclosed below.

## Scope, publication and continuity
The publisher's series list explicitly groups all three, calling Dawn a prequel and War a sequel. The prequel's Lucas/Cinder and first novel's Noah/Asha converge in War. This is one connected fiction, not three cards. The author describes Scorched and Dragon Ops as separate series; no common-universe evidence merges them. Original title published4October2022, prequel2September2025, sequel6October2026US/29October2026UK. The latter is published as of this23November2026walk, not a forthcoming hold. Publisher UK and NetGalley publisher-supplied metadata independently fix dates; Kirkus series listing corroborates. Hardcover/paperback ISBNs are editions, not extra novels. One aggregator called Dawn a novella, contradicted by author's prequel-novel framing, publisher320pages, Kirkus and completed reviews. Retain as novel.

## Whole-fiction content
New Dragon City follows twelve-year-old Noah surviving a dragon-decimated New York with his father, while his mother has joined a human/dragon coexistence community. When Noah and Asha save one another, a healing-linked telepathic bond lets each see the other's perspective. The completed Medium review recounts the capture of Asha, confrontation with Noah's father, intervention by coexistence allies and defeat of violent human antagonists. Its criticism of rushed reconciliation is evidence of a finished review, not a claim that the plot has no stakes. YABC editor reviews corroborate healed burns, community and family conflict.

Dawn goes back to the invasion's beginnings in upstate New York. Lucas rescues young Cinder, human/dragon misunderstandings turn goodwill into violence, rogue dragons worsen the wider conflict, and the peaceful small group cannot prevent the worldwide fall. Kirkus and YABC completed reviews give the dual viewpoints and local resolution. The author's2025interview explicitly explains the challenge of a happy prequel ending leading into civilization's destruction: small right actions matter even when the wider catastrophe happens.

War reunites both pairs five months after the first story. Boston's City of Scales claims a restored human/dragon paradise, but its dragons have replaced heavy machinery and are being mistreated. YABC's completed review describes divergent human/dragon reactions and concealed injustice. It is reproduced nearly verbatim from Mark My Words, so those are one evidential line, not two independent confirmations. The review states the conflict is resolved with below-ground information affecting Maya, but does not give every ending detail. No invented detailed final battle or villain reveal is supplied here.

## Root conflict
Common Sense Media assigns the first novel Science Fiction and describes a sci-fi twist. This is a serious rival, not suppressed. Dawn's ancient dragons awakening after hibernation could by itself be biological/cryptid SF. Fire breathing, intelligence and a monarchy alone would not prove literal magic. Against that, completed Children's Book Review explicitly calls the dragons magical; the completed Medium review identifies magical healing and communication following wound-healing, YABC's original editor reviews independently corroborate cured burns, and its prequel review supplies telepathic communication. No read source explains these powers through genetics, machinery, an implant or a scientific transformation. These are actual sentient dragons with supernatural healing/bonding, not game avatars, metaphors, hoaxes or merely a modern dinosaur disaster. Fantasy is the reasoned classification. Moderate rather than absolute confidence because no complete primary novel text was read, and literal biology is the alternative if future evidence explains the powers scientifically.

## Root-down walk
1. Speculative fiction: real dragons, extraordinary healing/bond and destroyed civilization are material to all three novels.
2. Fantasy beats Science fiction on the supported supernatural dragon powers. Contemporary-SF Apocalyptic/Monster/cryptid and futurist post-holocaust routes lose at the root. Unknown ancestry is not evidence of aliens, and hibernation alone is not magic. Do not label the fall a named divine curse.
3. Real earth fantasy, not independently invented Fantasy realms: New York City, upstate New York and Boston are our Earth, including recognizable institutions and culture. Dragon society does not create an independently invented world. Portal fantasy fails because no inter-world crossing is established; underground hibernation is not a portal.
4. Future fantasy, not Historical fantasy: no past historical era or retold myth. Contemporary fantasy is the strongest setting rival because Dawn depicts the modern-world onset and the first novel retains modern ruins. But the original and sequel concern established post-collapse life and attempts to remake human/dragon society. The prequel explains that world's fall rather than turning the whole fiction into unchanged contemporary dragon life. Future is not decided by simply counting two of three books, but by the series' post-civilization organizing condition. Comparable mixed-strand existing fictions retain this ancestor.
5. Future fantasy > Post-apocalyptic fantasy: ruined city, surviving settlements, lost infrastructure and disputed rebuilding dominate. Not biblical angels/demons End-times; not never-fallen Vampire future or Psionic fantasy; telepathy alone does not make a never-fallen psychic social order; no spacefaring civilization for Space fantasy.
6. Post-apocalyptic > Post Magicalypse, not Fantasy after Disaster: actual dragons materially caused civilization's fall. Warming/hibernation may precede their appearance, but the supported destructive agent is the dragons, not a climate collapse followed incidentally by dragons. They can cause destruction with fire and still be the supernatural beings responsible. The fall and subsequent magical bonds belong to one causal fiction. No definition change.
7. Post Magicalypse is currently9fictions and has no children. Add this tenth and freeze for content-first split audit. The contemporary-dragons alternative fails its unchanged modern-life context; the destroyed/rebuilt Earth is not ordinary modern sept politics. Dragon-rider fellowship/high-fantasy/magic-system leaves fail the Fantasy-realms ancestor; the interspecies bond and youth audience cannot bypass setting.

## Contradictions and limits
Publisher/author first-book blurb says three years after the fall. Kirkus/Dawn review and other finished readers indicate Noah7at onset/12later, five years later, and Common Sense mentions five/six years. Do not choose one precise year for the entire sequence. Both lines establish the material fact of an already-fallen civilization and seasonal underground survival. No Earth calendar date assigned. Common Sense's SF label retained above. Origin/historical hibernation is lower-confidence completed-review evidence, not proof all dragons share one origin or that their powers are scientific. Newdragoncity.com was not used as author authority, since its binding was not established. No franchise comparisons establish shared continuity.
