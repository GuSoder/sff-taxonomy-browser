Walk: Speculative fiction > Fantasy > Fantasy realms > Heart fantasy > Romantasy > Bonded romantasy > Duty bond romantasy. One complete three-novel fiction, not three collections despite the old sweep's title-based exclusions.
Tales of a Monstrous Heart (2024): Fey scholar Kat must accept a Mage Partnership with Emrys Blackthorn to finish her education. Their shared investigation of murders, disappearing Fey and cursed texts runs alongside the forbidden romance. The mage is her ally and mentor, not a factional enemy. Tales of a Deadly Devotion (2025): Fairfax Manor is ruined, dark magic threatens Kat, and Alma's additional viewpoint and relationship with Thean expand the romantic and found-family stakes. Independent reviews, including a negative one, describe Kat's love and need to protect Emrys as central, rather than incidental. Tales of a Treacherous Fate (2026): Alma is the Countess's prisoner; Kat, Emrys and Gideon must rescue her while Montagor's magic pursues the fleeing Fey. The lovers seek the Old Gods' help against the unleashed demons. The third-volume Bookstr feature quotes the text and describes increased romance, passion and chosen-family stakes, supporting continuity of the bond-first shape. Caveat: that feature is promotional in tone, and no independent critical account of the final outcome was found. No ending beyond the sourced plot is asserted.
Root: literal Fey, demons and spells, not SF. Elysior is invented, with no Earth frame or portal; references to earth mean the ground, not proof of our planet. At Fantasy realms, Epic is a genuine rival because of war, persecution and gods. Yet romance is co-equal with that external conflict throughout the published corpus, not merely the setting's emotional garnish. Mage training is not a whole-series Heroic class story, war is not Military ranks or campaign life, the political order is not the independent narrative prize, gods enter as allies rather than the theological subject, and no Hard magic engineering or governing High quest displaces the relationships. Sword and Sorcery's action-first adventure, Far shores culture-aboutness and governing Comic fantasy fail. Jane Eyre is a novel, not a fairy tale, so its influence does not supply the Fairy-tale revision gate.
Gothic realms is the strongest rival: the manor, curses, demons and dark academia are real Gothic furniture. But the love story remains co-equal rather than dread alone being the payoff; neither the dead, appetite nor demonic power defines the entire relationship-centered series better. The murder investigation is not the invariant engine once the second and third volumes expand into war and rescue. Manners and social courtship as ritual are not the governing form. Heart fantasy fits, but not Cozy's safety and low intensity or Horse's animal bond. Kat/Emrys and Alma/Thean sustain the romantic reading.
Romantasy's starting-tie axis: Bonded, not Enemies-to-lovers. Kat and Emrys are not personal rivals, hostile rulers or lovers on opposed factional sides. Their compulsory professional partnership throws them into one investigation and later a shared catastrophe; Duty bond describes the romance growing through that work. Soul bond would invent an unverified innate mate or blood tie. Magic bond would confuse individual curses and supernatural enemies with a curse binding the lovers to each other. Court bond is a genuine rival because a reader calls the Mage Partnership a training contract, but it is an academic work placement, not a court-decreed marriage, diplomatic match or royal bargain. The law forbids the romance rather than imposes their match; the tie is their shared work. No definition widening or new leaf is required.
