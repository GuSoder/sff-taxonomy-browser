## Both included novels
Moonstorm follows orphaned clanner Hwajin/Hwa Young into the empire that destroyed her moon, through academy and emergency lancer recruitment, bonding with Winter's Axiom, and uncovering the empire's gravitational genocide weapon. She and fellow pilots ultimately destroy the vessel whose worshippers power the black-hole weapon, saving more lives at the cost of those aboard, and defect to the clanners. Starstrike continues that same pilot group among the clanners, whose competing beliefs produce a different gravitational order. Reintegration, contested loyalty and war atrocities on both sides complicate the insurgent-vs-imperial picture. This is not merely a school novel, and the second volume does not replace the first volume's supernatural premise with a scientific explanation.

## Root mechanism
The creator describes a deliberate science-fantasy metaphor in which conformity maintains gravity and nonconformity threatens a world's stability. The authorized Moonstorm opening actually depicts a household disagreement loosening local gravity: furniture rises, an adult calls for consensus, and the knife-voting ritual steadies it. This is not just subjects mistakenly attributing a technological gravity generator to religion. Completed reviews independently explain that actual worship supplies gravity and can be concentrated into a black-hole weapon. Locus's Starstrike review confirms that faith in the Empress still maintains consistent gravity in the sequel while the clanners' divergent beliefs maintain another order.

The fantasy-side decision rests on ritual and faith having literal material efficacy, not on generic impossible FTL, sentient machinery, temporarily breathable aether, a review's fantasy label, or failure to explain every component. No accessed source supplies a technological mediation that turns the consensus ritual into disguised engineering. The distributor's old publisher-style description does mention discovery of advanced technology; this is retained as a counterpoint but does not explain away the specific primary scene and completed two-book mechanism. The author's science-fiction marketing is a label conflict under this custom tree's strict no-real-magic rule, not a veto on that rule. This is not Rhine-style psychic faculty or a medical/pseudoscientific research programme: communal observance itself sustains the physical world.

## Setting and ancestor walk
Real earth fantasy rather than Fantasy realms: Stardist's distributor record explicitly identifies a lost colony of Earth in the far future. The same publisher-style wording is independently repeated by other booksellers. This is a positive origin source, not an inference from Korean names. Current PRH-style jackets omit the origin detail rather than contradict it. Consequently do not use the independently invented-world Magitech leaf merely because there are mecha and ritual engines. No sustained crossing between Earth and an independent realm makes this Portal fantasy.

Future fantasy rather than Historical/Contemporary: the spacefaring colony civilization, giant lancer machines, imperial fleets and engineered worlds are future human society, not a historical Korean period or modern secret world.

Future-fantasy siblings: Post-apocalyptic fantasy fails because no civilization-ending Earth catastrophe and its remnant life govern this fiction. The destruction of one moon and the threatened black-hole genocide are not a licence to invent Earth's collapse. Neither Post Magicalypse nor Fantasy after Disaster passes that parent. End-times fantasy fails biblical apocalypse; Vampire future fails vampires; Psi future fails the specific psychic social-order engine and does not explain the literal ritual cosmology; Dying Earth fantasy fails terminal deep-time Earth as the subject. Space fantasy positively fits sustained interstellar human civilization with genuine supernatural efficacy. Existing Star of the Guardians and Archonate are comparators, not permission to alter this definition.
