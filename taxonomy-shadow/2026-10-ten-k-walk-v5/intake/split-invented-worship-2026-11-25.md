# Invented worship split recommendation, 25 November 2026

## Recommendation

Retain Invented worship as an umbrella with zero direct fictions. Create **Defying invented gods** (7 fictions, 22 novels) and **Invented god quests** (3 fictions, 8 novels). These are descriptive working genre names, not claims of established critical terms. Research recommendation below was subsequently approved for execution as recorded at the end.

Full ancestor path: Speculative fiction > Fantasy > Fantasy realms > Epic fantasy > Mythological fantasy > Invented mythology > Invented gods > Invented worship.

Axis: the governing mortal undertaking in relation to the divine. Is the continuing story about securing freedom from gods' claims, or about seeking/recovering a divine presence, power or relic? This is not good versus evil, loyalty versus betrayal, or gods present versus absent. Neither leaf requires all protagonists to share one intention. Whole-fiction subject takes priority over an ending, a blessing, a single mission or one character's role.

The7/3 distribution is intentional. The apparent5/5 service/defiance split breaks on Green and Gossamer. A4/3/3 Temple/Divine possession/Divine quest split is more balanced, but makes religious institutions rather than personal claims stand for Green and Gossamer, and makes late Soulfire possession stand for all six Legacy/Soulfire books. Those are worse reader promises.

## Definitions and complete mapping

### Defying invented gods, 7 fictions, 22 novels

Invented-world fantasy in which people's struggle for agency against the claims, demands or domination of active gods is the governing subject. Defiance can mean resisting a divine assignment, exposing a religious order, escaping possession, restraining a god or overthrowing divine rule. It need not mean killing every god or rejecting every form of worship. Protecting one god from another threat does not erase a continuing struggle against divine claims.

- Age of the Five,3. Auraya's priesthood becomes an explicit choice between loved people and divine demands, while the Wilds seek the truth behind the gods. A priestess is not necessarily a loyal-service invitation across the trilogy. ### Invented god quests, 3 fictions, 8 novels

Invented-world fantasy whose governing undertaking is a journey or search to find a god, recover divine power or obtain/protect a concrete divine relic. The divine object is central, not merely a deity commissioning an ordinary quest. Conflict with gods can occur, but emancipation from their claims is not the fiction's primary invitation.

- Thunderer,2. Arjun's search for his lost god the Voice anchors the first novel and the continuing divine/city mystery. The sequel has a real rescue, identity and temporal-city shift; that is a whole-fiction wobble inherited from the already approved Invented worship placement, not a second lost-god quest asserted without evidence. https://fantasybookcritic.blogspot.com/2007/12/thunderer-by-felix-gilman.html and https://fantasybookcritic.blogspot.com/2008/12/gears-of-city-by-felix-gilman-reviewed.html
- The Godslayer Chronicles,2 published novels. Tylar is falsely accused of killing a goddess, receives her Grace and an embedded guardian, and quests for Rivenscryr. The second novel requires keeping an ancient god's skull from the Cabal and a journey into the Hinterland. The title does not make this a sustained revolt against gods. The incomplete planned series is not counted. https://fantasyliterature.com/reviews/shadowfall/ and https://www.publishersweekly.com/978-0-451-46113-1 and https://fantasybookcritic.blogspot.com/2013/08/a-paean-to-myrillia-godslayer.html
- Dragonships of Vindras,4. The saga's explicit quest is for the sacred spiritbones to summon Ilyrion and save the people from an evil god. Seeking the final bone remains central in the finale. There is a divine war, but the recovery expedition is more specific than calling the whole fiction rebellion or obedient worship. https://www.torforgeblog.com/2016/11/28/paperback-spotlight-doom-of-the-dragon-by-margaret-weis-tracy-hickman/ and https://www.deseret.com/2016/2/20/20582820/book-review-doom-of-the-dragon-is-an-excellent-example-of-authors-world-building-skills/

## Boundary tests

- Green and Gossamer are not sold as loyal service. Both contain divine protection and opposition, and the definition openly preserves that complexity.
- Gossamer also has a search, but its purpose is ending compulsory fatal magic; the search is the means to defiance, not primarily recovery of something divine.
- Age of Five also has a scroll quest, but its religious reckoning and the gods' claims govern the whole trilogy.
- Godslayer has divine bodies/Grace and a suspect title, but the published books' concrete quest/relic plot wins over projecting a god-killing program from the series name.
- Dragonships opposes an evil god, but the four-volume relic recovery is the sustained undertaking.
- Legacy/Soulfire's final possession is not back-projected across all six books. Agency under divine manipulation is the common subject; hosting is only one mechanism.

## Name checks

Final cold-read pick is Defying invented gods, not the clunky noun stack God defiance fantasy. Divine rebellion fantasy can mean gods themselves rebelling and more strongly promises organized overthrow. Defying invented gods keeps personal refusal and resistance within scope without promising total victory, apostasy or killing. Defying gods fantasy reads less naturally as a genre modifier. No dash follows the owner naming rule.

No established critical subgenre located that exactly names either subject without changing its scope. Godpunk is established but imports modern-world reinterpretation and does not name this invented-world division. Godbound/Godsworn sound like willing allegiance and have bare-word game/book associations. Godslaying falsely promises killing in every fiction. Temple fantasy names institutional furniture rather than all these fictions' shared conflict. Divine possession fantasy would make late Soulfire host mechanics stand for the complete fiction. No dash is used in either recommended name.

Public exact-phrase searches for Defying invented gods, Divine rebellion fantasy, Defying gods fantasy, God quest fantasy and Invented god quests returned no exact complete-name property conflict. Bare Divine Quest has several games/serials; that is not the user's perfect-name collision test and no legal clearance is claimed. Defying invented gods is an ordinary descriptive phrase rather than a new compound. The final exact full-name check was completed before handoff.
