## Strongest replacement: Supernatural kingmakers

This changes the naming approach instead of finding a softer word for enchantment. The shared action is making, maintaining, constraining and unmaking rulers through powers to which those rulers are beholden. Kingmaker is a hard political actor-word, not a decorative throne or fantasy-title compound. Supernatural makes the magic explicit without limiting it to ghosts, witches or gods alone.

The lexical foundation is ordinary English, not a new formal genre claim. Oxford defines a kingmaker as someone whose political influence brings someone else to power: https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/kingmaker . The proposed label extends that familiar actor-function to supernatural powers and their binding instruments. Cambridge supernatural: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/supernatural .

## Important scope caveat

The old binding-versus-wielding distinction must not silently become every story with a magical adviser. Dragon Prince has Andrade's marriage planning, Cheysuli has gods' prophecy, and Drowning Empire has magical nonroyal actors. They stay with Sorcerer dynasties because the whole-fiction decisive instrument is producing, acquiring and wielding royal magical capacity. Supernatural kingmakers would require that sovereignty be materially beholden to the external power or binding arrangement itself. The actor-word is a reframing of the existing second group's mechanism, not permission to expand membership or retcon away these crossovers.

If the owner hears kingmakers as only one adviser who picks a new incumbent, the label is too narrow. That is its genuine weakness, especially Obsidian. Do not promise a perfect partition or present the rename as approved. The definition should explain making, sustaining, constraining and undoing rulers, while retaining the supernatural-dependence priority over ordinary royal spellcraft.

## Rejected sharper possibilities

Cursed dynasties: punchy, but falsely makes beneficial Kinghood a malign curse. Royal geasa: fan/myth vocabulary and genuine sacred-kingship connection, but a geas is a specific prohibition/obligation, not every divine vessel or revenant curse. Puppet crowns/Crown thralls: implies rulers lack agency, false across all five. Occult dynasties: describes hereditary secret knowledge, too close to the sorcerer side and to conspiracy language. Witch kingmakers: excludes Living Flame, Beaconfolk and divine Kinghood. Spirit kingmakers: returns to the rejected spirit-only implication. Spellbound dynasties: near-singular existing Spellbound Dynasty novel and too close in shape to the rejected name.
