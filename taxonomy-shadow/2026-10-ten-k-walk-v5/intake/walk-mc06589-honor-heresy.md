## Verdict and corpus

## Root-down walk
Speculative fiction > Fantasy. The ghosts are literal, not an atmospheric metaphor or an unconfirmed haunting. Completed reviews describe magical powers and ghosts revealing the suppressed history at the climax. Science fiction loses: no technology-based causal explanation, no SF reality-writing mechanism. Books in a library and censorship are not proof of Fabulation. The academic discourse is about the world's history, not characters discovering that their world is a text.

Fantasy > Fantasy realms. Northgard is an invented country, not a documented Earth locale or historical period; the publisher and completed reviews do not supply Earth origins. Real earth fantasy's Haunted house or Ghost stories therefore cannot be borrowed merely because the library is haunted. Portal fantasy fails because no exchange with Earth is established.

Fantasy realms > Gothic realms. Menace, persecution, confinement, the uncanny library and ghosts define the reader's experience. Epic fantasy's wars are a real alternative, but the external siege supplies pressure while the story mostly follows two confined researchers. This is not an ensemble campaign, military narrative or invented mythology told for its own sake. Sword and sorcery loses to the inquiry rather than martial adventure. Fairytale fantasy has no revised or invented fairy-tale form. Far shores fantasy lacks a specific counterpart culture as its subject; the northern name alone is not evidence. Comic fantasy realms lacks a governing comedy contract. Heart fantasy is the strongest alternative: the rivals-to-lovers relationship and mutual healing matter, but the commission, historical mystery and haunted institution remain the central plot, not romance co-equal with an independent epic story in the Fourth Wing lineage. The detailed Book Grind review explicitly describes much of the romantic interest as scholarly intrigue and the romance as brief and chaste. Chasteness itself is not disqualifying; it is the relative narrative weight that decides. The other Heart children, Cozy and Horse fantasy, plainly fail on threat/trauma and absence of an equine bond.

Gothic realms > Fantasy mystery. Roy and Percival are assigned to identify the mysterious invaders and their weakness through the Orphic Basilica's forbidden records. They uncover the library's concealed past and the larger conspiracy. The completed reviews agree on research and mystery as the book's main activity, even while disagreeing over its execution. Dark realms > Haunted realms is a serious rival: the spectral menace and confinement are vivid, but those are the investigative environment, not the whole contract. Isolationist fantasy loses because the building is a research prison imposed within an ongoing external society and war, not an isolated culture whose ritual stasis is the fiction's subject. Fantasy of manners loses because etiquette and class performance do not organize the plot. Technomancy lacks magical machinery or occult engineering.

Fantasy mystery > Ghost-sleuth fantasy. This is not selected merely because ghosts appear. The ghost supplies the decisive witness to the suppressed history. Fable's completed reader reviews explicitly identify a late vision from a ghost that answers the historical questions, corroborated by another account of ghosts explaining ancient history; the completed StoryGraph review identifies magical ghost intervention at the resolution. The scholar inquiry becomes a ghost-assisted investigation into an uncanny institution and its buried wrongs, close to The Tower of the Tyrant and The Briar Book of the Dead on the existing shelf. Detective fantasy is a strong rival because the governor compels the inquiry, but the case is not primarily a procedural of an established office or police institution: these are persecuted independent scholars, and the dead are the indispensable witness. Private-eye fantasy fails the hired hardboiled gumshoe expectation. No leaf gate was broadened to fit the book.
