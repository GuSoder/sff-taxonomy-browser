## Every novel's own engine
Conqueror's Moon: volcano famine threatens High Blenholme's four kingdoms. Prince Conrig of Cathra aims to reconquer/unite the island despite his dying father's caution, through alliance with sorceress Ullanoth of Moss. Royal intelligencer Snudge/Deveron uses hidden wild talent and invisibility sigil to serve the conquest; father demands an oracle's truth. Ullanoth's Beaconfolk powers provide decisive royal weapons at an unpredictable price. First review stresses royal relationships and succession, not mere military spectacle.
Ironcrown Moon: Conrig rules the united Sovereignty, but first wife Maudrayne survives with his firstborn legitimate heir, threatening succession and exposing his hidden magic that legally disqualifies him from kingship. Snudge is sent to find them and confronts loyalty/conscience. Beynor and Kilian steal moonstone sigils, initiate civil war, and seek the Unknown Potency to break the Beaconfolk's painful price; Source versus Great Lights manipulates both sides. SFRevu specifically says there is less Deveron here and identifies this larger cosmic contest; Rambles independently connects hidden inherited Talent to succession. Not just first volume's conquest repeated.
Sorcerer's Moon: sixteen years into Conrig's rule, Salka invasion and deteriorating king meet a new succession crisis. Heir Orrion refuses arranged marriage; twin Corodon obtains a Beaconfolk pact letting Orrion marry Nyla at the cost of his inheritance. Corodon also unfit, concealed first son Dyfrig and mother Maudrayne reappear with Deveron. The Beaconfolk factions use human dynastic conflict as pieces in their own war. Completed Concatenation and CA reviews independently establish these engines. Magical contracts, concealed royal magic and spiritual manipulation decide the ruling line across all three; the spy is not the sole whole-fiction mechanism.
