# World of the White Rat: whole-fiction correction
6 November 2026

## Decision and scope
Place World of the White Rat as one fiction, eight published novels, in existing Duty bond romantasy. Merge the Clocktaur War card in Revisionist quest fantasy and the incomplete Saint of Steel card in Paladin hero. The author explicitly places the three strands in one world. Current publisher pages independently identify Swordheart, Daggerbound and all four Saint of Steel books as White Rat fiction. This is a shared universe, not a collection of merely similar authorial settings.

Published scope: Clockwork Boys (2017), The Wonder Engine (2018), Swordheart (2018), Paladin's Grace (2020), Paladin's Strength (2021), Paladin's Hope (2021), Paladin's Faith (2023), Daggerbound (2026). The author's page is stale about Swordheart having one book; Hachette and Tor's NetGalley listing confirm Daggerbound published 25 August 2026. The four Saint of Steel novels are published installments of a planned seven; do not count the remaining planned books. First-publication years are not the dates of the later print jackets selected here. Locus's March 2018 Clockwork Boys print edition is not the first publication: ISFDB records November 2017 hardcover and ebook editions.

## Each strand's own engine
- Clockwork Boys and The Wonder Engine: condemned forger Slate, Caliban, Brenner and Edmund accept a dangerous mission to discover and stop the mechanical army. The shared coerced mission and Slate/Caliban's growing romance both run across the duology. Anuket's machinery, Slate's past and Caliban's trauma are real, not grounds to call all eight books paladin adventures. Titan's second-volume synopsis makes attraction and the mission explicit; Locus reviews the complete two-volume story.
- Swordheart: Halla's inheritance and coercive relatives set her on a journey for legal help with sword-bound Sarkis. Protecting her, recovering her freedom and negotiating his coerced service intertwine with their romance. Reactor's completed review grounds the autonomy and romance reading. The sword curse is a substantial Magic bond rival.
- Paladin's Grace: Stephen's dead god has left him broken; Grace is a perfumer with her own wounds. Rescue and a murder/poisoning mystery throw them together. Locus calls it a fantasy romance and discusses the couple's emotional arc, not merely a cover blurb.
- Paladin's Strength: Istvhan's escort and clay-head investigation join Clara's search for her kidnapped werebear sisters. Shared travel and attraction lead to an arena rescue and clay-head confrontation. Locus's completed review supplies the rescue climax and judges it fantasy romantic adventure with sword-and-sorcery action.
- Paladin's Hope: Galen and lich-doctor Piper are pushed into a corpse investigation by gnole constable Earstripe. The murder maze puts protection and trust under pressure. Locus explicitly calls it a fantasy romance at its core and treats the pair and the paladins' lost purpose together.
- Paladin's Faith: Shane protects spy Marguerite during her dangerous work involving an artificer's device, court and demon cult. Attraction and their incompatible habits run alongside the protection/espionage plot. Both the author/publisher description and Locus's finished review cover this book itself.
- Daggerbound: transporting a relic brings Edmund into contact with sword-bound Dervish; an attack triggers the sword and its compulsory service. Edmund then seeks a way to release him. Publisher copy and completed advance-reader accounts on NetGalley ground the road adventure and romance. This is the second strong curse-bond straddle, not evidence that the other six lovers are magically bound.

## Root-down walk
Speculative fiction > Fantasy > Fantasy realms > Heart fantasy > Romantasy > Bonded romantasy > Duty bond romantasy.

Fantasy is positive: gods, demonic possession, magical creatures and sword curses are actual causes, not advanced technology or explained-away tricks. The Clockwork Boys' artificial soldiers do not make the whole universe science fiction. These are internally invented lands, not Earth or Earth-to-otherworld crossings.

At Fantasy realms, the recurring romantic bond is the whole fiction's strongest governing engine. Heart fantasy wins over Epic fantasy's large-scale worldbuilding and Sword and Sorcery's intimate action. The duology has a war-stopping mission, but the other books' murder, protection and rescue adventures do not make armies/campaigns the whole universe's story. Warmth and humor are real but neither safety nor jokes govern the whole: murders, captivity, trauma, combat and mortal jeopardy materially shape the romances. No identifiable fairy-tale source is revised across all eight, no non-Western counterpart culture is the point, no horse bond is the spine, and Gothic menace is a texture rather than the whole fiction's register.

At Epic, considered as the strongest rival: Heroic's power-source classes cannot honestly make this whole universe a paladin fiction. Forger Slate, Halla, Edmund, perfumer Grace, Piper and Marguerite matter as protagonists, not background to seven paladins. High/Revisionist describes the coerced Clocktaur quest well, but does not cover all the later relationship-led mysteries and protection stories. Political power, hard scientific magic, invented myth itself and military ranks each capture some furniture, not the whole eight's organizing engine. No existing gate was widened to accommodate the universe.

At Heart, romantic arcs are coequal with external dangers across all three strands. The primary author's fantasy-romance grouping is corroborated by completed critical reviews, not used alone as a genre verdict. At Romantasy, Bonded wins: the couples are thrown together by missions, investigations, protection or rescue. They are not consistently on opposed sides. Discomfort, trauma, misunderstanding or the Dervish's resentment of all wielders are not enough to call the whole universe Enemies-to-lovers.

At Bonded, Duty is the least-distorting whole-fiction fit: a shared task brings the pair together and the romance develops through it. Soul bond would falsely imply an innate paired magical/soul tie. Bargain bond's Court child would falsely impose an arranged or diplomatic match. Its Magic child is the strongest wobble because Swordheart and Daggerbound genuinely contain imposed sword curses. But those curses are not present between the lovers in the four Saint of Steel novels, and the Clocktaur mission is a joint assignment, not a supernatural lover's claim. The broad shared-task engine remains positive across the whole. Do not split the shared universe to make the two sword books cleaner fits.
