## Definition and positive evidence
Chivalric myth fantasy: Fantasy retelling or reimagining the inherited medieval chivalric legendary cycles and their later literary elaborations. Current branches are the Matter of Britain (Arthurian fantasy) and Matter of France (Frankish myth fantasy). The inherited legendary cycle is the source; a generic knight, a historical medieval setting, romance-as-love-story, and a wholly invented quest are insufficient. Critical, feminist, queer and satirical reimaginings still pass because this is a source-family umbrella, not an endorsement of knightly ideals.

Goucher's literature teaching source places the Matter of France (Charlemagne/Roland) and Matter of Britain (Arthur/court) under the three matters of romance and explains their literary crossings. Britannica's scholarly Romance article identifies the later assimilation of the Charlemagne cycle into Arthurian prose romance, including Boiardo/Ariosto, while preserving the distinction between chanson de geste and later romance. EBSCO's Medieval Romance survey independently treats France, Britain, Rome and England as inherited matters. Thus these two are not an invented pairing. University of Glasgow Library specifically calls Orlando Furioso chivalric fantasy and traces its lineage from Roland through the later romances.

Caveat: Chivalric romance is the established term, but it has both a narrower literary-form sense than all epic antecedents and a modern love-story ambiguity. Chivalric myth fantasy is a descriptive tree label built from the established chivalric/source tradition plus the existing myth-fantasy pattern. It is not claimed as an established exact scholarly genus. Its definition includes legendary epic antecedents and later reimaginings, not only formal romances. The exact full-name search did not surface a same-named franchise, not trademark clearance. Chivalric fantasy alone is attested, but too broad in common use and does not clearly preserve this tree's Mythic retelling hint. The word myth supplies that hint. Generic medieval, knightly, heroic, warrior and romance labels fail either ancestry or findability and are not proposed.

## Member and sibling walk
All current Arthurian branches stay intact, including Arthur classic and Arthur reimagined, together14fictions/35books. Feminist or critical retellings still draw on the inherited Arthurian cycle. Frankish1/1 is Kingdom of the Grail, directly verified again from its publisher and a completed review: Roland, Charlemagne, Merlin and the Grail form a deliberately mixed chivalric-cycle story. The new umbrella totals15fictions/36books, with Arthurian14/35 and Frankish1/1. Lopsided by fiction count, but this is a grouping of source genres2children, not a split of15fictions. This imbalance should not be concealed.

Test all other Mythic siblings against the unchanged source-family gate:
* Celtic remains separate. Its Irish Ulster Cycle and Welsh Mabinogi/Otherworld traditions are not wholesale branches of the chivalric court cycles. Literary influence and some Arthurian Welsh roots do not make all Celtic mythology chivalric.
* Norse and Slavic remain separate. Contact, shared motifs and comparative Indo-European ancestry do not place their entire myths within the chivalric-cycle family.
* Classical remains separate. Medieval Matter of Rome romances genuinely belong to the broad chivalric literary field, but the existing Classical umbrella covers the full inherited Greco-Roman corpus, including ancient narratives not mediated through medieval romance. Moving the whole Classical node would be overbroad. No current classical fiction is individually moved or split in this proposal. A future Matter of Rome-specific work must be walked rather than forcing the current Classical whole under this label.
* Biblical remains separate: miracles and religious sources, not chivalric-cycle descent. Grail use of Christianity does not make Biblical fantasy a child.
* Japanese remains separate: inherited Japanese myth/legend, not this medieval European chivalric literary lineage.
* Mayan/Aztec belong in the separate Mesoamerican source-family grouping. No knightly adventure rule crosses those traditions into Chivalric.

Thus both proposed new bundles group by inherited source tradition, with distinct current memberships. All other leaves/bundles remain direct children, allowed by6November partial-bundle precedent. This is not a European/rest-of-world partition.

## Other candidates tested and rejected
Norse+Slavic: no verified equally clear shared category narrower than an overbroad Indo-European umbrella, which would also claim Classical and Celtic, and potentially much more. Do not invent Northern merely to satisfy the rule.
Arthurian+Celtic under Insular/Albion: boundary fails continental Arthurian literary development and Ireland/Britain mismatch. Owner's6November exploratory conversation already exposed this; the new source evidence also shows French/German development. Not proposed.
Arthurian+Frankish+Celtic under broad medieval: time period is not shared source inheritance; the Celtic nodes cover distinct myth cycles. Not proposed.
Bulfinch fantasy: the publisher's actual contents do combine Arthur, Charlemagne, classical, Scandinavian, Celtic and Oriental material. That makes the compiler boundary broader and messier than the earlier memory-only discussion, not a clean exclusive taxonomy. It is also not an intuitive genre. Not proposed.
Indo-European: academically meaningful comparative scope, but too sweeping for the needed local second bundle and risks flattening different kinds of literary/traditional source. Requires separate research into every included and excluded tradition. Not proposed as an easy compliance patch.
European/beyond Europe: rejected by owner6November, geographically uneven and no reason to revisit for this seed.
