## Root evidence
The inherited placement mistook the story's religious language and seeming spells for literal supernatural causation. Scholes's completed-series2017 interview describes distant-future post-apocalyptic SF experienced as fantasy by characters with limited knowledge. His2024 interview explicitly says the apparent supernatural resolves by the ending into reality-grounded advanced civilization technology, citing Clarke's law as a design principle. This is direct evidence about the whole completed fiction, not an inference that the mere presence of robots rules magic out.

The publisher-authorized Hymn prelude supplies concrete technological machinery beneath the mystical vocabulary: covert airships, invisibility coating, controlled communications and a spatial translation pool. Instant translation prevents claiming the known-physics bar for Hard science fantasy. Finished reviews describe old technology and interlocking religious/political manipulation. No full-novel reading is claimed.

## Root-down walk
Speculative fiction > Science fiction > Atemporal SF > Science fantasy > Ozymandian tech.

Fantasy fails at the ontology gate after the author's explicit completed-series explanation. Historical and Modern day SF fail their setting gates. Futurist is a serious rival because this is human far future. However, the original17September user discussion expressly kept deep-future fantasy-surface/tech-engine worlds under Science fantasy in Atemporal, including future-Earth City Under the Stars. The user's explanation was that rebuilding society and hiding the machinery makes a fantasy world possible in a remote future or another universe; he approved the resulting plan in phonemsg-01M2QGBV2P0RZ2C73KV3RTTY0R. Applying a literal future-versus-atemporal exclusion would contradict that original guidance. No new exception or ancestor widening is needed here.

Science fantasy fits the sustained epic-fantasy surface and technologically explained engine. Hard science fantasy fails the known-physics requirement because instantaneous spatial translation is shown. Old Mars fails place and tradition. Planetary romance is a serious rival, especially Far-future planetary romance and Hidden lander romance: a remote human colony, forgotten origins and knowledge monopoly are present. But the whole fiction is not mainly a visitor's exotic-world adventure or the specific suppression of an original colony's scientific recovery. It is an unfolding revelation of apparently sacred powers, inherited institutions and manipulation built on a lost technological civilization's still-working machinery. Ozymandian tech's buried-tech/mistaken-magic definition describes that reading experience more precisely.

Other Atemporal siblings fail: no demonstrated literal time travel for Time travel fiction, no parallel-world transit premise, no reincarnation premise, no consciously unstable Fabulation reality or New weird ontological mixture, and no established simulation reveal. Prophetic/shared dreams and spatial translation are not automatically time travel.

## Setting contradiction preserved
Tor's whole-series marketing page calls it distant-future Earth. A completed Hymn review specifically describes Lasthome as another world colonized long ago by humans. The author's2024 explanation describes humanity's last pocket after millions of years spreading from Firsthome through the galaxy; the2024 Pilgrims announcement describes returning to the moon and forgotten heritage. These support a remote lost-human-history setting but do not require settling the exact astronomical identity of Lasthome for this placement. The future-Earth-dependent Lost knowledge keepers route was considered and rejected rather than treating early publisher copy as conclusive against a completed-finale review. No constructed-world or literal time-travel claim is adopted from search highlights.
