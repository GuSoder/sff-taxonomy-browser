# Space techno fiction, bounded membership audit,25November2026

Ten fictions,20included novels after ae10f50. No structural edit approved or executed. Scope is the receiving nine plus the consolidated Schroeder fiction, not a corpus-wide sweep. Findings based on creator/publisher accounts, completed reviews and the already recorded primary/legal excerpts. None claims full primary-text coverage.

## Root gates shared by all ten

All use literal technology, not established supernatural magic. All are anchored to humanity's future rather than a free-standing fantasy cosmology, literal historical setting or recognizable present-day Earth. No actual temporal crossing is established in the included novels. The special traps were checked individually below. Body reconstruction, simulated historical environments, recorded earlier selves, longevity and inconsistent author chronology are not alone travel to another era. The owner's included-novels-only temporal scope applies, not excluded shorts.

At Futurist, interstellar/post-Earth technology and civilization govern rather than an Earthbound future. Hard SF is a serious rival in Stross and Moriarty, but the existing narrow children do not match whole fictions better: no governing orbital-engineering problem, planetary science cycle, rigorous recognition of a new world-model or matter-transmission thought experiment as the sustained sole reading contract. Their scientific toolkits support computational lives and civilizations. Alien fiction is weaker even when alien routers/technology exist: the continuing subject is human/posthuman agency, not encountering alien life as such.

Within Spacefaring, all positively meet existing Space techno fiction: interstellar identity transfer, computation, networks and posthuman technology. Space opera's adventure is a real mode but loses to the technologies' continuing social/identity implications. Military is whole-fic weak except a strong Beyond Enemies surface rival; its explicit infantry/navy/academy/command partition does not honestly contain a tank/AI pair who leave military life. Future history requires sustained changing civilization as the story rather than a world with a long chronology. Generation starships requires multigenerational communities, not immortals or cryogenic voyages. Space dystopias requires closed oppressive society as the governing horror, not merely coercion somewhere. Colony cases/politics catch individual plots, not necessarily an included shared world's whole technology inquiry. Comedy is not the governing form.

## Honest split discovery

The recurring contrast is where computation most directly changes lived life: the person,or the shared world. A proposed5/5was rejected on its own evidence: Freyaverse cannot be presented as purely virtual/environmental when its shared premise is manufactured/copyable people. Six/four better respects it.

Personal computational identity6/14novels: Takeshi Kovacs3,Eight Worlds4,Glasshouse1,Freyaverse2,Spin3,Beyond Enemies1.
Shared programmed environments4/6novels: Accelerando1,Old Twentieth1,Web of Angels1,Ventus3.

Overlap genuinely remains. Accelerando has many transferred/copied people but its escalating environments/economics carry the progression; Glasshouse constructs an oppressive environment but the backed-up/remade person and consent are the immediate conflict; Freyaverse's second novel is strongly systems/economics but copied robot personhood joins its two books; Ventus's three novels center AI but their consistent shared concern is intelligent constructed environments. This is a foreground distinction,not a false claim that half lack minds or half lack worlds.

Three technology buckets (identity transfer,virtual worlds,AI) were rejected because AI is not exclusive: it pervades all three; Beyond and Freyaverse would become a manufactured remainder. Noir versus non-noir likewise makes style govern over common technology and turns the non-noir half into absence. Names remain to be tested against full standalone/context rule before service.
