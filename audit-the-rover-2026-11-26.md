## Whole story
Wick, a dweller librarian, is shanghaied by dwarven pirates, enslaved by goblins, adopted by thieves and tested by a dragon, winning through remembered lore and ingenuity. Later his former slave/apprentice Juhg brings an accursed book into the Vault; invading monsters destroy it and Wick is abducted. Juhg, Craugh and companions seek the four fragments of the Book of Time and another library. Fourth novel returns to the world's still-live ancient Kharrion threat through interwoven Wick journals and Juhg's search for three texts and magical weapons. Knowledge versus ignorance, library preservation and companion journeys hold the changing protagonists together. This is not four modern office-library novels.

## Full root-down walk and rivals
Speculative fiction > Fantasy > Fantasy realms > Epic fantasy > High fantasy > Classic quest fantasy > Fellowship quest fantasy > Revival quest fantasy > Throwback quest fantasy. Existing leaf8to9, not a split/seed.

Positive magic: wizard spells, enchanted book opening a monster gate, cursed artifacts, magical weapons, old-world beings and dragons. Not SF justified by dwellers having an ancient creator race; no recorded Earth origin. Real Earth/Portal fail: Greydawn Moors and invented peoples are the world, no Earth strand. Sea crossings and magical gates within that imaginary cosmology do not satisfy Earth-required Portal fantasy. Time-affecting magic is not SF time travel that outranks Fantasy root.

Fabulation deliberately tested despite literal books and title Book of Time: Kirkus says the artifact has spells able to unmake the world/alter past and future; that is a genuine caution, not denied. Accessed plot accounts describe pursuit of a spellbook artifact with divided magical powers, not characters rewriting sentences to revise the experienced world, worlds revealed as authored fiction or fictional people stepping out of a novel. Fourth volume's narrative reads Wick's real in-world journals in alternating adventures, no proof Juhg literally enters a fictional text. Merely story-within-story or powerful written spells is not sufficient to assert authored reality. Owner29September revision-of-reality rule remains: confirmed active reality revision through authored text would require rewalk to Fabulation, even called magic. No fabricated ending claim that book can never affect history. Finished criticism to date supports conventional magical adventure, not metafiction as governing mode. Confidence moderate-high with this specific limit.

Fantasy realms actual children: no counterpart culture whose meaning is the subject (Far shores); no deliberate inherited fairytale revision or fairytale task grammar (Fairytale); no sustained romantic/cozy/horse bond spine (Heart); no Gothic dread/confinement as whole-world reading (Gothic), even with dark goblin slavery; no governing parody joke/absurd institution satire (Comic), even though Kirkus/Booklist find funny, puns and pastiche. Homage and familiar Hobbit furniture are not alone parody. Sword and sorcery is a strong local rival for sea voyages/thieves and fighting, but piracy/caper is episodic; whole world revolves around restoring guarded knowledge, age-old races/history and world-threatening texts across long journeys. Magic and battle serve that sustained epic quest rather than celebrating combat as the invitation.

Epic actual children: Military fails organized military service/campaign as primary story, Political fails governing contest/institutions as main plot, Hard fails rigorous spell-system puzzle, Mythological fails deity worship/struggle or Silmarillion-style independent cosmology as primary subject. Heroic > NPC heroes is the strongest competing route: Wick/Juhg rely on ordinary learning and courage, not cast spells or elite martial skill. Booklist and creator explicitly confirm this, so do not classify either as rogue/bard/mage just for traveling with thieves/wizard. Yet hero-class fantasy is less exact for the whole continuing four-novel world than the consequential external journeys of alternating protagonists and fellowship against ancient threat. The libraries' mission gives knowledge public stakes, but no civic-office life replaces the companion adventure. NPC remains plausible, rejected by whole-world aboutness rather than an invented rule that NPCs cannot travel.

High fantasy: internally realized world journey is consequential to preservation of knowledge and safety of the races. Classic rather than Revisionist: the modest librarian revises hero stereotype in a broad sense but neither refuses a fated mantle nor begins principally as a reckoning with a finished heroic quest. The explicit Tolkien/Brooks homage accepts companion heroism. Within Classic: Fellowship rather than Destiny, since no defining hidden royal bloodline, unique chosen mantle, acquired magic-power destiny or named office calling carries the whole fiction; librarians start librarians, title promotion is not in itself a supernatural calling. Dark quest fails dying-world/gothic register as dominant tone; violence and old catastrophe do not turn this funny hopeful adventure into that shelf. Fellowship's company is positive: small librarian with human/dwarf/elf/wizard allies on recovery road, not merely a named Dark Lord somewhere.

Evergreen requires first publication before late1980s, fails2001. Revival: later reconstruction of tradition. Throwback versus Reimagined: Kirkus calls first a Tolkien/Brooks pastiche and third too similar to previous genre blockbusters, PW calls Tolkienesque, Library Journal homage, creator calls successor to legacy. Friendly accessible pacing/humor and a librarian rather than warrior add novelty, but do not establish rebuilt modern sensibility rather than classic continuation. Strong disclosed Reimagined rival; traditional inherited company/ancient threat and deliberate genre register govern across all four. Bibliography date alone does not select leaf.
